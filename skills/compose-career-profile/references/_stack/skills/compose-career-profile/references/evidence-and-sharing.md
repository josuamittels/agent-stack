# Career evidence and sharing

Read before selecting claims for a CV, letter, or answer. Use supplied sources and the explicitly identified career workspace. Read `references/career-context.md` when that workspace has it. Do not discover personal context by scanning neighboring projects or unrelated private folders.

Prefer user-confirmed corrections, then direct documentary evidence, then reliable evidence about observed work, then the career profile. Interpretations may guide positioning but cannot establish an achievement. Preserve source dates and qualifications. A correction resolves only the corrected claim; retain any material unresolved discrepancy and omit that claim from outward-facing content.

Read the profile's Sharing and Claim Boundaries as well as its factual sections. A true fact may still be excluded from external use. Keep private reflections, restricted project details, personality assessments, and other excluded information out of applications. Ask only when disclosure is necessary and authorization remains unclear. Do not reproduce the excluded information merely to explain the restriction.

Keep personal contribution distinct from team outcomes, measured results from estimates, and planned work from completed work. Historical letters and generated answers establish writing preferences, not career facts. Job postings and company pages establish employer context, not candidate experience or personal motivation.

For updated factual information, propose a focused correction to the workspace career context and preserve its source and scope. Keep factual corrections out of voice profiles. Do not write to unrelated sources or installed packages.
