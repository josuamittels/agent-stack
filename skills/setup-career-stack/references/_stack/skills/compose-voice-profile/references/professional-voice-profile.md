# Professional Voice

Use this default for cover letters, screening answers, and interview answers. It defines writing behavior, not a candidate's personality, values, background, or seniority. Adapt to the person's actual language and examples. Keep format-specific structure in the owning skill.

## Shared voice

- Write directly and respectfully. Prefer plain words and precise verbs to self-praise, generic employer admiration, and unsupported enthusiasm.
- Let the evidence show capability. Preserve ownership, qualifiers, uncertainty, and the difference between direct and adjacent experience.
- Explain motivation through the person's stated interests and the actual work. Do not infer personal connections from employer research.
- Use short or medium sentences where they carry the thought naturally. Expand only when context or qualification helps the reader.
- Match formality to the recipient and language. In German, keep formal or informal address consistent. Follow the requested locale without translating English idioms literally.
- Preserve distinctive expression supported by user direction or writing samples. Do not assign analytical, entrepreneurial, collaborative, or other personality traits by default.
- End when the reader has what the format requires. Avoid rhetorical flourishes, repeated conclusions, decorative metaphors, and unnecessary jargon unless the user deliberately requests them.

## Channel adaptations

| Channel | Adaptation |
| --- | --- |
| Cover letter | Use the letter skill's greeting, role introduction, connected evidence and motivation, and courteous closing. |
| Application or screening answer | Answer the question immediately, retain only relevant evidence, meet each limit, and omit greeting and sign-off. |
| Spoken interview answer | Use natural spoken phrasing and a clear example; include reflection when asked. Avoid making every response a formal pitch. |

## Personalized guidance and precedence

Read the career workspace's `references/professional-voice-profile.md` when present. Apply the current request first, then relevant scoped workspace guidance, then shared workspace guidance, then this default. Factual accuracy and sharing restrictions apply throughout.

Both writing skills use the same workspace profile. Record general preferences once; label channel, language, or role-family exceptions. Keep one-application feedback in that application's working notes or the conversation. Do not silently generalize it.

When an older workspace has separate letter and answer profiles, use Compose Voice Profile's update mode to reconcile them into the shared workspace file. Preserve the originals, retain valid differences under channel headings, and record unresolved conflicts rather than selecting a preference arbitrarily. Do not change unrelated workspace instructions without authorization.

Keep installed references immutable. Without an identified workspace or a user-selected profile destination, apply feedback in the conversation only. Store writing behavior, not career facts, contact details, employer histories, private writing samples, or documents in the profile. Keep provenance as short descriptions of preferences without reproducing private samples.

## Calibration

Constructed example of a bounded claim: “I coordinated the rollout with the service team. I would bring that experience while learning the tools used in this role.” Use this only as a writing illustration; it establishes no fact about a candidate.
