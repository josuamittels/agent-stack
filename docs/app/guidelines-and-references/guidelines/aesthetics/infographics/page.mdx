# Infographics

> **Reference** · **experimental** · **v0.1.0** · updated 2026-11-11

## Overview

Use this guide to turn established information into truthful visual explanations: charts, process diagrams, technical illustrations, geographic maps, and metro schematics.

## How to use this guide

Define the reader's question and the source information before choosing marks or visual style. The guide establishes boundaries before overlaps: it owns representation, encoding, scales, and topology; analytical work establishes the evidence, while neighboring aesthetic guides own layout, type, colour, symbols, interaction, and access requirements.

Its governing rule is to make the intended relationship easy to understand while preserving the evidence and limits needed to interpret it correctly. Consult the chart and scale sections for quantitative comparisons, the diagram sections for processes and mechanisms, and the map sections for geography and route tracing. Use the worked examples and final review checks before delivery.

A metro schematic preserves connections and station order while simplifying geography. Its apparent distances do not establish journey times or walking distances. Quantitative marks must retain accurate positions and extents when the surrounding composition changes.
