# Audit UX

> **Skill** · **experimental** · **v0.1.1** · updated 2026-11-12

## Purpose

Review whether a design helps its intended users achieve their jobs and outcomes with understandable actions, appropriate effort and control.

## Inputs

Provide an identifiable artifact and enough access to inspect it. Include the purpose, audience, medium, platform, relevant constraints and output destination when known. Reuse existing context; consequential missing facts are clarified before dependent conclusions.

## Workflow

1. **Establish users and outcomes.** Supply the customer context or let the workflow inspect the app and existing brief. It asks when unresolved user needs, task priorities or expectations would change the assessment.
2. **Walk meaningful tasks.** Examine entry, orientation, actions, feedback, completion and recovery. Distinguish inspected facts and user evidence from hypotheses about behavior.
3. **Include accessibility.** [Audit Accessibility](/capabilities/skills/audit-accessibility) reviews the same artifact as a required helper and returns its report and criterion register.
4. **Fill the UX report and prioritize.** Use the [modular UX template](https://github.com/josuamittels/agent-stack/blob/main/src/skills/audit-ux/templates/report.md) for user/jobs context, task findings, applicable principles, accessible decisions to preserve and an integrated action plan.

The review explicitly checks task hierarchy, consistent terminology and behavior, and misleading or inappropriate language. For interfaces, it compares where features and options appear, when they are available, and what they do with the expectations supported by user evidence or relevant conventions. Findings include concrete examples, suggested changes or replacement wording, and verification. Static mockups leave behavior unverified.

## Result and limits

Receive report.md plus accessibility/report.md and accessibility/audit.json. The review addresses user/job fit, expectations, conventions, learnability, effort, continuity and recovery. An inspection does not establish observed user success or product-market fit.

An audit authorizes inspection and recommendations. Implementation, publication and consequential live actions need their own authorization. Reports are saved in a fresh audit folder; previous reports are preserved.
