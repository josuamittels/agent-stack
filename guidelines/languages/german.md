# German Prose Writing – Best Practices

Natural German makes the intended meaning available without unnecessary effort. Its vocabulary, grammar, sentence movement, and organization fit the subject, reader, situation, and form. It can be formal, conversational, terse, analytical, or literary. Native phrasing does not require bureaucratic distance, conspicuous informality, or elaborate sentences.

Preserve meaning, facts, necessary terminology, and the integrity of quotations. Apply the general conventions across forms. The style sections add requirements and narrowly necessary exceptions for academic, technical, absolute, article, and storytelling prose.

Compose relationships in German rather than reproducing English words and sentence shapes. German has its own means of expressing emphasis, social distance, uncertainty, and continuity. Grammatical correctness, idiomatic expression, and fitness for the occasion are related but distinct qualities.

The presentation conventions combine German orthographic rules with a restrained house style. Straight quotation marks and the exclusion of sentence dashes are house preferences. A word, construction, punctuation mark, or level of complexity does not establish authorship.

Examples illustrate writing choices. They do not report actual research findings, events, product behavior, or measurements. Alternatives that introduce a fictional action demonstrate a possible scene, not an interchangeable paraphrase of the original.

## Meaning, reader, and register

### Preserve the proposition

Keep what a sentence says, who or what it concerns, and how strongly it says it. A smoother revision must retain conditions, exclusions, quantities, causality, chronology, attribution, and uncertainty. Concision is useful when the reader receives the same relevant meaning.

| Original | Meaning-changing revision | Faithful revision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Die Behandlung kann bei manchen Betroffenen die Beschwerden lindern." | "Die Behandlung lindert die Beschwerden." | "Bei manchen Betroffenen können die Beschwerden durch die Behandlung nachlassen." |
| "Nur Administratoren dürfen archivierte Datensätze löschen." | "Administratoren dürfen Datensätze löschen." | "Archivierte Datensätze dürfen ausschließlich von Administratoren gelöscht werden." |
| "Das Team meldete nach der Änderung weniger Fehler." | "Die Änderung verhinderte Fehler." | "Nach der Änderung meldete das Team weniger Fehler." |
| "Er war müde und wollte nicht reden." | "Völlig erschöpft zog er die Stille vor." | "Er war müde. Reden wollte er nicht." |
| "Diese Erklärung könnte auf Hunde zutreffen, die mit anderen Hunden zusammenleben." | "Das Verhalten ist bei Tieren mit anderen Haustieren verbreitet." | "Für Hunde, die mit anderen Hunden zusammenleben, könnte diese Erklärung zutreffen." |

Do not strengthen fatigue into exhaustion, permission into capability, an observed sequence into a cause, or a possible explanation into a frequency claim. A more vivid verb can change the facts as easily as an omitted qualification.

Keep deliberate ambiguity where it serves a literary work. Remove accidental ambiguity from explanations, instructions, and factual claims. A character's uncertain intention differs from an instruction whose object cannot be identified.

### Write for the reader's actual knowledge

Establish unfamiliar concepts before relying on them. Explain a necessary term near its first relevant use. Skip elementary explanations that the intended reader does not need.

> Opaque for a general reader: "Die Idempotenz gewährleistet robuste Wiederholungsversuche."
>
> Clearer: "Wenn dieselbe Anfrage erneut gesendet wird, entsteht keine zweite Bestellung."

Keep the term where it helps the reader: "Die Anfrage ist idempotent: Eine Wiederholung erzeugt keine zweite Bestellung." The explanation requires that behavior to be established. Do not turn a technical property into a promise that every retry will succeed.

Give unfamiliar abbreviations their full form at first use, followed by the abbreviation when it will recur. Prefer the full term in a short passage where the abbreviation saves little. Familiarity depends on the audience, not on the writer's profession.

An abstract, caption, table note, or independently displayed interface message needs enough context to stand alone. A definition in the main text does not necessarily make an isolated chart intelligible. Use the same German term and abbreviation consistently across independently readable elements.

### Match register to the form

Use formal precision for scholarly claims, literal language for procedures, and character-appropriate diction for fiction. Natural German does not require slang, anecdotes, rhetorical questions, intimate address, or a friendly opening.

Distinguish familiar standard-language contractions from informal reductions. Forms such as "im", "am", "zum", and "beim" are ordinary German. They do not need expansion to make prose formal. Forms such as "geht's" or "hab" carry different register choices and may belong to dialogue or conversational writing.

Formality comes from accurate terms, complete relationships, and proportionate claims. It does not require "hiermit", "diesbezüglich", "zwecks", or repeated "erfolgt" constructions. A technical term can be appropriate in a professional manual and unhelpful in a public notice.

### Keep address and social distance coherent

Choose address according to the established relationship and medium. Use "du" for an individual addressed informally, "ihr" for an informally addressed group, and "Sie" for formal address to one or more people. The choice affects verbs, possessives, commands, and the relationship expressed by the whole passage.

Do not alternate between "du" and "Sie" to create variety. Keep address consistent across a page and its related actions unless a change of speaker or relationship explains the difference. A product voice can address the reader directly without putting a possessive before every noun.

| Relationship | Coherent wording |
| --- | --- |
| Informal singular | "Du kannst deinen Antrag später ergänzen." |
| Informal plural | "Ihr könnt eure Anträge später ergänzen." |
| Formal singular or plural | "Sie können Ihren Antrag später ergänzen." |
| Neutral action label | "Antrag ergänzen" |

The formal example concerns the application relevant to each addressed reader; use a plural object when several applications are actually meant. Pronoun choice must not silently change number or ownership.

Respect does not require ceremonious padding. "Bitte senden Sie den Beleg bis Freitag" can be professional without "Wir möchten Sie hiermit höflichst darum bitten". Conversely, removing every courtesy does not automatically improve a sensitive refusal.

Choose directness according to the burden, stakes, and relationship. An ordinary instruction can be direct. A request for discretionary help may need a reason or an acknowledgment of inconvenience. Repeating "bitte" cannot compensate for an unclear demand.

### Use the intended variety of German

Keep spelling, terminology, and conventional expressions coherent within the intended standard variety. German used in Germany, Austria, and Switzerland includes legitimate differences. Do not treat a regional standard term as an error simply because another variety uses a different one.

Use "ß" according to the intended variety's rules; Swiss standard German uses "ss" instead. Preserve umlauts rather than replacing them with letter pairs in ordinary prose. Technical limitations, identifiers, and established names can require different spellings.

Match relevant regional vocabulary without assembling an artificial dialect. "Jänner" can be appropriate in Austria, while "Januar" suits other contexts. A regional greeting can express a real local relationship; it should not become decoration in every text.

Do not infer a reader's preferences, politeness expectations, humor, or personality from nationality alone. Adapt to the actual publication and relationship. Avoid combining incompatible regional forms merely to make prose seem less standardized.

### Describe people accurately and respectfully

Use names, pronouns, and descriptions relevant to the subject. Respect stated identity preferences. Do not infer identity from appearance, a name, or group membership.

Mention age, disability, ethnicity, nationality, sex, gender, or another personal characteristic when it contributes necessary information. Avoid stereotypes, demeaning labels, and descriptions that reduce a person to a diagnosis. Person-first and identity-first language can both be appropriate; follow known individual or community preferences without assuming unanimity.

Distinguish grammatical gender from a person's gender. A noun's grammatical form does not establish a referent's identity. English gender-neutral pronoun solutions do not transfer automatically into German morphology.

Use precise neutral formulations where they retain the intended group. "Das Team", "die Lehrkräfte", or direct address can work in suitable contexts. "Studierende" does not name every possible group described by "Schüler", and a role-neutral collective must not obscure the individual whose responsibility matters.

Paired forms, neutral nouns, and marked inclusive spellings have different grammatical, institutional, and accessibility implications. Follow the applicable convention consistently. Internal special characters are not interchangeable with ordinary inflection or universally required by standard orthography. Do not describe one institution's preference as the only respectful form.

In research, name what was recorded or measured. Sex and gender are distinct where the distinction affects the result. Preserve participants' self-descriptions and the limits of the data.

> Vague: "Die Alten kamen mit dem Angebot nicht zurecht."
>
> More specific, if supported: "Teilnehmende zwischen 70 und 84 Jahren berichteten, dass sie die kleine Schrift nicht lesen konnten."

The second sentence requires those ages and reports to be established. Respectful wording does not justify invented precision.

### Preserve voice without preserving avoidable confusion

A voice may depend on restraint, expansiveness, dry humor, directness, or a characteristic movement of thought. Preserve those features while removing accidental repetition, faulty grammar, unsupported claims, and obscure phrasing.

Multilingual writers can retain distinctive emphasis, experience, and perspective in idiomatic German. Correct an unclear construction without adding colloquialisms, borrowed cultural references, or an invented local personality. Native phrasing does not require erasing the writer's identity.

Do not introduce typos, artificial hesitation, random digressions, rare words, or arbitrary irregularity to imitate a person. A polished sentence can be natural. An unfinished sentence can be formulaic. Preserve intentional speech patterns in their proper context rather than manufacturing them throughout the narration.

## Evidence, specificity, and uncertainty

### State the concrete claim

Say what happened, what something does, what changes, or what the reader needs to know. Replace atmospheric reassurance with an established mechanism, consequence, instruction, or number.

> Vague: "Der neue Ablauf ermöglicht ein nahtloses Nutzererlebnis."
>
> Concrete, if established: "Wenn das Absenden fehlschlägt, bleiben die Eingaben im Formular erhalten."

Specificity must come from the material. A plausible product behavior is not a fact. If the mechanism is unknown, do not invent one to replace an adjective.

### Separate observation, attribution, and interpretation

Keep observed events, reported accounts, and conclusions distinguishable. Identify whose judgment a sentence expresses.

| Kind of statement | Illustrative wording |
| --- | --- |
| Observation | "Drei Personen brachen vor der letzten Frage ab." |
| Attributed account | "Die Befragten bezeichneten die letzte Frage als unklar." |
| Interpretation | "Die Formulierung der Frage könnte zu den Abbrüchen beigetragen haben." |

The interpretation needs a defensible connection to the available evidence. Do not silently turn a person's explanation into the sole cause, an impression into consensus, or a plausible mechanism into an established finding.

An explanation compatible with one case does not establish prevalence. Keep the population, setting, and kind of claim intact. The possibility that a behavior serves a function does not show that it always does.

### Name the source of a claim

Replace vague authority such as "Experten sind sich einig", "Studien belegen", and "Kritiker warnen" with the actual source and relevant claim when available. Otherwise remove the borrowed authority or state what remains unknown.

Attach a reference to the proposition it supports. A genuine publication can still be misrepresented through an inaccurate paraphrase, broader population, missing condition, or unsupported causal conclusion. Distinguish the source's finding from the writer's inference.

Differentiate preprints, published studies, datasets, and secondary reporting. Publication status describes the source; it does not by itself establish that the conclusion is true. Reputation and citation counts do not replace relevant evidence.

Keep titles, authors, dates, identifiers, findings, quotations, and locators consistent with the version cited. Do not attach a preprint's finding to a later paper's bibliographic details when the versions differ. An attributed idea needs a recognizable connection to its source, not an unexplained name.

### Mark reported speech without manufacturing doubt

Indirect speech can distinguish a person's statement from the reporting voice. Konjunktiv I is useful for that purpose, especially in formal reporting.

> Direct statement: "Die Daten sind vollständig."
>
> Reported statement: "Die Leiterin erklärte, die Daten seien vollständig."

The second sentence attributes the claim. Konjunktiv I does not inherently declare it false or doubtful. Avoid adding skeptical verbs such as "behaupten" when neutral attribution is intended.

Indicative reporting can also be appropriate, depending on register and construction. Do not require a subjunctive in every clause introduced by "dass", or treat its absence as automatic endorsement. Make the reporting boundary and source intelligible throughout a longer passage.

When a Konjunktiv I form is indistinguishable from the indicative, a clear Konjunktiv II form can mark indirect speech. A "würde" construction can help where other forms are unclear or unnatural. Choose an intelligible formulation rather than a succession of conspicuous subjunctives. Do not let replacement forms accidentally turn a report into a hypothetical event.

### Make numerical comparisons interpretable

Identify the population, timeframe, units, denominator, and comparison baseline. State what was counted or measured. A percentage of respondents differs from a percentage of all eligible people; incident totals differ from incident rates.

| Distinction | Writing requirement | Illustrative wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Count and rate | Supply relevant population sizes or comparable rates. | "Gebiet A verzeichnete 40 Vorfälle bei 10.000 Einwohnern, Gebiet B 60 bei 30.000." These correspond to 4 and 2 per 1,000 residents. |
| Percentage and percentage points | Separate relative change from the difference between percentages. | "Der Anteil stieg von 20 % auf 30 %." That is 10 percentage points and a 50% relative increase. |
| Mean and median | Name the summary actually used. | "Die mediane Wartezeit betrug zwölf Minuten" does not report the arithmetic mean. |
| Money over time | Distinguish nominal values from inflation-adjusted amounts. | "Nominal stiegen die Ausgaben; in Preisen von 2024 gingen sie zurück." Both comparisons need evidence. |

Compare like periods, geographic boundaries, definitions, and populations. Explain material changes in coverage or classification rather than presenting them as changes in the underlying phenomenon. Pair a striking relative change with its starting value or absolute size when scale matters.

The arithmetic mean is the sum divided by the number of observations. The median is the middle ordered value, or the midpoint of the two central values for an even number of observations. Extreme values can shift the mean without a comparable change in the median. Avoid an ambiguous "Durchschnitt" when the choice changes the interpretation.

Do not give more decimal places than the measurement supports. Preserve the underlying value when localizing its presentation; number and unit conventions appear under punctuation and presentation.

### Distinguish magnitude, precision, and importance

An effect's size, estimate precision, statistical significance, and practical importance answer different questions. Explain consequences in relevant units. A small effect can be estimated precisely; an important possible effect can remain uncertain.

Identify the type and level of an uncertainty interval where relevant. Interpret it according to the method. A confidence interval is not a range containing a stated proportion of individual observations.

A nonsignificant result does not establish no effect or equivalence. State what the evidence can distinguish.

> Overstated for an inconclusive comparison: "Die Gruppen unterschieden sich nicht."
>
> Bounded, if imprecision is the issue: "Die Schätzung war zu ungenau, um einen praktisch relevanten Unterschied zwischen den Gruppen festzustellen."

Use the alternative only when that account fits the analysis. Do not turn every nonsignificant result into an assertion about inadequate precision.

### Keep the level of inference clear

A relationship among regions, schools, or institutions does not establish the same relationship among individuals. State the level at which the evidence was gathered and the conclusion applies.

Aggregate results may conceal subgroup differences or change when group proportions change. Explain material discrepancies instead of selecting whichever level favors the conclusion. Do not invent subgroup patterns.

Adjustment alone does not establish causality. A temporal sequence, prediction, or plausible mechanism also needs suitable evidence before it becomes a causal explanation. Relevant alternatives can include selection, confounding, reverse causation, and regression toward the mean. Report the design's inferential limits without turning every sentence into a catalog of possibilities.

### Explain the step from evidence to conclusion

Explain why the evidence supports the claim. A quotation, number, or citation may need an account of its relevance. A confident "folglich" cannot supply a missing premise.

Represent opposing arguments accurately. Address material counterevidence and plausible alternative explanations. Explain relevant differences in population, measurement, design, or setting. Avoid personal attacks, selective reporting, and invented balance between positions with unequal support.

Distinguish a finding, a supported explanation, and speculation. Compatibility with one explanation does not exclude another. State the consequential assumption where the argument depends on it.

### Anchor time-sensitive claims

Use a date, period, or version where "aktuell", "kürzlich", and "neueste" could become ambiguous. Preserve "ehemalig" or relevant dates when a person's earlier role determines the relationship to the subject.

Distinguish an event's date from a later account's publication date. Do not infer chronology from the order in which a source presents events, or suggest that an event was influenced by a document published afterward.

> Time-dependent: "Die neueste Fassung enthält eine Ausnahme."
>
> Anchored, if established: "Die Fassung von 2024 ergänzt die Regel von 2020 um eine Ausnahme."

An added date is useful only when accurate. Explicit chronology must preserve the difference between a planned change, an adopted decision, and its effective date.

### Prefer evidence to evaluative adjectives

"Bedeutend", "beeindruckend", "leistungsstark", "effektiv", and "signifikant" need an intelligible basis. State the result or consequence when available. Use "statistisch signifikant" for the defined statistical result, without implying practical importance.

> Unsupported: "Die Änderung verbessert die Leistung erheblich."
>
> Illustrative measurement: "Im Benchmark sank die mediane Verarbeitungszeit von 120 auf 90 Millisekunden."

Name the relevant comparison conditions. Without measurements, describe known behavior rather than implying a measured improvement.

### Preserve useful qualifications and modal meanings

Remove stacked uncertainty without removing uncertainty itself. "Möglicherweise" may replace "unter Umständen eventuell vielleicht"; it cannot replace a condition that determines whether the claim is true.

> Padded: "Es könnte potenziell durchaus möglich sein, dass die Wirkung nur vorübergehend ist."
>
> Direct: "Die Wirkung könnte vorübergehend sein."

Keep "in dieser Stichprobe", "bei niedriger Temperatur", or another relevant limit. Brevity must not turn a bounded observation into a universal claim.

Distinguish a modal verb's practical meaning from its epistemic meaning. "Sie muss arbeiten" states necessity; "Sie muss schon angekommen sein" can express an inference. "Er soll dort gewesen sein" can convey a report; "Er will dort gewesen sein" can convey his own claim.

Do not substitute "kann", "könnte", "dürfte", "muss", or "wird" simply to avoid repetition. They can change possibility, likelihood, obligation, intention, or prediction. "Sie müssen nicht kommen" removes a necessity; "Sie dürfen nicht kommen" states a prohibition. Preserve that distinction.

### Make examples carry information

An example should demonstrate a principle, expose a limit, or distinguish similar cases. Explain its connection only where needed. Do not repeat an implication the example already makes clear.

A single incident may illustrate an experience without establishing prevalence. Distinguish an illustrative anecdote, a counterexample, and evidence for a general conclusion.

Identify hypothetical scenarios as illustrative in factual writing. In fiction, invented detail belongs to the story world and must remain consistent with it. Plausibility does not authorize invented reporting.

### Give quotations a purpose

Quote when the wording supplies evidence, reveals a perspective, or matters to the argument. Identify the speaker and necessary context. Do not leave a quotation to replace reasoning.

Paraphrase when exact wording adds little. Preserve uncertainty, scope, and attribution. A close paraphrase that retains a source's structure while changing individual words still depends on the source and may reproduce its expression too closely.

Attribute borrowed ideas as well as wording. Identify indirect citations rather than implying direct access to material not consulted. Retain required page, paragraph, or section locators.

Do not invent speech. Distinguish a translated quotation from an original-language quotation where that distinction matters. A fluent translation may adjust syntax while preserving the speaker's claim, register, and uncertainty; it may not silently repair the argument or add an explanation.

## Organization and paragraph development

### Begin with something useful

Open with the claim, situation, observation, event, or genuine question that gives the piece a purpose. Remove greetings, sweeping declarations about the modern world, and announcements that the text will examine its subject.

> Padded: "In unserer heutigen schnelllebigen Welt ist das Thema Datensicherung wichtiger denn je."
>
> Direct: "Eine Sicherung hilft nur, wenn sich die Daten wiederherstellen lassen."

An opening may be quiet. It needs relevance, not manufactured drama. A question is useful when the piece answers a real uncertainty; it should not insinuate an unsupported allegation.

### Let the subject determine the structure

Arrange material around purpose and dependencies. Procedures follow action order, arguments develop reasons, references support lookup, and narratives control experience and disclosure.

Do not impose an introduction, three equal sections, and a recap on material that needs another shape. The number and length of sections should follow their contributions.

German does not require a long theoretical preamble or a digressive route to the answer. Nor does every paragraph require an English-derived fixed sequence of topic sentence, evidence, explanation, and summary. Make the progression intelligible in the form the subject needs.

### Give each paragraph a distinct contribution

A paragraph can establish a point, develop it, provide evidence, qualify it, connect ideas, or create a narrative effect. Keep sentences together when they develop the same movement.

Start a new paragraph for a meaningful shift in subject, time, speaker, argumentative function, or attention. Do not separate every sentence for emphasis or preserve a dense block merely because its sentences share a broad topic.

Details may establish context, reveal a limitation, or complicate an argument. They need not restate one thesis. Repeated explanation can resemble depth while adding nothing. Supply the missing reason, condition, consequence, or alternative if needed; otherwise end the paragraph.

### Connect ideas through their relationship

Make the next sentence's contribution recognizable: consequence, contrast, concession, explanation, sequence, or a new subject. Choose a connector only when it expresses that relationship.

| Relationship | Suitable expressions when the relationship holds |
| --- | --- |
| Reason | "weil", "denn", "da" |
| Consequence | "deshalb", "daher", "sodass" |
| Concession | "obwohl", "dennoch", "trotzdem" |
| Contrast | "aber", "dagegen", "hingegen" |
| Sequence | "danach", "anschließend", "bevor" |

These forms differ in syntax and emphasis; they are not interchangeable synonyms. A cause introduced as already known can differ from a newly supplied explanation. Preserve the actual relationship rather than choosing the most elaborate transition.

> Mechanical: "Darüber hinaus speichert der Dienst Protokolle. Des Weiteren zeichnet er Fehler auf."
>
> Connected, if both describe the same behavior: "Der Dienst zeichnet Fehler in seinen Protokollen auf."

German cohesion also comes from maintaining a topic, naming the same entity consistently, and developing an established concept. Not every paragraph needs "zunächst", "ferner", or "abschließend". Reproducing every connector in an English source can overexplain the structure.

### Develop enough, then stop

Concision retains what the reader needs in less unnecessary language. It does not compress a substantive thought into a generic label.

"Das Buch war interessant" may need an account of what interested the writer. A paragraph that already demonstrates the point may need no restatement. Match the depth to the reader's task and the consequence of misunderstanding.

Do not give every section a miniature introduction and conclusion. A heading can establish the topic; the first sentence should then contribute information.

### Preserve complexity that belongs to the subject

Represent real exceptions, competing motives, unresolved questions, and costs. Do not force a tidy binary or universal lesson onto the material.

Reduce linguistic difficulty while preserving conceptual complexity. An explanation can be clear about a problem that remains unresolved. Simplification should expose the important relationships, not erase them.

### Avoid manufactured contrasts and false ranges

State the point without rejecting an alternative nobody proposed. Formulas such as "Es geht nicht um X, sondern um Y" and "nicht nur X, sondern auch Y" are useful when both sides matter. Used mechanically, they create theatrical emphasis.

> Staged: "Es geht nicht nur um Daten. Es geht um Menschen."
>
> Concrete where supported: "Fehlerhafte Adressdaten können dazu führen, dass Bescheide die Betroffenen nicht erreichen."

The alternative makes a particular claim and requires that consequence to be established. It is not a universal paraphrase of the first sentence.

Use "von X bis Y" for a meaningful span, scale, or progression. A list such as "von Zusammenarbeit bis Innovation" does not by itself establish one. Name unrelated topics directly.

### Let substance determine the wording

The quality of a piece depends on what it includes, omits, relates, and concludes. Replacing suspect words cannot repair an empty argument, missing explanation, or unearned narrative turn.

Let terminology follow the actual distinctions. Do not disguise the same assertion as a sequence of insights by renaming it. Avoid invented objections and false choices used solely to give a paragraph movement.

### End where the material has earned an ending

An ending can establish a consequence, answer the opening question, state a remaining uncertainty, or identify a justified action. Remove conclusions that only repeat the introduction or praise the future.

Avoid automatic "Abschließend lässt sich festhalten", uplifting forecasts, engagement questions, and universal lessons. Keep a synthesis when it connects findings the reader needs to hold together; omit it when it merely announces that the text is ending.

In narrative, an action or image may close the work without an explanatory moral. In factual prose, a limited conclusion is preferable to an invented solution.

## Sentence structure and syntax

### Give the sentence an identifiable subject and action

Make the relevant actor, action, and object easy to find. A sentence need not begin with its subject, but its grammatical relationships should be available on first reading.

> Dense: "Die Durchführung der Anpassung des Speicherlimits erfolgt nach Berücksichtigung der derzeit im Projekt enthaltenen Dateien über die Konfigurationsdatei."
>
> Clearer, for the stated mechanism: "Das Speicherlimit wird in der Konfigurationsdatei angepasst. Dabei werden die Dateien berücksichtigt, die das Projekt derzeit enthält."

The revision retains consideration of the current files instead of silently dropping it. If the configuration file sets the limit automatically, name that behavior directly. Do not invent automation or a human actor to obtain a more active sentence.

Avoid introductory noun phrases that conceal the predicate. A sentence about responsibility should identify who is responsible; a sentence about a process can legitimately foreground the process.

### Build clauses around German verb placement

In an ordinary declarative main clause, the finite verb occupies the second constituent position. A constituent may contain several words.

> "Das Team prüft den Antrag morgen."
>
> "Morgen prüft das Team den Antrag."
>
> "Nach der nächsten Sitzung prüft das Team den Antrag."

An English-shaped sequence such as "Nach der nächsten Sitzung das Team prüft den Antrag" is not standard declarative order. A preceding subordinate clause normally fills the first position: "Wenn die Sitzung endet, prüft das Team den Antrag."

In a yes-or-no question, the finite verb normally comes first: "Prüft das Team den Antrag?" In an introduced subordinate clause, it normally follows the other clause elements: "weil das Team den Antrag prüft". Complex verb groups have their own ordering requirements; do not reduce all clauses to a single universal template.

Spoken verb-second clauses after "weil" can express a particular conversational organization. They do not establish a general default for edited explanations or formal writing.

### Keep the sentence bracket manageable

German often separates the finite verb from an infinitive, participle, or separable prefix. That bracket is ordinary grammar.

> "Sie hat den Antrag gestern eingereicht."
>
> "Sie reicht den Antrag morgen ein."
>
> "Sie muss den Antrag morgen einreichen."

Do not force the parts together in an English-shaped order. Instead, limit material that makes the reader forget what the opening verb requires.

> Overloaded: "Sie hat den Antrag nach mehreren Rückfragen, die sich auf Angaben bezogen, die im ursprünglichen Formular gefehlt hatten, gestern eingereicht."
>
> More manageable: "Sie hat den Antrag gestern eingereicht. Zuvor gab es mehrere Rückfragen zu Angaben, die im ursprünglichen Formular gefehlt hatten."

Move a substantial qualification into a following sentence or an appropriate clause after the bracket when that clarifies the relationship. Preserve whether the information is background, a condition, or the main assertion. Reordering must not change chronology or focus.

### Use word order to connect and emphasize

German word order is flexible within constraints. Case marking permits some rearrangements that English would interpret as different grammatical roles. Flexibility serves information structure, not arbitrary variety.

> "Der Ausschuss lehnte den zweiten Vorschlag ab."
>
> "Den zweiten Vorschlag lehnte der Ausschuss ab."

The second version can continue a discussion of the proposals or contrast the second with another. It does not change who rejected what.

Often, established information provides the point of departure and newer or contrastive information receives later prominence. This is a useful tendency, not a rigid sorting algorithm. Pronouns, definiteness, emphasis, clause type, and the particular verb affect natural ordering.

Do not begin every sentence with its subject, time, or a connector. Choose the opening according to the link with the preceding text. Avoid fronting a random object merely to make syntax less predictable.

### Express one main thought without imposing one sentence length

A sentence can carry a claim together with its condition, reason, or consequence. Split it when several claims compete for attention or a reader must repeatedly reconstruct its dependencies.

> Overloaded: "Der Server wies die Anfrage zurück, bei der das Token fehlte, und der Client versuchte es erneut, obwohl das Konto inzwischen gesperrt war, was einen weiteren Fehler verursachte."
>
> Clearer: "Der Server wies die Anfrage zurück, weil das Token fehlte. Der Client versuchte es erneut. Dieser Versuch scheiterte am inzwischen gesperrten Konto."

Use the clearer version only if both causes are established. A syntactic repair must not select an interpretation that the original leaves unresolved.

German's capacity for subordination does not oblige the writer to use it extensively. Equally, a sequence of short main clauses can obscure the relationship among conditions and consequences. Complexity and readability depend on the organization of a sentence as well as its length.

### Use active and passive voice according to perspective

Use active voice when the actor's responsibility or action matters: "Das Team prüft den Antrag." Use passive voice when the process, affected object, or unknown actor is the relevant focus: "Der Antrag wird geprüft."

Distinguish process from state. "Die Tür wird geschlossen" describes closing; "Die Tür ist geschlossen" can describe the resulting state. Not every participle after "sein" needs conversion into an action.

German permits impersonal passives such as "Hier wird gearbeitet". They need not acquire an invented subject for the sake of a subject-first rule.

Alternatives have different meanings and perspectives:

| Construction | Relevant distinction |
| --- | --- |
| "Wir prüfen Ihren Antrag." | Names the responsible group and addresses the reader. |
| "Ihr Antrag wird geprüft." | Foregrounds the application's current treatment. |
| "Man prüft Ihren Antrag." | Leaves the actor generic; it may sound evasive where responsibility matters. |
| "Der Antrag lässt sich online einreichen." | Describes a possibility, not a completed submission. |
| "Der Antrag ist einzureichen." | Can express a requirement; it is not a neutral synonym for every passive. |

Do not invent agency, certainty, or obligation to remove a passive. Avoid hiding responsibility behind passive or "man" constructions when the reader needs to know who acts.

### Make clause relationships explicit

Coordinate ideas of comparable weight. Subordinate a reason, condition, concession, or dependency where that relation is established. Repeated "und" can conceal the relationship.

> Sequence only: "Die Batterie war leer, und der Alarm blieb stumm."
>
> Explicit cause, if known: "Der Alarm blieb stumm, weil die Batterie leer war."

Use "wenn" for the relevant condition or repeated situation and "als" for a single past occasion where standard usage requires it. Distinguish "wenn" from indirect-question "ob": "Prüfen Sie, ob die Datei vorhanden ist" asks for a determination, not an action conditional on the file's presence.

Avoid a detached "wobei", "wodurch", or "was" clause that appears to qualify everything but identifies no clear relationship. Name the proposition or separate it when the relation needs explanation.

### Combine related actions without adding detail

Combine actions when they form one movement and separate emphasis is unnecessary.

> Repetitive: "Sie ging zum Markt. Sie kaufte Gemüse. Sie kehrte nach Hause zurück."
>
> Connected: "Sie ging zum Markt, kaufte Gemüse und kehrte nach Hause zurück."

Do not add "frisch", a mood, or a reason merely to improve flow. Preserve the subject across coordinated actions. If an omitted subject could refer to another actor, repeat the name or pronoun.

Parallel items need parallel grammatical treatment. Keep required prepositions and case endings when combining phrases. Two verbs can share an object only when their government permits it; otherwise supply the appropriate complement for each.

### Express appended consequences as actual claims

German versions of English promotional riders often appear as "was ... unterstreicht", "wodurch ... gewährleistet wird", or a detached participial phrase. They can append an unsupported benefit or interpretation.

> Empty addition: "Der Dienst speichert die Datei, wodurch ein nahtloses Erlebnis gewährleistet wird."
>
> Direct: "Der Dienst speichert die Datei."

If the consequence is substantive, identify it precisely and support the connection. Do not replace "nahtlos" with another adjective and leave the unsupported inference intact.

English nonfinite constructions can require a German clause, infinitive group, noun phrase, or direct statement. Do not mechanically attach "sicherstellend", "ermöglichend", or "hervorhebend" to reproduce their shape.

### Keep modifiers and their scope clear

Place restrictive and qualifying expressions where their relation is intelligible. Scope is not determined by adjacency alone, but placement must not invite the wrong interpretation.

> "Nur Anna prüft den Antrag."
>
> "Anna prüft nur den Antrag."

The first limits the actor. The second can limit what Anna examines or does, according to context and emphasis. Add an explicit contrast when the distinction must be unambiguous.

"Nicht müssen" differs from "nicht dürfen"; "noch nicht" differs from "nicht mehr". Preserve those relations. Do not move "fast", "erst", "schon", or a time expression merely to improve rhythm.

Introductory participial or infinitive constructions need a recoverable actor. "Am Bahnhof angekommen, begann es zu regnen" is an awkward way to describe a person's arrival. "Als ich am Bahnhof ankam, begann es zu regnen" makes both subjects explicit.

### Unpack overloaded attributes

German naturally places inflected adjectives and expanded participial attributes before nouns. "Die gestern veröffentlichte Fassung" is a useful compact expression.

Several nested relationships can become harder to understand before the noun appears.

> Dense: "die von der nach der Umstrukturierung neu eingesetzten Kommission vorgeschlagenen Änderungen"
>
> More manageable as two sentences: "Die Kommission wurde nach der Umstrukturierung neu eingesetzt. Sie hat die Änderungen vorgeschlagen."

The alternative retains the timing of the restructuring as well as the commission's role. In a surrounding sentence, a lighter attribute or relative clause may preserve the same information without requiring this exact division.

Choose a relative clause, separate sentence, or lighter attribute according to emphasis. A ban on all pre-nominal modification would make ordinary German needlessly cumbersome.

### Give pronouns and reference words clear antecedents

"Er", "sie", "es", "dies", "das", and relative pronouns need recognizable referents. Grammatical gender alone may not resolve competing antecedents. Repeat a noun when responsibility or sequence would otherwise be ambiguous.

> Ambiguous: "Als der Client den Server erreichte, startete er neu."
>
> Clear, if that is the event: "Der Server startete neu, als der Client ihn erreichte."

Use "damit", "darauf", "darüber", and similar pronominal adverbs for suitable things, propositions, or situations. "Darüber sprechen" is natural when the topic is clear. If several previous statements are possible topics, name the intended one.

Keep person-reference distinct: "mit ihr" refers to a person; "damit" does not serve as a universal replacement for it. A vague "diesbezüglich" rarely clarifies a missing antecedent.

### Preserve case, agreement, and verb government

Use the case and preposition required by the German construction, rather than translating English complements word by word. "Jemandem helfen", "auf etwas warten", and "sich für etwas entscheiden" have their own patterns.

Retain a required reflexive pronoun. "Sich erinnern" and "jemanden erinnern" do not identify the same relationship, and a verb that is reflexive in German may not have an equivalent reflexive construction in English. Use the intended German construction rather than adding or deleting "sich" mechanically.

In a relative clause, the pronoun takes gender and number from its antecedent and case from its role inside that clause.

> "Der Bericht, den ich gelesen habe, liegt hier."
>
> "Der Bericht, dessen Ergebnisse wir besprochen haben, liegt hier."

English-style relative-pronoun omission does not work in these constructions. Keep "dass" where it introduces the clause; distinguish it from referential "das".

Maintain article, adjective, noun, pronoun, and verb agreement, including after a revision that changes number or sentence structure. An attributive adjective normally inflects: "ein schneller Zug". Predicative and adverbial uses do not acquire an English-style adverb ending: "Der Zug ist schnell"; "Der Zug fährt schnell".

### Keep the small words that carry grammar

Retain articles, verbs, conjunctions, and prepositions when they make relationships explicit. "Entfernen Sie die Sicherungsdatei" is ordinary explanatory prose; "Sicherungsdatei entfernen" is suitable as a task heading or action label.

Repeat an article or noun when a coordinated phrase could otherwise identify one entity instead of two. "Der Kunde und der Anbieter" clearly names two roles. Shared articles are possible when the intended grouping remains clear.

Do not remove ordinary "es" constructions just because English advice discourages empty openings. "Es regnet" requires no invented actor. An anticipatory "es" can clarify a following clause, while repeated "Es ist wichtig, dass ..." may be removable padding.

Complete parallel constructions where omission makes the reader reconstruct a missing relationship. Concision should not turn a sentence into notes.

### Use grammatically complete prose by default

Use complete sentences for ordinary explanations. Arrows, missing verbs, and clipped noun strings are appropriate only where the medium supports recoverable compression.

> Compressed: "Datum ungültig → Abbruch, keine Speicherung."
>
> Explanatory: "Ist das Datum ungültig, bricht das Programm ab und speichert nichts."

Labels, headings, interface statuses, dialogue, and specific literary effects can legitimately use fragments. That exception does not justify an entire explanation made of dramatic sentence fragments.

### Preserve tense, aspect, and temporal reference

Choose tense according to temporal perspective, register, and context. Perfekt and Präteritum are not mechanical equivalents of English present perfect and simple past.

Präteritum is common in written narration; Perfekt is common in conversation and current accounts of completed events. Region, verb, and context affect the choice. Do not impose one tense on every kind of German prose.

Present forms can express scheduled or contextually established future events: "Der Kurs beginnt morgen." A "werden" construction is not obligatory whenever English uses a future form. Keep it where prediction or another relevant modal effect belongs.

An ongoing action often needs no special progressive form. "Ich lese gerade" can express a current activity. "Ich bin am Lesen" carries additional register and regional implications and should not become a universal translation pattern.

Preserve the time relationship when changing constructions. "Ich wohne seit drei Jahren hier" describes a continuing situation; a mechanically chosen past tense can imply that it has ended.

Use Plusquamperfekt where needed to orient an earlier event within a past account, without marking every earlier action repeatedly once the sequence is clear. Tense shifts should reflect time, perspective, or voice rather than accidental imitation of the source.

## Vocabulary and expression

### Prefer the familiar word that says the right thing

Choose the ordinary term when it expresses the concept accurately: "nutzen" or "verwenden" instead of "zur Anwendung bringen", "helfen" instead of "Hilfestellung leisten", and "wenn" instead of "für den Fall, dass", where the meaning is unchanged.

Precision can require a less common technical, historical, legal, or literary word. Familiarity alone does not determine accuracy. A rare term needs a reason beyond sounding sophisticated.

Natural German may use a compact compound where an English text uses several words. It may also use an ordinary verb where a literal translation produces a heavy abstraction. Judge the German expression, not the number of words in the source.

### Replace inflated wording with the actual meaning

Repeated elevated wording makes unrelated subjects sound alike. State the action, condition, result, or relationship rather than its supposed importance.

| Inflated or vague use | More direct German treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "eine entscheidende Rolle spielen" | "entscheidet über ...", "beeinflusst ...", or the actual relationship |
| "von zentraler Bedeutung sein" | State what depends on it, or remove the unsupported judgment. |
| "einen tiefen Einblick gewähren" | "zeigen", "erklären", "untersuchen", according to the actual contribution |
| "das volle Potenzial entfalten" | Name the capability, use, or supported improvement. |
| "neue Maßstäbe setzen" | Name the comparison and measured difference. |
| "nachhaltig verbessern" | Specify the duration and improvement, if known. |
| "ganzheitlich" | Name what is included and how its parts relate. |
| "nahtlos" | Describe the transition, compatibility, or absence of interruption. |
| "zukunftsweisend", "bahnbrechend", "revolutionär" | State the established change without declaring its historical importance. |
| "innovativ" | Name what is new and relevant. |
| "maßgeschneidert" | State which requirement the solution addresses. |
| "vielschichtig", "facettenreich" | Explain the particular distinctions. |
| "dynamische Landschaft" | Name the field, market, setting, or change. |
| "Synergien schaffen" | Explain what the combined work enables or saves. |
| "eindrucksvoll unterstreichen" | State the supported conclusion and its limits. |
| "als Beleg für ... stehen" | Name the evidence and the claim it supports. |

These expressions are not prohibited strings. A literally seamless material, a defined sustainability criterion, or a real change of scale can require the word. Empty praise is the defect. Substituting a grander synonym preserves it.

### Say "ist" or "hat" when that is the relationship

Use direct identification and possession. "Das Werkzeug ist ein Parser" is clearer than "Das Werkzeug fungiert als wegweisende Parsing-Lösung" when no additional function is meant.

Keep "dient", "fungiert", "verfügt über", or "zeichnet sich aus" only when it states a relevant distinction more accurately. "Der Raum dient auch als Archiv" identifies a use; "Das Haus verfügt über drei Eingänge" may simply be "Das Haus hat drei Eingänge".

Do not force every predicate into a forceful action verb. Classification, existence, possession, and state are legitimate meanings.

### Remove filler without removing meaning

| Padded expression | Direct expression when equivalent |
| --- | --- |
| "aufgrund der Tatsache, dass" | "weil" |
| "zu dem Zweck, dass" | "damit" |
| "zum Zwecke der Prüfung" | "zur Prüfung", or a clause naming who checks what |
| "im Hinblick auf die Frage, ob" | "zur Frage, ob", or the question itself |
| "an dieser Stelle sei erwähnt" | State the relevant information. |
| "es ist wichtig zu betonen, dass" | State the consequential fact and its consequence. |
| "abschließend lässt sich zusammenfassend festhalten" | Give a necessary synthesis or end the passage. |
| "unter Umständen möglicherweise" | Keep the qualification the evidence needs. |
| "in gewisser Weise", "gewissermaßen" | State the limited respect in which the claim holds. |
| "grundsätzlich", "prinzipiell" | Keep only a genuine distinction between the rule and exceptions. |
| "tatsächlich", "wirklich", "sehr" | Keep an actual correction, degree, or contrast. |

Do not replace a useful limitation with an absolute claim. "Grundsätzlich zulässig" can leave room for exceptions; deleting the adverb may change legal or practical meaning. Explain material exceptions instead of using a vague qualifier to conceal them.

Do not turn "ein wirklich guter Hinweis" into "ein hervorragender Hinweis" and assume it now contains information. Explain the hint's usefulness where relevant.

### Keep terminology stable

Use one name for an entity, operation, or defined concept. Repetition of an accurate noun is clearer than cycling through approximate synonyms.

Calling the same item "Antrag", "Anfrage", "Gesuch", and "Vorgang" can imply distinctions the system does not contain. A character need not become "der Protagonist", "der Held", and "die zentrale Figur" within one paragraph merely to avoid a repeated name.

Vary construction when sentences become monotonous. Do not vary technical meaning. Explain genuine terminology differences across institutions or regions rather than smoothing them away.

### Choose idiomatic combinations

German words combine according to established usage and the intended meaning. "Eine Entscheidung treffen", "einen Antrag stellen", and "Verantwortung übernehmen" are natural combinations. Their fluency does not depend on sounding like the source's verb.

An individually correct translation can form an unnatural phrase. Express the relationship in German instead of matching each source word. Distinguish a recognizable professional collocation from an invented combination that merely sounds formal.

Prepositions belong to the expression as well as the grammar. Keep an established pattern such as "Interesse an", "abhängen von", or "zuständig für" when that is the intended relation. Do not transfer a source preposition because the dictionary lists the same basic gloss.

Preserve differences between near-synonyms. "Erkennen", "anerkennen", "feststellen", and "wiedererkennen" describe different acts. Idiomatic phrasing must remain faithful to the particular act.

### Use compounds without hiding relationships

German noun compounds are productive and often precise. The final component normally determines the compound's grammatical category and gender. "Datenschutz" and "Bedienungsanleitung" are ordinary words, not defects to unpack automatically.

An unfamiliar compound can hide who acts, what belongs to what, or how components relate. Use a phrase or relative clause when it makes those relations clearer.

> Dense: "Kundendatenfreigabeantragsprüfung"
>
> Clearer, for one intended meaning: "Prüfung des Antrags auf Freigabe der Kundendaten"

The expanded form identifies one interpretation; a different intended grouping needs different wording. A hyphen can expose components but cannot resolve every semantic ambiguity.

Avoid noun strings copied with English spaces. Ordinary noun compounds are joined or hyphenated according to German rules. Joining and hyphenation details belong under punctuation and presentation; the writing choice here is whether the compound is intelligible and useful.

### Prefer direct verbs without banning nominal expressions

Use a direct verb where it preserves the action: "Wir prüfen den Antrag" often improves "Wir nehmen eine Prüfung des Antrags vor". "Das Team entschied" may express "Das Team traf eine Entscheidung" more economically.

An established functional verb construction can express aspect, state, or perspective. "In Gang bringen" differs from "in Gang halten". "Zur Verfügung stehen" and "zur Verfügung stellen" distinguish availability from making something available.

Keep an abstract noun when the concept is itself the subject: "Die Authentifizierung ist fehlgeschlagen" can name the exact event. Do not replace it with an unspecified human failure.

Remove chains that hide the relation among actions. "Nach Abschluss der Durchführung der Überprüfung" usually needs a clearer statement of what finishes before what begins. Brevity is not the sole criterion; the revision should expose the dependency.

### Choose verbs that preserve the actual action

A precise verb can replace a weak verb plus modifier when it describes the same action. "Schlurfte" may replace "ging langsam" if the gait really is a shuffle. "Flüsterte" adds a kind of speech that "sagte leise" does not always imply.

Do not remove every adverb. Scope, frequency, degree, sequence, and uncertainty can depend on one: "scheitert häufig" differs from "scheitert". An ordinary phrase can be more accurate than a conspicuous verb.

English verbs with broad meanings may require different German verbs in different contexts. Choose the actual operation or relationship. Do not replace a translated catch-all with another catch-all such as "realisieren" or "adressieren" throughout.

### Handle false friends, calques, and anglicisms by meaning

Use the German word for the intended concept, not the nearest-looking form.

| German expression | Meaning to preserve |
| --- | --- |
| "eventuell" | A possibility; it does not mean an eventual outcome. |
| "aktuell" | Current or topical; it does not automatically assert actuality. |
| "sensibel" | Sensitive; it does not normally mean sensible or reasonable. |
| "bekommen" | Receive or obtain; it does not describe becoming something. |
| "konsequent" | Consistent or resolute in a relevant course; it does not mean every consequential event. |
| "Kontrolle" | Can mean checking or supervision; not every kind of command or influence. |
| "realisieren" | Can mean putting something into effect or, in established usage, becoming aware of it; context decides. |

Do not invent German phrasings by preserving an English metaphor or verb combination. A literal phrase may be understandable yet conspicuously translated. State the intended relation in established German.

Borrowings are appropriate when they are established, necessary, or useful to the reader. "Software" does not need a strained replacement. Unexplained "Alignment", "Impact", and "Ownership" can conceal ordinary distinctions among agreement, effect, and responsibility; name the relevant distinction.

Integrate borrowed terms into German grammar where appropriate. Preserve official product names, identifiers, and exact labels. Avoid alternating German and borrowed terms for the same concept without a reason.

Do not impose purist bans on established usage. "Sinn machen" can suit conversational German; "Sinn ergeben" may fit a more formal passage. Register and context matter more than the origin story of a phrase.

### Use modal particles for a real interpersonal effect

Particles such as "ja", "doch", "denn", "mal", "eben", and "halt" can express shared assumptions, expectation, insistence, resignation, or an invitation. They are not automatically disposable filler.

> "Das weißt du."
>
> "Das weißt du doch."

The second can appeal to knowledge the speaker assumes is shared. Depending on context, it can reassure, reproach, or insist. Removing the particle can change that stance.

"Was ist denn passiert?" and "Komm mal her" differ from their particle-free forms in interpersonal effect. Add particles only where that effect belongs. Their distribution depends on clause type and construction; they cannot be scattered freely.

Avoid "Das ist doch ganz einfach" when it implies the reader should already understand. Do not fill formal explanation with particles to imitate spontaneous speech. In dialogue, let them reflect the speaker and relationship.

### Use literal meaning and purposeful imagery

In explanatory prose, name the operation, relation, responsibility, or goal. Technical-sounding metaphors can obscure an ordinary requirement.

| Vague metaphor or abstraction | More direct German treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "die Weichen stellen" | Name the decision and its consequence. |
| "den Hebel ansetzen" | Name the parameter, intervention, or responsibility. |
| "als Nordstern dienen" | Name the goal or decision criterion. |
| "ein Fundament schaffen" | State the prerequisite or supporting work. |
| "ein Ökosystem aufbauen" | Name the interacting services, organizations, or conditions. |
| "eine Brücke schlagen" | Explain what is connected and how. |
| "Komplexität navigieren" | Name the difficult choices and constraints. |
| "eine Stellschraube drehen" | Name the adjustable value and its effect. |
| "das Thema auf die nächste Ebene heben" | State the actual change. |
| "ein Spannungsfeld" | Identify the competing requirements or interests. |
| "ein Paradigmenwechsel" | Explain the change in assumptions or method. |
| "ein Katalysator" | State the process accelerated and the evidence for that role. |
| "eine neue Dimension eröffnen" | State the new capability or implication. |
| "ein Gewebe aus Erinnerungen" | Give the particular memory or relation. |

Literal and established technical meanings remain available. An ecological system, a chemical catalyst, a mathematical dimension, or an actual bridge can require the word.

Do not replace a vague metaphor with another. A compressed slogan such as "Anbinden oder abschalten" may conceal the actual requirement. State the conditions and action.

In narrative, an image can create an experience that literal summary cannot. Let it arise from the character, world, and moment. Its particulars should matter more than a reusable atmosphere.

### Do not assign unsupported intentions

Describe animal behavior, system operation, or a process's effect without inventing thoughts or motives. An observed behavior does not establish the imagined message "Das gehört mir".

Ordinary technical shorthand such as "Der Server weist die Anfrage zurück" describes behavior. A claim that a product "versteht, was Sie brauchen" needs a precise explanation of the actual capability.

Personification can serve a literary perception without asserting literal intention. Keep that function distinct from factual explanation.

### Express enthusiasm proportionately

Let the material establish interest. Avoid superlative chains, ceremonial praise, exclamation marks, flattering openings, and invitations to marvel together.

A measured tone can express pleasure, anger, grief, curiosity, or conviction. It need not sound emotionally absent. Choose the degree appropriate to the event and voice.

"Das ist wirklich beeindruckend" does not become evidence when revised to "Das ist außerordentlich bemerkenswert". State the supported achievement or let a relevant personal judgment remain clearly a judgment.

## Rhythm and coherence

### Vary sentence movement for meaning

Short sentences can establish a point or mark a turn. Longer sentences can carry a connected condition, explanation, or sustained perception. Choose length according to the thought, not a numerical pattern.

German permits meaningful variation in what precedes the finite verb. Use that resource to maintain a topic or shift emphasis, as explained under syntax. Do not force inversion, a subordinate-clause opening, and a subject opening into a repeating cycle.

Consider how a sentence unfolds aloud, including where its verb bracket closes and its emphasis lands. This does not mean reproducing spoken hesitations in edited prose. Written rhythm can be deliberate and still natural.

### Avoid both monotony and artificial variety

Repeated "Es ist ...", "Dabei ...", or "Dies zeigt ..." can obscure differences among claims. Replacing them with a succession of equally empty introductions changes little.

> Mechanical: "Ich denke, der Vorschlag ist sinnvoll. Ich denke, wir sollten ihn erproben. Ich denke, er wird funktionieren."
>
> More direct: "Der Vorschlag erscheint mir sinnvoll. Wir sollten ihn erproben. Ich erwarte, dass er funktioniert."

The revision retains judgment and prediction rather than declaring success. Varying phrasing does not authorize stronger certainty.

Avoid both extended subordinate-clause chains and a series of isolated main clauses that makes every statement equally abrupt. Link, subordinate, or separate according to meaning. A repeated pattern may be purposeful in dialogue, instruction, or rhetoric.

Do not insert unusual vocabulary, mistakes, abrupt fragments, or digressions to reduce predictability. Neither smoothness nor irregularity alone determines quality.

### Let paragraph length follow the material

Give a difficult explanation enough space and an important narrative moment enough development. A short paragraph can emphasize a genuine turn; using one for every sentence makes emphasis meaningless.

Paragraph breaks can mark changes in thought, speaker, time, scene, or attention. Use them according to those functions. Uniform lengths are not a standard, and constant variation is not one either.

German compounds and clause structure make direct sentence-length comparisons with English unreliable as quality rules. Do not stretch or compress a German paragraph to match the source's word count. Preserve its role and relevant information.

## Punctuation and presentation

### Use German grammatical punctuation

Use periods and commas for ordinary separation. Punctuation should clarify grammatical structure and meaningful boundaries rather than imitate pauses in an English source.

Separate introduced subordinate clauses and relative clauses as required. Enclose an inserted subordinate clause with commas on both sides.

> "Wenn die Datei fehlt, endet der Vorgang."
>
> "Die Datei, die gestern erstellt wurde, ist beschädigt."

A comma separates a sentential infinitive group from its governing clause under current standard rules: "Sie versucht, die Datei zu öffnen." The same applies when it precedes or interrupts the governing clause. Distinguish such groups from infinitives that belong to a shared verbal construction, as in "Sie braucht nicht zu kommen", and from non-sentential uses. Do not insert a comma before every occurrence of "zu".

German can join coordinated independent clauses with a comma: "Sie öffnete die Datei, er prüfte die Angaben." The English prohibition on comma splices does not transfer unchanged. Choose a period or conjunction when the relation or sentence density calls for it.

In ordinary enumerations with "und" or "oder", do not add an English serial comma mechanically. Commas that close embedded clauses still remain. Punctuation must follow the actual construction.

### Keep sentence punctuation restrained

Do not use em dashes, en dashes, or substitute hyphens as sentence separators. Integrate a short addition grammatically, use a separate sentence, or use appropriate parentheses where the material is genuinely supplementary.

Parentheses can contain a brief aside, abbreviation, or necessary qualification. Do not put an essential condition where readers are encouraged to skip it. Repeated parenthetical commentary can fracture an otherwise direct explanation.

A semicolon can separate closely related clauses or complex list items when that relation is useful. It should not become a substitute dash or an ornamental mark of sophistication. Prefer a period when no special connection needs marking.

Use question and exclamation marks according to communicative function. Avoid repeated rhetorical questions, multiple exclamation marks, and ellipses used merely to manufacture suspense.

Preserve exact punctuation in faithful quotations, identifiers, commands, and required notation. Meaningful lexical hyphens and conventional ranges are not sentence dashes.

### Give colons an actual introductory function

A colon can introduce a list, example, quotation, explanation, or elaboration. The preceding wording should establish what follows. Do not use a colon as an automatic bridge after every condition, heading, or bold label.

> Awkward: "Wenn Sie die Einstellungen ändern: Starten Sie den Dienst neu."
>
> Clear: "Starten Sie den Dienst neu, nachdem Sie die Einstellungen geändert haben."

After a colon, capitalize an independent sentence or direct speech as required. A phrase or list item does not acquire an initial capital solely because of the colon, although nouns and other normally capitalized words retain theirs.

> "Die Meldung ist eindeutig: Die Datei fehlt."
>
> "Für den Antrag sind zwei Unterlagen nötig: ein Ausweis und zwei Fotos."

Keep colons inside times, paths, identifiers, and formal notation exact. A technical colon is not an editorial transition.

### Use straight quotation marks

Use straight double quotation marks for authored German examples, dialogue, and quoted expressions. This is a house preference rather than a claim about traditional German typography. Use straight single quotation marks for a quotation within a double-quoted passage where needed.

Apply German punctuation around the quoted material.

> "Ich komme", sagte sie.
>
> "Kommst du?", fragte sie.
>
> Sie sagte: "Ich komme."

Do not place surrounding commas inside quotation marks simply because an English convention does so. Preserve sentence punctuation that belongs to the quotation, including its question or exclamation mark. A following reporting clause still receives the required comma.

Keep faithful source quotations and required publication notation intact. Do not use quotation marks as decorative emphasis or to insinuate doubt about an ordinary term without stating the reason.

### Capitalize German words, not English title patterns

Capitalize nouns and substantivized expressions according to German rules, including inside headings. Sentence-case headings retain those capitals.

> Informative heading: "Wann die Sicherung abläuft"
>
> Vague heading: "Wichtige Überlegungen"

Do not capitalize every significant-looking word as in an English title pattern. Keep adjectives lowercase unless their grammatical or naming function requires a capital. Proper names retain their established spelling.

Capitalize formal "Sie", "Ihnen", and possessive "Ihr". Ordinary "du", "ihr", and their possessives are lowercase; capitalization can be appropriate in personal correspondence under the applicable convention. Distinguish formal "Sie" from third-person "sie" and possessive "Ihr" from unrelated "ihr" by meaning.

Use a logical heading hierarchy and enough headings to support navigation. A short paragraph does not need a decorative title.

### Join and hyphenate compounds correctly

Write ordinary noun compounds together unless a hyphen is required or usefully clarifies their structure. Avoid marketing-style spaces that leave the grammatical relationship implicit.

> "Kundenkonto", not "Kunden Konto"
>
> "E-Mail-Adresse"
>
> "3D-Drucker"
>
> "Ein- und Ausgang"

Compounds with abbreviations, numerals, or embedded word groups can require hyphens. Where a multiword name becomes part of a larger compound, preserve an intelligible connection according to the applicable spelling rules. Official names may have their own exact forms.

Use a hyphen to clarify an unusual compound when appropriate; do not split every familiar compound into components. Supplementary hyphens show omitted shared elements, not a general permission to abbreviate prose arbitrarily.

German does not use an English possessive apostrophe before every genitive "s". "Marias Antrag" normally has none; names ending in a relevant sibilant can require an apostrophe, as in "Max' Antrag". Preserve established names and exact identifiers.

### Present numbers, dates, and units for the intended locale

Use unambiguous German presentation appropriate to the audience. Decimal commas are usual in German prose: "1,5 Liter". Grouping conventions for larger numbers vary by locale and publication; do not confuse a grouping separator with a decimal point.

Separate a number from its unit or percent sign according to the applicable convention, preferably with a nonbreaking space where the medium supports it: "20 %", "15 km", "23 °C". Exact code, exported data, identifiers, and required scientific notation retain their own syntax.

Spell a month out where an international numeric date could be misread: "3. April 2026". German numeric dates commonly follow day, month, year. Preserve the actual date and timezone significance where relevant rather than localizing the appearance alone.

Distinguish local formatting from conversion. Changing a decimal separator does not change a value. Converting currencies, measurements, or timezones requires a reliable basis and must retain relevant precision and reference conditions.

Use German unit and currency names naturally without changing the underlying amount. Do not silently turn a dollar price into a euro price or localize a location that belongs to a factual account.

### Use emphasis sparingly

Bold text should help readers find an important distinction or required interface label. Do not bold every key noun, acronym, opening phrase, or repeated term.

Avoid a bold label followed by a sentence that repeats it. "Leistung: Die Leistung wurde verbessert" contributes no meaningful distinction. A useful label introduces new information.

Use italics or quotation marks for a genuine linguistic or publication function rather than habitual emphasis. Multiple competing emphasis systems make the hierarchy harder to read.

### Let lists express actual structure

Use numbered lists for ordered sequences and bullets for parallel unordered items. Give list items parallel grammar. Introduce a list with enough context to show what its items represent.

Whole-sentence items retain sentence punctuation. Fragments and syntactically integrated items follow a coherent presentation convention suited to their construction. Do not force labels into full sentences when the medium needs labels.

Use paragraphs for connected argument. A list of fragments cannot replace an explanation of relationships. Tables suit comparisons and mappings. Do not force three items, three examples, or three clauses onto material with another natural shape.

### Remove decorative interface and chatbot residue

Begin with the useful information. Omit automatic acknowledgments, flattering introductions, congratulations, repeated offers of help, and sign-offs from substantive prose.

Typical padding includes "Natürlich!", "Sehr gerne!", "Eine ausgezeichnete Frage!", "Du hast absolut recht", "Ich hoffe, das hilft dir weiter", and "Lass mich wissen, wenn du weitere Fragen hast". A real greeting or closing can belong to correspondence; a quoted exchange can require the words. Their function determines whether they stay.

Omit decorative emojis from headings and lists. Keep symbols that are data, required interface content, or the subject of discussion.

Remove commentary about composing the answer, generic assurances of completeness, and self-praise about clarity. A finished passage should provide the material rather than announce the quality of its own presentation.

## Style-specific conventions

Apply the general principles throughout. Each style adds requirements suited to its purpose and narrow exceptions that perform a concrete function. A document may contain several forms, such as an explanatory introduction followed by instructions. Keep each passage appropriate to its role.

Style does not authorize factual invention, unsupported certainty, decorative terminology, mechanical repetition, or obscure syntax. A literary device needs a narrative purpose; a scholarly convention needs a scholarly function. Neither creates a general exemption from clarity.

### Academic

#### Formality with accessible language

Use precise, accessible German suited to the scholarly context. Avoid slang, promotional claims, conversational padding, forced intimacy, and ceremonial abstraction. Formality comes from disciplined claims and explicit reasoning.

> Inflated: "Die vorliegende Untersuchung unternimmt den Versuch, das vielschichtige Zusammenspiel der vorgenannten Einflussfaktoren einer eingehenden Betrachtung zu unterziehen."
>
> Direct: "Diese Untersuchung analysiert das Zusammenspiel der genannten Einflussfaktoren."

Name the factors where the surrounding text does not identify them. A defined statistical interaction needs its proper term; a loose paraphrase about interplay may change the claim.

German academic prose can be readable without becoming colloquial. Avoid choosing uncommon words, Latin expressions, or extended nominal groups merely to advertise intellectual seriousness. Familiar terminology can carry a complex argument.

#### Disciplinary voice and researcher position

Use first person when it clarifies an argument, decision, or relevant relationship to the material. "Ich verstehe die Passage als Einwand gegen diese Deutung" identifies an interpretation as the writer's. "Wir schlossen unvollständige Antworten aus" identifies agency.

First person is neither universally required nor universally forbidden. Follow the particular field, form, and venue without assigning one voice to an entire discipline. Passive voice can foreground a method or specimen while active voice identifies a decision. Avoid "der Verfasser" as a cumbersome substitute for an otherwise appropriate "ich".

| Kind of writing | Relevant emphasis |
| --- | --- |
| Empirical research | Distinguish observations, reported experiences, analytical choices, and interpretations. Make consequential researcher agency visible. |
| Interpretive scholarship | Ground readings in the text, record, object, or experience. Explain how particular evidence supports the interpretation. |
| Theoretical argument | Define concepts, expose assumptions, develop relationships, and separate proposed propositions from established findings. |
| Practitioner-facing research | Explain practical consequences, intended users, conditions, and constraints without promising unsupported outcomes. |

Where positionality affects interpretation, describe the relevant access, involvement, institutional role, or experience. A generic declaration of neutrality or a personal confession does not explain that relationship. Keep participants' perspectives distinguishable from researcher interpretation.

#### Research questions and contributions

State the question precisely enough to identify the relevant phenomenon, population or material, setting, and relationship. Distinguish descriptive, comparative, predictive, causal, and exploratory purposes. The question answered should match the problem established.

Describe the contribution as a specific addition to evidence, understanding, method, or interpretation. Relate it to the nearest relevant work. Another prevalence estimate does not by itself answer a gap concerning a mechanism.

Bound novelty claims by the literature actually covered. "Soweit bekannt" does not explain the scope of that knowledge. Where an absence claim matters, state the relevant scope and date. A limitation of a review is not automatically an absence across the field.

> Overbroad: "Zu diesem Problem liegt bislang keinerlei Forschung vor."
>
> More specific form: "Die berücksichtigten Studien untersuchen die Häufigkeit an städtischen Schulen. Über den zugrunde liegenden Mechanismus an ländlichen Schulen ist weniger bekannt."

Both statements in the alternative require evidence. Do not invent an unexplored population or mechanism to make a contribution appear distinctive.

#### Titles, abstracts, and keywords

A title should identify the central subject and the scope needed to distinguish the paper. Prefer meaningful concepts to empty openers such as "Eine Untersuchung über". Include population, setting, material, or design where it changes what readers should expect.

A declarative title needs the same evidential restraint as the paper. A question title should identify a question the work actually addresses. Brevity, wordplay, or an attractive claim does not justify removing a decisive qualification.

An abstract should stand alone. Represent the purpose, approach, principal findings, and contribution accurately. Include decisive sample or contextual information and relevant limits. Report findings rather than announcing that results will be discussed.

Keep abstract and body consistent. Do not add a causal claim, recommendation, or numerical result absent from the paper. Define unfamiliar abbreviations sufficiently for independent reading.

Structured and unstructured abstracts serve different forms. Use the applicable form without imposing a universal number of words or sentences. Remove repeated background before removing a qualification that determines truth.

Keywords should identify useful concepts, populations, settings, or distinctive methods. Avoid generic entries such as "Forschung" and "Untersuchung" unless those concepts are the subject. Align terminology across title, abstract, keywords, and main text.

#### Literature synthesis

Organize literature around questions, findings, methods, theoretical positions, or disagreements relevant to the argument. Use chronology when development over time matters. An author-by-author list is insufficient where the reader needs relationships.

Explain agreement, conflict, and dependence on measurement, population, design, or context. Distinguish an author's argument from an empirical result. Group references that support the same proposition and separate references that support different ones.

> Inventory: "Eine Studie untersuchte die Teilnahme. Eine weitere Studie befasste sich ebenfalls mit der Teilnahme. Eine dritte Studie diskutierte dasselbe Thema."
>
> Synthesis form: "Die Studien berichten übereinstimmend von einer sinkenden Teilnahme, erfassen jedoch unterschiedliche Zeiträume. Nur die Längsschnittstudie begleitet dieselben Personen über mehrere Jahre."

The synthesis requires those relationships to be established. Do not invent two opposing camps, agreement, or a shared mechanism for rhetorical neatness.

Explain why a reported gap matters. Limited coverage of a location does not automatically justify another study there. Keep foundational work where it establishes conceptual origins and current evidence where claims depend on recent developments. Age and prestige alone do not determine relevance.

#### Sections that perform distinct functions

Let the article type and reasoning determine the organization. Functions may occupy separate sections or be combined where the form requires.

| Section | Writing function |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction | Establish the problem, necessary context, uncertainty, question, and contribution without a sweeping historical preamble. |
| Literature or conceptual background | Establish the concepts and evidence the argument depends on; explain the unresolved issue. |
| Methods | Describe the design, participants or material, measures, procedures, and analysis sufficiently to understand the evidence and consequential choices. |
| Results or findings | Present relevant findings, including negative and inconclusive ones; lead with what was found rather than an unexplained test name. |
| Discussion | Explain meaning, relation to prior work, alternatives, limits, and supported implications. Separate these from proposed future work. |
| Conclusion | State the established contribution and essential bounds without new evidence or a stronger version of the finding. |

Where findings and discussion are separated, retain the difference between reporting evidence and explaining implications. Qualitative and interpretive work may integrate evidence and interpretation more closely. A section label does not require concealing the explanation needed to understand a finding.

| Form | Additional structural emphasis |
| --- | --- |
| Empirical paper | Connect question, design, findings, and interpretation. State what the design permits. |
| Research review | Explain coverage and selection principles; distinguish systematic coverage from selective or interpretive synthesis. |
| Theoretical paper | Establish the problem in an existing account, develop the reasoning, and identify what the proposed account explains or leaves open. |
| Case study | Supply context, selection rationale, evidence, and analytical relevance; bound transfer beyond the case. |
| Research proposal | Separate completed work and existing evidence from planned methods, expected outcomes, and possible benefits. |
| Policy brief | Present the problem, evidence, options, tradeoffs, recommendation, and relevant constraints for the intended decision-maker. Distinguish findings from value judgments. |

These are available forms, not mandatory outlines or word allocations. Use only the divisions and signposting the argument requires.

#### Methods and findings in concrete terms

Describe what was actually done, measured, excluded, and observed. Specify relevant selection, recruitment, material, timing, instruments, missing data, and analytical choices. Do not fill unknown details with plausible conventions.

Use "zufällig zugewiesen" only for actual random assignment. Distinguish a planned procedure from completed work and an observed practice from formal approval.

Define constructs operationally where needed. A self-report of confidence is not direct measurement of competence. An interview account differs from observation of behavior. Name the distinction without dismissing a method appropriate to the question.

Separate prior hypotheses from explanations developed after seeing the findings. Describe exploratory analyses as exploratory. An unexpected result can matter without being rewritten as a prediction.

For qualitative findings, make the relationship between interpretation and supporting material intelligible. Preserve participants' words, relevant context, and the meaning of translated terms. A quotation can illustrate a theme without establishing its prevalence. Counts of coded interviews require their denominator and do not automatically describe a wider population.

#### Statistical findings with substantive meaning

Give important estimates the sample information, magnitude, units, and uncertainty needed for interpretation. Supply the statistic, degrees of freedom, exact probability value, effect measure, and interval where relevant to the method and publication.

Explain the substantive result instead of leaving symbols to carry the conclusion. Distinguish standard deviation, standard error, and intervals for estimates. Identify adjusted estimates, comparison groups, and relevant differences between the original sample and the analytical sample.

Describe multiplicity adjustments, sensitivity analyses, and consequential departures from the stated analysis when they affect interpretation. Do not call a finding "robust" solely because one alternative specification produces a similar result.

Report relevant negative, contradictory, and inconclusive findings. Do not hide them through an outcome change, omitted subgroup, or selective emphasis on an exploratory pattern.

Keep numbers, directions, labels, and qualifications consistent across prose, tables, figures, titles, and abstracts. Use tables for detail that benefits from comparison; use prose for the principal finding and its meaning. Captions and notes need the sample, measures, units, abbreviations, uncertainty, and source information required to understand the display.

#### Claims, evidence, and limits

Keep the claim tied to the actual design. A survey account of productivity is not direct measurement of productivity, and an association does not establish a causal effect.

> Too strong: "Die Befragung beweist, dass Homeoffice die Produktivität steigert."
>
> Bounded, if supported: "In dieser Befragung berichteten Beschäftigte im Homeoffice von höherer Produktivität."

Place material limits beside the claim they qualify. The distinction between evidence, interpretation, attribution, and modal strength follows the general evidence conventions. Scholarly tone does not require stacking "möglicherweise", "vermutlich", and "es ließe sich annehmen".

Do not append a generic limitations paragraph while leaving earlier claims overstated. A limitation should explain its actual consequence for interpretation, transfer, or precision.

#### Substantive sentences and stable terminology

Each sentence should establish a necessary fact, define a concept, explain a relationship, supply evidence, or delimit the argument. Avoid passages that merely assert the topic's importance.

Use the same name for a construct, measure, group, or method. Rephrase repetition without replacing a defined term with a near-synonym.

> Empty repetition: "Die Ergebnisse sind von großer Bedeutung. Diese bedeutsamen Befunde haben wichtige Implikationen."
>
> Substantive treatment: State the particular implication and the evidence supporting it.

Do not introduce a fashionable conceptual label when the passage only means a familiar relationship. Where a specialized distinction is necessary, define it and use it accurately.

#### Syntax that makes reasoning explicit

Keep the main proposition visible. German subordination can express actual conditions, dependencies, and concessions; it should not conceal the assertion inside several levels of qualification.

Use active voice to identify decisions and passive voice to foreground relevant material or methods. Avoid impersonal constructions that obscure who made an interpretive choice.

Completed procedures and observations normally concern the past; definitions, the present argument, and continuing propositions can use the present. A literary work may be discussed in the present while its publication history remains past. Preserve the difference between a reported finding and an asserted continuing fact.

Do not imitate English academic syntax with a succession of trailing participles. State the inferential step directly. Nor should an English abstract's compact structure force a German sentence to retain every qualification in one clause.

#### Necessary formal conventions

Use the required citation system consistently. Author-date and numbered systems organize references differently; neither is a universal rule of German scholarship. Keep reference entries, quotation locators, and attached claims aligned.

Preserve required parentheses, brackets, abbreviations, title capitalization, bibliographic punctuation, statistical symbols, equations, variables, and units. Required notation can differ from ordinary prose and locale formatting.

Formal requirements do not justify stacks of optional asides, needless Latin, or words selected merely for academic appearance. Keep exact quoted wording distinct from the surrounding house typography.

#### Correspondence and declarations

Academic correspondence should identify the substantive point in professional German. A submission letter can state the question, contribution, and relevance without praising the journal or inflating novelty.

A response to criticism should identify the issue, explain the change or reasoned disagreement, and locate the relevant passage where useful. Separate completed changes from proposals. Formulaic gratitude does not answer a methodological objection; respect does not require accepting an unsupported criticism.

State contributions, funding, conflicts, ethics approval, consent, assistance, and data or code availability accurately when required. Describe actual activities rather than vague assurances. Do not imply approval, access, preregistration, independent review, or verification that did not occur.

Distinguish open material, restricted access, access on request, and material that cannot be shared. Give real conditions. Describe relevant assistance by its actual use and extent without transferring responsibility for the claims to a person or tool.

#### Worked example

> Before: "Es ist wichtig hervorzuheben, dass unsere Ergebnisse eindeutig beweisen, dass alle Teilnehmenden von der Maßnahme profitierten, was auf einen revolutionären Fortschritt hindeuten könnte."
>
> Bounded alternative, for evidence supporting only reported benefits: "Teilnehmende berichteten nach der Maßnahme von Verbesserungen. Die Untersuchung zeigt nicht, ob die Maßnahme diese Veränderungen verursacht hat."

The alternative changes an overstated passage into a different, bounded account. It requires evidence for both the reported improvements and the inferential limitation. If the evidence supports benefits for every participant or a causal conclusion, preserve that information instead of weakening it automatically.

### Technical

#### Organize around the reader's purpose

A technical document should support one main purpose. Identify substantial material serving another purpose separately and connect it with a descriptive link where appropriate.

| Form | Reader's need | Writing requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tutorial | Learn through a successful practical experience. | State what the reader builds, provide a supported sequence and visible results, and keep background proportionate. |
| How-to | Complete a specific task. | Assume relevant competence, name necessary actions and choices, and keep unrelated explanation outside the sequence. |
| Reference | Find dependable facts. | State options, behavior, constraints, and errors in the system's recognizable structure. |
| Explanation | Understand a bounded subject and its reasons. | Explain design decisions, context, alternatives, and tradeoffs apart from a task sequence. |

A tutorial can use "wir" for a genuinely shared learning sequence. Instructions can address readers formally or informally according to the established voice. A reference normally describes the system; an explanation can state reasoned design judgments.

Use task titles such as "Eine Instanz erstellen" and concept titles such as "Aufbau des Speichers". Do not force the English wording of a title into German if a natural verb phrase is clearer.

#### Make instructions actionable

Use direct commands in the established address: "Wählen Sie Speichern" or "Wähle Speichern". Use a neutral infinitive for suitable labels and task headings: "Datei speichern". Do not alternate these forms randomly within a sequence.

Give each instruction one principal action. Put warnings and prerequisites before the action they govern. State the common case before exceptions unless an exception must be known first to prevent a consequential mistake.

Describe the visible result where it helps readers recognize progress: an output, changed state, prompt, or relevant log entry. Do not invent a screen state or claim a command succeeded without evidence.

Avoid routine "bitte" in every procedural step. Do not minimize effort with "einfach", "problemlos", "nur schnell", or "selbstverständlich". A supported duration can be stated separately.

Use the present for current behavior. Distinguish actual future plans from existing capability. English future wording does not require "wird" in every German instruction.

#### Keep terminology and syntax dependable

Use the actual symbol, operation, file, flag, command, and interface label. Keep one meaning for each term. Distinguish "prüfen", "validieren", "aktivieren", and "auswählen" where they name different actions.

Explain necessary terms near first relevant use. Avoid idioms, figurative operation names, unnecessary Latin abbreviations, and unfamiliar borrowed vocabulary that makes interpretation harder.

Keep conditions close to the actions they govern, with a manageable sentence bracket. An instruction with several exclusions may need separate sentences or a clearly structured list. Length is a density signal, not a fixed German word limit.

Preserve case and agreement around identifiers. Rephrase with a suitable generic noun when an identifier cannot be inflected naturally. Do not alter the identifier to make the grammar easier.

#### Preserve exact notation

Use explicit alternatives when a slash or "und/oder" hides the intended grouping. "A, B oder beides" expresses an inclusive choice where that is the meaning. Distinguish it from "entweder A oder B".

Avoid improvised singular-plural forms such as "Datei(en)" in explanatory prose. State the actual number where relevant or choose a grammatical formulation covering the intended cases.

Format code, commands, flags, and paths as code. Preserve their exact characters, case, punctuation, and meaningful whitespace. Retain tabs or spaces according to the language and project, rather than imposing a prose preference on code.

Keep interpolation variables, markup, links, and placeholders intact. German agreement and sentence order may require rearranging surrounding prose, not changing a variable name or translating a command.

Use real names and paths in factual documentation. Mark illustrative values as examples. Counts and directory trees must match the state described; include their reproducible basis where it matters.

#### Make documents easy to navigate

Use informative task and concept headings with a logical hierarchy. Prefer descriptive links to "hier klicken". Put essential context on the current page instead of requiring a separate link for the basic explanation.

Use German list punctuation rather than an English serial-comma rule. Avoid "usw." when it hides an unspecified category; state whether a list is partial and what its boundary is.

Name interface controls accurately, including their language. Do not translate an actual English-language control into a nonexistent German label. The explanatory prose can remain German while the exact label retains its displayed wording.

#### Keep change descriptions focused

A change description or commit message should identify the concrete problem and resulting behavior. Include the condition exposing the problem and the relevant evidence.

Extensive logs, commit lists, and measurement tables should support the explanation rather than replace it. Distinguish observed behavior, intended behavior, and unverified expectation.

Interface text follows its screen and action. Documentation conventions do not justify a paragraph inside a button. A German action label may use an infinitive; a status message should distinguish an available action from an action already completed.

#### Example

> Dense: "Die Konfiguration der Parameter des Importbudgetprüfungsskripts erfolgt über die Datei `budget.json`. Es ist zu beachten, dass die Ausführung mit `--write`, wodurch der hinterlegte Wert an die aktuelle Anzahl angepasst wird, ausschließlich bei einer Absenkung vorzunehmen ist. Bei Überschreitung schlägt die Prüfung fehl."
>
> Clearer: "Das Skript liest das Importbudget aus `budget.json`. Überschreitet die aktuelle Anzahl das Budget, schlägt die Prüfung fehl. Verwenden Sie `--write` nur, um das hinterlegte Budget zu senken."

The alternative preserves the file, condition, and restricted action without inventing behavior. Retain any additional condition required to use the command correctly.

### Absolute

#### Directness without lost meaning

State the useful answer in plain German. Keep the reason, condition, consequence, or necessary limitation close to it. Remove unnecessary jargon and explain required terms through their actual effect.

Keep the tone neutral and coherent. Directness does not require bluntness, hostility, excessive certainty, or an imitation of English compressed notes.

#### Complete sentences with no padding

Omit greetings, pre-narration, decorative headings, repeated conclusions, flattering acknowledgments, and invitations to continue. Retain articles, verbs, case endings, and other elements the reader needs.

> Compressed: "Token fehlt. Neuer Versuch sinnlos."
>
> Clear: "Der Anfrage fehlt ein Token. Fügen Sie eines hinzu, bevor Sie die Anfrage erneut senden."

The alternative assumes the missing token is established and can be supplied. If that is only a possibility, preserve the uncertainty or state the relevant condition.

Use a fragment when the medium genuinely calls for one, such as an action label. Do not make ordinary explanation a succession of labels.

#### Necessary explanation stays

Keep a qualification that changes the answer, a warning that affects an action, and the difference between recommendation and requirement.

> Insufficient: "Löschen Sie die Datei."
>
> Sufficient for the stated case: "Löschen Sie das Duplikat erst, nachdem Sie geprüft haben, dass sich die Originaldatei öffnen lässt."

The condition is part of the recommendation. If it does not belong to the actual case, do not invent it as a generic precaution. A direct answer should be sufficient for its real stakes.

#### Worked example

> Before: "Selbstverständlich! Auf einer übergeordneten Ebene scheint hier möglicherweise eine authentifizierungsbezogene Problematik vorzuliegen, der potenziell durch eine Erneuerung des Tokens begegnet werden könnte."
>
> After, if expiry is the relevant possibility: "Das Token könnte abgelaufen sein. Erneuern Sie es und senden Sie die Anfrage erneut."

If expiry is established, "Das Token ist abgelaufen" is appropriate. If renewal requires a condition or a different procedure, keep it. Directness is not permission to replace a broad unknown with a convenient diagnosis.

### Article

#### Concrete material and a reason to read

Lead with an artifact, observation, example, event, supported number, or specific tension. Let it establish why the subject matters. Introduce the broader point after concrete material where that order helps comprehension.

Avoid sweeping claims about "unsere schnelllebige Zeit", irrelevant biography, and announcements that the article will explore its topic. An opening need not be loud; it should give the reader a reason to continue.

German articles can be restrained, expansive, skeptical, reflective, or conversational. Do not impose a generic local personality or translate an English promotional voice word for word.

#### Headlines, standfirsts, and news leads

A headline should reflect the story at the level of certainty its evidence supports. Preserve consequential population, timing, and scope. A question headline must not insinuate an allegation the article cannot substantiate.

Search-friendly wording and brevity do not justify exaggeration. An idiomatic German headline can use appropriate compression, but must not turn an allegation into fact or a proposal into a decision.

A standfirst should add useful development, context, or stakes rather than repeat the headline in synonyms. Keep factual scope consistent across headline, standfirst, and body.

A news lead establishes the consequential development and essential people, place, or timing. Do not force every available fact into its first sentence. Prefer the decision or consequence to the fact that a routine meeting occurred.

> Meeting-centered: "Der Rat beriet am Dienstag über die Fahrpreise."
>
> Outcome-centered, if established: "Der Rat beschloss am Dienstag, den Fahrpreis im Januar von 2 auf 2,50 Euro zu erhöhen."

The alternative adds a vote, amounts, and effective date that must be established. Include the year where needed. It is not a permissible paraphrase of a meeting whose result is unknown.

A feature may open with a scene or individual experience. Supply a timely explanation of the broader subject and stakes. Delayed explanation should create purposeful interest rather than conceal a weak premise.

#### A coherent line of development

Give each section one main contribution. Develop the line through evidence, examples, implications, and relevant objections. Avoid a symmetrical outline that the material does not support.

Keep the author's position recognizable. A distinctive voice can be quiet or argumentative without becoming a generic sequence of commentary formulas.

A sustained thought may need a longer sentence or paragraph. Expansion should add explanation or experience rather than another paraphrase of the thesis. Use transitions according to the actual relationship, not a translated list of English essay connectors.

#### Credibility and proportion

Support factual claims with available evidence. Distinguish reported cases, illustrative examples, opinion, and interpretation. Do not invent customer experience, credentials, results, conversations, or personal vulnerability.

Attribute allegations and disputed accounts clearly. Separate what records establish, what a source says, what the subject disputes, and what remains unanswered. German reported-speech forms can maintain source distance according to the general evidence conventions.

Choose attribution verbs accurately. "Sagte", "erklärte", "bestritt", "räumte ein", and "behauptete" express different relationships to the claim. Avoid a skeptical or conceding verb merely to vary a neutral "sagte".

Represent an available response substantively. Do not imply that someone refused to comment unless that occurred, or treat silence as evidence of guilt.

#### Data reporting with context and human consequences

Lead with the finding that answers the reporting question. Give meaningful historical, geographic, or other comparisons to explain scale. Use comparable definitions and periods.

Explain whether an apparent change concerns the underlying phenomenon or its recording. Administrative records measure what their collection system captures, not necessarily every instance of the behavior.

Connect patterns to affected people where evidence supports the connection. Individual accounts illustrate experience without establishing prevalence. Make their selection and role intelligible, particularly when an unusual case supplies the opening.

Keep data and testimony distinguishable. Expert interpretation can explain a result without replacing the finding or eliminating uncertainty. A discrepancy between records and accounts may remain part of the story.

Implications should follow from established findings. Distinguish trends from forecasts and possible actions from demonstrated remedies. Striking numbers do not justify an inevitable future.

#### Public explanations of methodology

A methodology passage should explain how the finding was produced and where its limits lie. Identify the publisher and source, relevant release or access date, coverage period, population, geographic scope, and consequential definitions.

Explain important exclusions, missing records, transformations, adjustments, and comparison choices in plain language. Do not imply that a reported-incident count is a complete incident count.

Describe material sensitivity to definitions or exclusions and known limitations of coverage or accuracy. State what verification occurred without vague assurances. Explain access to data, code, or supporting material and actual restrictions.

A concise passage may direct readers to fuller detail, but must not hide a limitation that changes the central finding. Methodological transparency in the article is a reporting function, not a catalog of internal operations.

#### Captions, chart text, and accessible descriptions

A caption should identify the relevant person, place, event, or evidence and necessary context. Distinguish when an image was made from when the event occurred. Identify archival, illustrative, or reconstructed material when readers might otherwise mistake it for a record of the event.

Chart titles, labels, annotations, and notes need the quantity, units, period, population, uncertainty, and source information relevant to interpretation. Distinguish counts from rates and adjusted from unadjusted values.

Do not let a chart title assert a cause the display cannot establish. Keep decimal presentation, units, and comparison directions consistent between chart and prose.

Alternative text should convey the image's relevant information or function in context. For charts, give the important relationship and essential uncertainty rather than only "Diagramm der Ergebnisse". Supply a fuller description or accessible data table when a short description cannot carry the necessary detail.

Avoid redundant visual inventories, unsupported interpretation, and captions that quietly strengthen the article's claim. Give required credits accurately.

#### Corrections and substantive updates

A correction identifies the material error and states the corrected information plainly. Explain a resulting change to the central finding. Do not disguise an error with "präzisiert" or "aktualisiert".

Distinguish a correction from new information about an evolving event. Supply enough timing and context for readers to understand what changed, without repeating a misleading or harmful detail beyond what the correction requires.

#### Characteristic formulas to remove

Remove empty openers such as "In einer Welt, die sich ständig verändert", automatic "Gamechanger" claims, promotional "bahnbrechend" judgments, and bridges such as "Doch warum ist das wichtig?" when the text could state the consequence directly.

Avoid fake vulnerability arcs, irrelevant biography, staged revelations, and closing questions added solely to solicit engagement. Replacing a formula with a smoother German formula preserves its lack of substance.

Keep a genuine question or personal experience where it advances the piece. The issue is fabricated connection or unearned importance, not the existence of a first-person voice.

#### Guides, essays, opinion, and newsletters

| Form | Writing requirements |
| --- | --- |
| Technical guide | Open with the practical result. Use relevant commands, code, screenshots, and concrete output where available. End with a useful consequence. |
| Essay | Develop one connected inquiry or argument from a specific observation, tension, or real contradiction. Let the progression earn its conclusion. |
| Opinion | State the position and answer to evidence. Represent serious alternatives fairly instead of inventing weak opponents. |
| Newsletter | Make the opening screen useful. Keep diary material when it serves the piece and section labels when they support navigation. |

An article can use narrative techniques, but reported anecdotes must remain factual. Literary devices do not authorize invented reporting.

#### Worked example

> Before: "In unserer dynamischen Arbeitswelt ist unser revolutionäres Werkzeug ein echter Gamechanger für vielbeschäftigte Teams. Warum das wichtig ist? Ganz einfach!"
>
> Alternative for an illustrative product with the stated behavior: "Das Werkzeug speichert jeden Entwurf automatisch. Schlägt das Absenden fehl, bleibt die Arbeit des Teams erhalten."

The alternative replaces promotion with a concrete capability and consequence. Both must be true of the product. If they are unknown, no amount of stylistic improvement can supply them.

### Storytelling

#### Particular experience rather than generic atmosphere

Make the scene belong to these characters, in this world, under these conditions. A viewpoint character notices according to attention, knowledge, desire, fear, experience, and immediate purpose.

An inventory of everything a camera could record often contributes less than a detail revealing what matters to the character. The detail can be visual, auditory, tactile, olfactory, or conceptual. Do not add smells, bodily sensations, or unusual defects to every scene merely to make it vivid.

> Generic: "Das Zimmer war wunderschön und voller Erinnerungen."
>
> Illustrative scene: "Jemand hatte um das helle Rechteck herum poliert, an dem früher das Foto ihres Vaters gestanden hatte."

The alternative invents a particular situation. It illustrates fictional specificity rather than providing a faithful paraphrase. Use detail that belongs to the authorized story, not arbitrary novelty.

#### Voice, viewpoint, and distance

Keep the narrator's register and perspective intelligible. Changes of viewpoint or narrative distance should have a purpose and enough orientation for the reader.

Distinguish the narrator's knowledge from the character's. Do not give a character information they have not acquired. In close viewpoint, filter descriptions and comparisons through what the character could notice, know, and understand.

Vocabulary and syntax can reflect occupation, education, relationships, age, and emotional state. Avoid turning those influences into caricatures or using grammatical deviation as shorthand for intelligence.

Free indirect discourse can let character judgment enter third-person narration without an explicit thought tag.

> "Sie schob den Brief zurück. Sollte sie ihm etwa schon wieder glauben?"

The question can belong to the character's perspective without quotation marks. Keep the perspective clear enough that the reader does not mistake a character's prejudice or uncertainty for an established narrator's fact.

Do not imitate English interior narration by preserving its syntax or sprinkling German particles indiscriminately. Let the German voice establish its own distance and rhythm.

#### Make narrative elements work together

Meaning, character, plot, disclosure, world, relationships, and prose affect one another.

| Concern | Writing implication |
| --- | --- |
| Meaning | Let choices, consequences, relationships, and contrasts carry the thematic question. |
| Character change | Make desires, beliefs, pressures, decisions, and effects intelligible where an arc depends on them. |
| Plot | Establish consequential relationships among events rather than a list of incidents. |
| Mystery | Shape what readers know, suspect, misunderstand, and later reinterpret. |
| World | Make physical and social conditions constrain or enable action. |
| Relationships | Give bonds, status, obligations, and conflict particular stakes. |
| Prose | Make movement, detail, voice, and rhetorical choices serve the moment. |

A setting detail can reveal a rule and pressure a relationship. A line of dialogue can change an alliance and disclose a motive. Let these effects arise from the situation rather than assigning every sentence an equal load.

A flaw, crisis, and transformation can structure an arc. Stasis, refusal, tragic failure, ensemble change, and developing perception are also available forms. Do not impose the same corrective lesson on every character.

#### Causality, cost, and consequence

Make choices matter where the story depends on agency. Establish what makes an action possible, its immediate effect, and the consequences that alter later choices.

Coherence does not require a single-track plot or a protagonist who controls every outcome. Chance, institutions, other people, and external events may matter. Avoid arbitrary convenience and artificial tidiness.

Keep world rules consistent. A power, technology, or institution should not acquire an unprepared capability solely to solve the current problem. Reader understanding of a capability should support its credibility in resolving conflict.

Limits can create more interesting choices than additional powers. Develop established possibilities before adding new ones. A consequential disadvantage should affect what a character can do, not disappear when the scene needs a victory.

Maintain physical details, geography, travel time, seasons, relationships, and knowledge. Changed behavior should have an intelligible basis where the narrative depends on it. Continuity is part of prose meaning, not an optional background concern.

#### Information, suspense, and revelation

Give enough orientation to understand the present scene while withholding information that creates a purposeful question. Productive uncertainty differs from confusion about who acts, where the scene occurs, or what physically happened.

Mystery can concern events, motives, relationships, world rules, or future outcomes. Plant consequential details fairly. A revelation should change understanding where the story promises that effect.

Flashbacks, flash-forwards, delayed disclosure, and multiple timelines are available tools. Use them when their arrangement improves experience or interpretation. Temporal complexity is not a requirement, and linearity is not a defect.

An ending may resolve, partly resolve, or leave questions open. A continuing chapter can create forward interest without an automatic cliffhanger. A completed story may close its questions without resolving every relationship too neatly.

Let consequences carry thematic meaning. An outcome may retain ambiguity, acceptance, action, or unresolved cost. Do not append a universal lesson to explain what the scene has already made available.

#### Emotion, behavior, and subtext

Use emotion labels, action, silence, dialogue, bodily sensation, or thought according to the moment. "Sie hatte Angst" may be sufficient. Repeated tight chests, caught breaths, cold blood, and trembling hands can be as formulaic as an abstract label.

Possible fictional treatments include:

| General statement | Particular fictional action |
| --- | --- |
| "Sie wurde wütend." | "Erst als sie die Hand öffnete, bemerkte sie den Abdruck ihres Daumennagels in der Haut." |
| "Er hatte große Angst." | "Seine Hand zitterte so stark, dass er den Schlüssel nicht ins Schloss bekam." |
| "Der Abschied machte sie traurig." | "Sie rückte die Kissen noch einmal zurecht und schloss die Tür, ohne sich umzusehen." |

Each alternative adds behavior and implications that must belong to the character and scene. None is an exact translation of every instance of the emotion. The first adds self-awareness and bodily pressure; the last can suggest reluctance without establishing its only cause.

Avoid explaining subtext immediately after readers can infer it. Characters can evade, conceal, misunderstand, or speak plainly. Not every emotional exchange needs a hidden second meaning.

#### Dialogue with identifiable speakers

Distinguish speakers through vocabulary, syntax, rhythm, attention, and intention. A line should belong to a particular person in a particular relationship rather than to a generic witty narrator.

Dialogue can advance action, reveal character, establish a world, create humor, or express disagreement. Avoid making every exchange a philosophical debate, a miniature speech, or an explicit statement of theme.

Use ordinary speech tags such as "sagte" and "fragte" where identification is their purpose. Replacing every occurrence with "erwiderte", "hauchte", "zischte", or an adverb creates noise and may change the action. A relevant action beat can reveal hesitation or intention.

Keep the speaker intelligible. Distinct voices help, but every line need not be identifiable without context or attribution. Avoid an action beat after every utterance solely to avoid a repeated tag.

Address and particles carry relationship. A shift from "Sie" to "du" can be consequential. A "doch", "mal", or "denn" should fit the speaker's intention and clause, not serve as a generic marker of native speech.

Use omissions, contractions, and dialect where they belong to the character. Edited dialogue need not reproduce every hesitation of an actual recording. Technical exactness, formal speech, and deliberate restraint can also be credible voices.

#### Pacing through syntax and proportion

Short clauses can create urgency. A longer German sentence can sustain movement, thought, or pressure. Neither length automatically determines pace.

Give emotional consequences enough room. Keep transitions proportionate to their importance. Remove repeated action, redundant exchanges, and description that delays the scene without changing its effect.

Prepare emotional peaks through relevant desire, risk, relationships, and earlier experience. Intense wording cannot supply that preparation. Keep an effective action or image without immediately interpreting it.

Paragraph and section breaks shape attention, pauses, speaker changes, time, and viewpoint. Let variation follow the scene rather than a fixed alternation of long and short units.

#### Narrative tense and represented speech

Choose a coherent narrative time and perspective. Präteritum is available for past narration, present tense for an immediate narrative frame, and Perfekt for appropriate conversational accounts. None guarantees literary quality or authenticity.

Use tense shifts for actual changes of time, frame, or voice. Dialogue can use a conversational tense different from the surrounding narration. A remembered earlier event may require Plusquamperfekt for orientation before the account settles into a clearly established past frame.

Indirect speech and thought need not all use the same mood. A narrator's report, a character's hypothetical thought, and free indirect discourse have different functions. Preserve who knows, believes, doubts, or imagines what.

Do not make an entire passage depend on English tense correspondences. Establish the German narrative frame and keep temporal relationships intelligible within it.

#### Anti-cliché openings

| Stock opening | More particular treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "In einer Welt, in der ..." | Begin with a character's particular action, constraint, or predicament. |
| "Es war eine dunkle und stürmische Nacht." | Establish what the weather changes or what the viewpoint character notices. |
| "Noch ahnte sie nicht ..." | Create dramatic irony through a meaningful difference in knowledge. |
| "Es war einmal ..." | Keep the convention where the tale requires it; otherwise begin with the relevant event or detail. |

A familiar form can be intentional. Do not replace a fairy-tale opening with artificial novelty when the form itself supplies the desired voice.

#### Anti-cliché emotion

| Stock wording | More particular treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "Ein Schauer lief ihr über den Rücken." | Name a relevant response or consequential action. |
| "Ihr Herz setzte einen Schlag aus." | Describe what she does or notices in response. |
| "Tränen strömten über ihr Gesicht." | Use tears when they matter; otherwise find the particular expression of grief or resistance. |
| "Eine Welle der Gefühle überrollte sie." | Name the feeling or a specific action. |
| "Ihm gefror das Blut in den Adern." | Establish the fear and its particular effect. |
| "Sie hatte Schmetterlinge im Bauch." | Use a relevant nervous thought, behavior, or perception. |
| "Die Zeit stand still." | Describe narrowed attention or the altered experience of duration. |
| "Für ihn brach eine Welt zusammen." | Establish the realization and its first consequence. |

These are available alternatives, not requirements to physicalize every emotion. A particular bodily response can become repetitive too. Do not replace every familiar idiom in credible dialogue with conspicuously original description.

#### Anti-cliché description

| Stock wording | More particular treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "durchdringende blaue Augen" | Choose a detail that matters to the viewpoint character. |
| "rabenschwarzes Haar" | Describe relevant appearance, movement, or action without automatic beauty formulas. |
| "markante Gesichtszüge" | Give a distinguishing feature where appearance matters. |
| "eine wunderschöne Frau" | Name a particular perception or effect, or keep the direct judgment if that is all the scene needs. |
| "Die Sonne brannte erbarmungslos." | Describe what the heat changes in this setting. |
| "eine unheimliche Stille" | Identify the missing or remaining sound and the reason for unease. |
| "Der Raum versank in Dunkelheit." | Clarify what changed and what remains perceptible. |

Do not invent a scar, asymmetry, smell, or physical defect merely to distinguish someone. Specificity follows the fictional situation.

#### Anti-cliché action

| Stock wording | More particular treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "Die Hölle brach los." | Show the first failure or action and what follows. |
| "Sie kämpften um ihr Leben." | Describe a consequential action, error, technique, or desperate choice. |
| "blitzschnell" | Give the motion and its effect without automatic intensification. |
| "allen Widrigkeiten zum Trotz" | Establish the actual disadvantage. |
| "in letzter Sekunde" | Show the timing and consequence of almost being too late. |

Keep concise summary when the action itself is not the scene's focus. The goal is meaningful specificity, not exhaustive choreography.

#### Anti-cliché dialogue

| Stock wording | More particular treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "Wir müssen reden." | Let the speaker raise the particular matter, unless avoidance is the point. |
| "Du verstehst das nicht." | Make the actual misunderstanding intelligible. |
| "Es ist nicht so, wie es aussieht." | Give the speaker's particular explanation, evasion, or lie. |
| "Ich habe ein ganz schlechtes Gefühl dabei." | Identify the observation or conflict causing concern. |
| "Das lief ja großartig." | Give a response particular to what failed where generic sarcasm adds nothing. |

A familiar line can fit a particular interaction or a required quotation. Familiarity alone does not justify making every speaker unusually inventive.

#### Recurrent generic imagery

| Formula | More direct or particular treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "ein Teppich aus ..." | Name the actual pattern, elements, or relationship. |
| "die Last der Erinnerungen" | Establish the particular burden, recollection, or action. |
| "durch die Wirren des Lebens navigieren" | Show the difficult choice and constraints. |
| "im Gewebe des Schicksals" | State the event or remove the generic commentary. |
| "ein Vorbote von ..." | Identify the sign and what it reasonably predicts. |
| "eine Kakofonie aus ..." | Identify relevant sounds. |
| "eine Symphonie der Gefühle" | Name the particular feelings or interactions. |
| "der Tanz der Schatten" | Describe the movement and its significance to the observer. |
| "geflüsterte Versprechen" | State what was said, implied, or hoped for. |
| "ein Echo vergangener Zeiten" | Name the memory, reference, or actual recurring sound. |
| "das Gefüge der Wirklichkeit" | Describe the fictional world's particular change. |
| "Und so kam es, dass ..." | Begin with the action or observation unless the narrator's form requires the formula. |

Literal meanings remain available. An actual echo, dance, whisper, woven object, or musical performance can belong to the scene. The defect is reusable elevation detached from this situation.

#### Genre conventions without automatic plots

| Genre | Familiar shortcuts requiring particular causes and consequences |
| --- | --- |
| Fantasy | An orphaned farm child automatically chosen to save the world; conveniently exact prophecy; evil without motive; uniformly wise elves and gruff dwarves; magic without cost or limit. |
| Science fiction | Immediate hostility as the default response to AI sentience; an unexamined last-person premise; faster-than-light travel without social consequences; cosmetically different aliens; technology that fails only when convenient. |
| Mystery | Surprise based solely on the least suspected person; alcoholism as ready-made detective depth; a victim's secret life substituting for development; a conveniently discovered clue solving everything; a villain explaining the entire plan. |
| Romance | Hostility changing to love without development; body description replacing an encounter; decisive misunderstanding sustained by implausible silence; an obvious triangle choice; a rain kiss as an automatic climax. |

A convention can work when the story gives it particular meaning. Novelty does not repair weak causality, and familiarity does not invalidate a premise. Do not add moral ambiguity, nonlinear structure, or tragedy solely to oppose a recognizable convention.

#### Choose comparisons from the viewpoint

Draw images from the character's world and experience. A merchant may understand a threat through debt and payment; a courtier may think in reputation and rumor. The image should reveal viewpoint as well as describe action.

Avoid comparisons attachable to any scene. Surprise earns its place through what it reveals, not unfamiliar vocabulary alone. An English comparison that depends on a specific idiom or sound pattern may need another German expression to achieve the same function.

Cross-sensory imagery can express an unusual perception, such as a sound experienced through texture. A strained comparison can represent an extreme state of mind. Keep it intelligible within the scene rather than combining sensory domains to sound poetic.

#### Literary techniques

Choose a technique by the effect the passage needs. A classical term does not excuse decorative complexity, forced symmetry, or invented logic. Sound and wordplay must work in German; the source's pattern is not automatically reproducible through literal translation.

| Purpose | Available techniques | Useful application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Connect sound | Alliteration repeats initial sounds; assonance repeats vowel sounds; consonance repeats consonants. | Establish a restrained relationship among consequential words. |
| Shape atmosphere through sound | Sibilance emphasizes sibilants; cacophony uses harsh combinations; euphony uses smoother ones. | Support a perception without making every line a conspicuous sound exercise. |
| Place a sound in the scene | Onomatopoeia uses sound-imitating words, such as "summen", "knacken", or "klirren". | Make a consequential sound perceptible without replacing the event with effects. |
| Create a rhyme-like cadence | Internal rhyme and similar word endings connect nearby phrases. | Support ritual, comedy, or musical speech without making ordinary narration jingle. |
| Establish a refrain | Anaphora repeats openings; epistrophe repeats endings; symploce combines them. | Develop a sustained concern or insistence rather than mechanical rhythm. |
| Carry an idea forward | Anadiplosis repeats an ending at the next opening; epanalepsis returns to an opening phrase. | Make continuity or changed implication perceptible. |
| Express insistence | Epizeuxis repeats immediately; diacope separates repeated words. | Convey a particular emotional pressure or demand. |
| Characterize through redundancy | Deliberate restatement can express evasion, resignation, obsession, or ceremony. | Reveal the speaker rather than merely repeat information. |
| Develop a word's meaning | Polyptoton varies grammatical form; ploce and antanaclasis repeat with changed senses. | Expose an ambiguity or evolving understanding. |
| Articulate contrast | Antithesis balances opposing ideas; paradox and oxymoron express apparent contradiction. | Represent a real tension rather than invent an opposition. |
| Reverse a relationship | Chiasmus reverses structures or ideas; antimetabole reverses repeated words. | Give the reversal a consequence for the thought or exchange. |
| Create balance | Isocolon uses parallel units; a tricolon uses three. | Give a justified distinction rhythm without forcing the item count. |
| Control emphasis | A periodic sentence delays the main assertion; a loose sentence supplies it early. | Match unfolding to expectation, thought, or action within German syntax. |
| Control clause movement | Parataxis places independent clauses side by side; hypotaxis subordinates them. | Express sequence, dependency, or layered thought. |
| Control accumulation | Asyndeton omits conjunctions; polysyndeton repeats them. | Create urgency, persistence, or exhaustion where it belongs. |
| Build rhetorical intensity | Arrange phrases in increasing importance or force. | Let a declaration or realization develop rather than start every clause at maximum intensity. |
| Represent interruption | Aposiopesis breaks off an utterance; anacoluthon changes its grammatical course. | Express an actual interruption or disrupted thought through an appropriate construction. |
| Compress recoverable information | Ellipsis omits understood words; apposition identifies a noun through an adjacent phrase. | Retain what the intended reader can recover without making explanatory prose telegraphic. |
| Sustain an image | Simile compares explicitly; metaphor compares implicitly; an extended metaphor develops the image. | Express a perception particular to the character or scene. |
| Explain through comparison | Analogy relates domains; allegory extends representation across a narrative. | Develop a relationship while preserving the comparison's limits. |
| Transfer a perception | Personification gives human qualities to something nonhuman; a transferred epithet shifts a modifier to an associated object. | Reveal viewpoint without confusing perception with literal intention. |
| Name by association | Synecdoche uses part and whole; metonymy uses association; merism evokes a whole through parts or extremes. | Use cultural shorthand the reader can follow. |
| Layer knowledge | Dramatic irony gives readers knowledge a character lacks; foreshadowing prepares a later event. | Create fair suspense or reinterpretation. |
| Reorder time | Flashback reveals an earlier event; flash-forward changes anticipation. | Make chronology serve disclosure without losing orientation. |
| Enter an event directly | In medias res begins amid consequential action. | Establish involvement, then supply necessary context. |
| Create irony | Verbal irony separates intended and literal meaning; situational irony reverses an established expectation. | Reveal a discrepancy or meaningful relationship, including through an opposite-meaning nickname. |
| Vary emotional scale | Hyperbole exaggerates; litotes and meiosis understate; bathos moves from the important to the trivial. | Express a particular attitude, restraint, or comic reversal. |
| Represent address and desire | Apostrophe addresses an absent person or abstraction; rhetorical questions express a stance. | Give appeals, wishes, and warnings a credible speaker and specific stakes. |
| Reveal avoidance | Euphemism softens; dysphemism makes harsher; indirect naming can signal taboo. | Characterize a relationship to the subject without concealing facts in exposition. |
| Draw attention through omission | A speaker mentions something while professing not to discuss it. | Expose evasion or manipulation without making the narration habitually indirect. |
| Name through social perception | Epithets, titles, and culturally meaningful names can stand for a person or quality. | Show a community's judgment while keeping identity clear. |
| Develop an allusion | References to a work, myth, event, or chain of associations add another layer. | Give enough context for the relevant connection; obscurity alone adds no depth. |
| Use wordplay | Puns exploit sound or meaning; zeugma and syllepsis give one governing word different senses or applications. | Create relevant wit in German rather than preserve an unworkable source-language pun. |
| Build a fictional vocabulary | Neologisms coin terms; portmanteaus blend words; kennings create compressed metaphorical names. | Name distinctions the fictional culture needs. |
| Shape character diction | Category shifts, paired nouns expressing one idea, or a word inserted inside another can distinguish an utterance. | Let unusual formation belong to the speaker and remain intelligible. |
| Represent a verbal mistake | Malapropisms substitute similar-sounding words; spoonerisms transpose sounds. | Characterize a moment without stereotyping a group. |
| Focus description | Description can develop an artwork, place, era, season, appearance, or behavior. | Give consequential attention; an extended inventory can express fixation when it belongs to the viewpoint. |
| Accumulate detail | Enumeratio lists parts; amplification develops them; distributio assigns roles or consequences. | Establish scope without making every passage an inventory. |
| Reveal thought in motion | Aporia expresses genuine doubt; self-correction revises a formulation. | Make uncertainty or a changed judgment intelligible. |
| Reveal a reasoning strategy | Anticipated objections, eliminated alternatives, and contradictions derived from premises expose reasoning. | Supply a basis; an unsupported slippery-slope prediction is not a proof. |
| Constrain a choice | Alternatives may carry different costs or dangers. | Establish real constraints without inventing a binary or hiding a plausible option. |
| Expose an unstated assumption | A conclusion can depend on a premise the character assumes is shared. | Reveal worldview or unreliable judgment; factual argument still needs defensible premises. |
| Represent imagined speech | A character rehearses a conversation or imagines a response. | Reveal fear, anticipation, or judgment while distinguishing imagination from an actual exchange. |
| Express a worldview concisely | A maxim or pointed observation states a particular understanding. | Fit the speaker and moment without delivering an automatic lesson. |
| Return to an earlier idea | A closing image, phrase, or observation recalls prior material. | Earn closure through changed meaning rather than an appended moral. |

Sound, repetition, and syntax may support the same moment. Avoid stacking devices until construction overwhelms experience. Quiet, connective, and deliberately ordinary passages can sustain the larger work.

#### Necessary literary exceptions

Metaphor, repetition, fragments, unusual order, nonstandard grammar, direct address, questions, and exclamations can express a character or narrative effect. Their justification is a concrete function such as disrupted speech, pressure, cultural language, or viewpoint.

A proverb, curse, or strategic compliment should belong to a speaker and situation. Do not replace all ordinary conversation with conspicuous originality.

Keep dialect coherent and respectful. Deliberate deviations in speech do not require errors in narration. A literary exception does not authorize generic bodily metaphors, philosophical speeches, nonlinear scenes, or rhetorical flourishes everywhere.

Represent an interruption with a suitable construction, an action, or a break appropriate to the passage, without adding sentence dashes as decoration. Exact quoted documents and required embedded notation retain their own punctuation.

#### Worked example

> Before: "Eine Welle der Trauer überrollte sie, als sie erkannte, dass wahrer Mut manchmal bedeutet, loszulassen. Ihr Herz schmerzte unter der Last der Erinnerungen."
>
> Illustrative scene: "Sie legte seinen Ersatzschlüssel in den Umschlag. Eine Weile ließ sie die Hand darauf liegen."

The alternative invents a particular action and leaves its emotional implication available. It is not a paraphrase of every kind of grief. A direct emotion label may fit another moment better. Preserve whichever treatment serves this character and scene without explaining an already intelligible implication.
