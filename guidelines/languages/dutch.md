# Dutch Prose Writing – Best Practices

Natural Dutch makes the intended meaning available without unnecessary effort. Vocabulary, grammar, sentence movement, and organization fit the subject, reader, situation, and form. It can be formal, conversational, concise, analytical, or literary. Native phrasing does not require bluntness, forced informality, bureaucratic distance, or unusually short sentences.

Preserve meaning, facts, necessary terminology, and the integrity of quotations. Apply the general conventions throughout. The style sections add requirements and narrowly necessary exceptions for academic, technical, absolute, article, and storytelling prose.

Express relationships in Dutch rather than reproducing English words and sentence shapes. Similarities with German do not make German grammar a template for Dutch. Dutch has its own patterns for reference, emphasis, verb groups, modality, and social distance.

Presentation follows Dutch conventions with a restrained house style. Straight quotation marks and the exclusion of sentence dashes are house preferences. A word, construction, or punctuation mark does not by itself establish who wrote a passage.

Examples illustrate writing choices. They do not report actual findings, events, product behavior, or measurements. An alternative that invents an action demonstrates one possible fictional scene, not an interchangeable paraphrase.

## Meaning, reader, and register

### Preserve the proposition

Keep what a sentence says, whom or what it concerns, and how strongly it says it. A smoother revision must preserve conditions, exclusions, quantities, causality, chronology, attribution, and uncertainty. Shortening helps only when the relevant meaning survives.

| Original | Meaning-changing revision | Faithful revision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "De behandeling kan bij sommige patiënten de klachten verminderen." | "De behandeling vermindert de klachten." | "Bij sommige patiënten kunnen de klachten door de behandeling afnemen." |
| "Alleen beheerders mogen gearchiveerde gegevens verwijderen." | "Beheerders mogen gegevens verwijderen." | "Gearchiveerde gegevens mogen uitsluitend door beheerders worden verwijderd." |
| "Het team meldde na de wijziging minder fouten." | "De wijziging voorkwam fouten." | "Na de wijziging meldde het team minder fouten." |
| "Hij was moe en wilde niet praten." | "Uitgeput verkoos hij de stilte." | "Hij was moe. Praten wilde hij niet." |
| "Deze verklaring zou kunnen gelden voor honden die met andere honden samenleven." | "Het gedrag komt vaak voor bij dieren die met andere huisdieren leven." | "Voor honden die met andere honden samenleven, zou deze verklaring kunnen gelden." |

Do not strengthen tiredness into exhaustion, permission into capability, a possible account into observed prevalence, or sequence into causation. A vivid word can change the facts as easily as an omitted qualification.

Preserve deliberate ambiguity where it serves a literary work. Remove accidental ambiguity from explanations, instructions, and factual claims. A character's uncertain motive differs from an instruction whose object cannot be identified.

### Write for the reader's actual knowledge

Establish unfamiliar concepts before relying on them. Explain a necessary term near its first relevant use. Omit elementary background the intended audience can reasonably be expected to know.

> Opaque for a general reader: "Idempotentie garandeert robuuste herhalingspogingen."
>
> Clearer: "Als dezelfde aanvraag opnieuw wordt verstuurd, ontstaat er geen tweede bestelling."

Retain the term when useful: "De aanvraag is idempotent: opnieuw versturen maakt geen tweede bestelling aan." This explains one established behavior. Do not turn it into a guarantee that every retry succeeds.

Use concrete examples to introduce difficult ideas, then connect them to the general point where the connection needs explaining. Neither specialist vocabulary alone nor excessive elementary explanation serves every reader.

Give unfamiliar abbreviations their full form at first use, followed by the abbreviation when it will recur. In a short passage, repeating the full term can be clearer. Familiarity depends on the audience, not the writer's profession.

An abstract, caption, table note, or isolated interface message needs enough context to stand alone. Define an unfamiliar abbreviation separately where readers may encounter either element without the other.

### Match register to the form

Use precise formal language for scholarly claims, literal language for procedures, and character-appropriate diction for fiction. Natural Dutch does not require jokes, rhetorical questions, personal anecdotes, slang, or an intimate opening.

Written standard Dutch can be direct and approachable without copying speech word for word. Reductions such as "'k", "'t", and "da's" carry register choices. Use them where the voice warrants them rather than distributing them as signs of spontaneity.

Familiar words are compatible with formality. Repeated "hierbij", "inzake", "dient te", and "ten aanzien van" do not make a claim more precise. Conversely, an established legal or technical expression may carry a distinction an informal paraphrase loses.

Keep the degree of formality appropriate to the whole passage. A friendly salutation followed by an impersonal administrative demand can create an inconsistent relationship.

### Keep address and emphasis coherent

Use the established relationship and medium to choose "je" or "jij" for informal singular address, "jullie" for informal plural address, and "u" for formal address to one or more people. Keep verbs and possessives consistent with the choice.

"Je" is normally the unstressed form; "jij" adds emphasis or contrast. The difference is meaningful.

> Neutral address: "Je kunt je aanvraag later aanvullen."
>
> Contrastive address: "Jij kunt de aanvraag aanpassen; je collega kan die alleen bekijken."
>
> Formal address: "U kunt uw aanvraag later aanvullen."
>
> Informal plural address: "Jullie kunnen de aanvragen later aanvullen."

The contrastive example requires different permissions for the two people. Do not add that distinction merely to justify emphatic pronouns.

Avoid repeating "jij", "jou", and "jouw" where unstressed "je" is natural. Constant emphasis can sound insistent or promotional. Distinguish object "jou" from possessive "jouw", and object "u" from possessive "uw": "Dit bericht is voor jou"; "Dit is jouw bericht".

Use "u" and "uw" lowercase except where sentence position or a specific religious convention requires a capital. German courtesy capitalization does not transfer.

Direct reader address should remain coherent, but generic "je" can mean people in general. Its presence inside a formally addressed text is not automatically a switch to informal personal address. Make that distinction clear enough to avoid confusion.

Formal address can be warm, and informal address can be condescending. Choose respectful wording for the actual burden and stakes. A discretionary request may need a reason; an ordinary instruction may need only a clear action.

### Use the intended variety of Dutch

Maintain a coherent standard variety appropriate to the intended audience. Netherlands Dutch and Belgian Dutch share most of their system, while legitimate vocabulary, constructions, and usage preferences differ. Do not treat all Belgian forms as dialect or all Netherlands forms as neutral throughout the language area.

Use a regionally established word when it names the right thing for those readers. "Lidkaart" and "lidmaatschapskaart", for example, can belong to different regional preferences. Explain unfamiliar institutions rather than substituting a supposedly equivalent institution from another country.

For a broad audience, prefer widely understood standard expressions when available. That is an accessibility choice, not a judgment that other standard varieties are defective. Recognize other established Dutch-language contexts where relevant rather than assuming the Netherlands and Belgium exhaust every use of Dutch.

Keep standard-language variation distinct from dialect, conversational regional speech, and a deliberately local character voice. Forms with "ge" or "gij" can serve a particular relationship or voice; they are not generic replacements for every "je" in Belgian-facing prose.

Do not manufacture a regional persona through greetings, diminutives, or stereotypes. Nationality alone does not determine a reader's humor, politeness preferences, or knowledge. Preserve exact names, quotations, and institutional terminology.

### Describe people accurately and respectfully

Use names, pronouns, and descriptions relevant to the subject. Respect stated identity preferences. Do not infer identity from appearance, a name, or group membership.

Mention age, disability, ethnicity, nationality, sex, gender, or another personal characteristic when it contributes necessary information. Avoid stereotypes, demeaning labels, and wording that reduces a person to a diagnosis. Person-first and identity-first language can both be appropriate; follow known preferences without assuming a community is unanimous.

Use the pronouns appropriate to a known individual. Gender-neutral uses of "die", "hen", "hun", or other forms require attention to the person's usage and grammatical context. Do not impose one paradigm universally or transfer singular English pronouns word for word. Repeating a name or recasting the sentence can clarify reference without denying identity.

Distinguish grammatical reference from assumptions about gender. A role name does not establish who occupies it. Neutral role descriptions, plural forms, or direct address can work where they preserve the intended group and responsibility.

Do not copy German inclusive word formations or insert special characters into Dutch nouns as though the languages shared one system. Follow the relevant Dutch context without obscuring who is included.

Distinguish sex from gender when it affects meaning. Report what a study recorded rather than substituting a different category. Preserve self-descriptions and the limits of the data.

> Vague: "Ouderen konden niet met de dienst overweg."
>
> More specific, if supported: "Deelnemers van 70 tot 84 jaar meldden dat ze de kleine letters niet konden lezen."

The alternative requires those ages and reports. Respectful wording does not justify invented precision, euphemism, or stronger conclusions.

### Preserve voice without preserving avoidable confusion

A voice can depend on restraint, expansiveness, dry humor, directness, or the way a thought unfolds. Preserve meaningful characteristics while removing accidental repetition, unsupported claims, obscure phrasing, and faulty grammar.

Multilingual writers can retain distinctive emphasis, experience, and perspective in idiomatic Dutch. Correct unclear constructions without adding colloquialisms, invented local references, or a generic national personality.

Do not introduce mistakes, arbitrary fragments, rare words, random digressions, or artificial irregularity to imitate a person. A polished sentence can be natural; an unpolished one can be formulaic. Deliberate character speech differs from accidental errors in the surrounding narration.

## Evidence, specificity, and uncertainty

### State the concrete claim

Say what happened, what something does, what changes, or what the reader needs to know. Replace atmospheric reassurance with an established mechanism, consequence, action, or number.

> Vague: "De nieuwe werkwijze zorgt voor een naadloze gebruikerservaring."
>
> Concrete, if established: "Als het versturen mislukt, blijven de ingevulde gegevens in het formulier staan."

Specificity comes from the material, not from plausible invention. If the mechanism is unknown, describe the known behavior without supplying an imagined implementation.

### Separate observation, attribution, and interpretation

Distinguish events observed directly, accounts attributed to others, and conclusions about either. Identify whose judgment a statement expresses.

| Kind of statement | Illustrative wording |
| --- | --- |
| Observation | "Drie deelnemers stopten vóór de laatste vraag." |
| Attributed account | "De deelnemers noemden de laatste vraag onduidelijk." |
| Interpretation | "De formulering van de vraag kan aan het afhaken hebben bijgedragen." |

The interpretation needs a defensible relationship to the evidence. Do not turn a reported explanation into an established cause or a person's impression into consensus.

A possible function does not establish frequency. Keep the population and kind of claim intact. An explanation that could fit dogs living together cannot become a general observation about all animals merely to improve fluency.

### Name the source of a claim

Replace borrowed authority such as "experts zijn het erover eens", "onderzoek toont aan", and "critici waarschuwen" with the actual source and relevant claim where available. Otherwise remove the pretended authority or state the uncertainty.

Attach a reference to the proposition it supports. A real source can be misrepresented through an inaccurate paraphrase, wider population, missing condition, or stronger conclusion. Separate the source's finding from the writer's inference.

Distinguish preprints, published studies, datasets, and secondary reporting. Publication status describes the source; it does not establish truth on its own. Prestige and citation counts do not replace relevant evidence.

Keep titles, authors, dates, identifiers, quotations, findings, and locators consistent with the version cited. Do not attach a preprint's finding to a later paper's details when the versions differ. A named reference should locate an idea or evidence, not provide unexplained decoration.

### Attribute reported speech without adding skepticism

Dutch normally reports speech through an attributed clause, with appropriate changes of person, time reference, and tense.

> Direct statement: "De gegevens zijn volledig."
>
> Report: "De onderzoeker zegt dat de gegevens volledig zijn."
>
> Past reporting frame: "De onderzoeker zei dat de gegevens volledig waren."

The past frame can report the original claim without implying that the data have since become incomplete. A present tense may be appropriate for a proposition explicitly treated as continuing. Preserve the temporal relationship rather than backshifting every verb mechanically.

Choose reporting verbs by meaning. "Zei", "verklaarde", "ontkende", "erkende", and "beweerde" are not neutral substitutes for one another. A verb suggesting skepticism or concession needs that function.

Do not import German reported-speech subjunctives. Dutch "zou" can express reported or unconfirmed information, a hypothetical situation, a future viewed from the past, or politeness. Its insertion may change the status of the claim.

> Neutral attribution: "Volgens de directeur is het gebouw veilig."
>
> Potentially distancing report: "Het gebouw zou veilig zijn."

The second version leaves the attribution less explicit and may suggest information not confirmed by the writer. Use it only when that stance belongs to the account. Repeated "zou" is not a substitute for naming who said what.

### Make numerical comparisons interpretable

Identify the population, timeframe, units, denominator, and baseline. State what is counted or measured. A percentage of respondents differs from a percentage of eligible people; a count differs from a rate.

| Distinction | Writing requirement | Illustrative wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Count and rate | Supply relevant population sizes or comparable rates. | "Gebied A telde 40 incidenten bij 10.000 inwoners; gebied B telde er 60 bij 30.000." The rates are 4 and 2 per 1,000 residents. |
| Percentage and percentage points | Distinguish relative change from the difference between two percentages. | "Het aandeel steeg van 20% naar 30%." That is 10 percentage points and a 50% relative increase. |
| Mean and median | Name the summary actually used. | "De mediane wachttijd was twaalf minuten" does not report the arithmetic mean. |
| Money over time | Distinguish nominal amounts from inflation-adjusted values. | "Nominaal namen de uitgaven toe, maar uitgedrukt in prijzen van 2024 daalden ze." Both comparisons require evidence. |

Compare like periods, boundaries, definitions, and populations. Explain a material change in coverage or classification rather than presenting it as a change in the underlying phenomenon. Pair a striking relative change with its starting value or absolute size where scale matters.

The arithmetic mean is the total divided by the number of observations. The median is the middle ordered value, or the midpoint of the two central values for an even number of observations. Extreme values can shift the mean without a comparable median change. Avoid ambiguous "gemiddeld" when the distinction affects interpretation.

Use no more precision than the measurement supports. Preserve the underlying value when adapting number presentation; locale formatting belongs under punctuation and presentation.

### Distinguish magnitude, precision, and importance

Effect size, estimate precision, statistical significance, and practical importance answer different questions. Describe the consequence in relevant units. A small effect can be estimated precisely, while an important possible effect remains uncertain.

Identify an uncertainty interval's type and level where relevant. Interpret it according to the method. A confidence interval is not a range containing a stated proportion of individual observations.

A nonsignificant result does not establish absence or equivalence. State what the evidence can distinguish.

> Overstated for an inconclusive comparison: "Er was geen verschil tussen de groepen."
>
> Bounded, if imprecision explains the result: "De schatting was te onnauwkeurig om vast te stellen of de groepen in praktisch relevante mate verschilden."

Do not substitute the second sentence for every nonsignificant result. It makes its own claim about the evidence. An equivalence claim needs evidence suited to equivalence.

### Keep the level of inference clear

A relationship among schools, regions, or institutions does not establish the same relationship among individuals. Identify the level at which the evidence was collected and the conclusion applies.

Aggregate findings may hide subgroup differences or change when subgroup proportions change. Explain relevant discrepancies rather than choosing whichever level favors the argument. Do not invent subgroup patterns.

Statistical adjustment alone does not establish causality. Sequence, prediction, and a plausible mechanism also need suitable evidence before they become causal explanations. Relevant alternatives can include selection effects, confounding, reverse causation, and regression toward the mean.

Report actual inferential limits without turning every claim into an indiscriminate list of caveats. Keep the alternatives that materially affect interpretation.

### Explain the step from evidence to conclusion

Make the reason evidence supports a claim intelligible. A quotation, number, or citation may still require an explanation of relevance. "Daarom" cannot supply a missing premise.

Represent opposing arguments accurately. Address material counterevidence and plausible alternatives, including relevant differences in population, method, measurement, or setting. Avoid selective reporting, personal attacks, and manufactured balance between positions with unequal support.

Distinguish findings, supported explanations, and speculation. An observation compatible with one account does not uniquely establish it. Name a consequential assumption instead of hiding it in a confident transition.

### Anchor time-sensitive claims

Use a date, period, or version where "momenteel", "onlangs", or "de nieuwste" could become ambiguous. Preserve "voormalig" or relevant dates when an earlier institutional role matters.

Distinguish the date of an event from the publication date of a later account. Do not infer chronology from the order a source presents material, or imply influence from a document that did not yet exist.

> Time-dependent: "De nieuwste versie bevat een uitzondering."
>
> Anchored, if established: "De versie van 2024 voegt een uitzondering toe aan de regel uit 2020."

A date improves specificity only when accurate. Distinguish a proposal, an adopted decision, and its effective date.

### Prefer evidence to evaluative adjectives

"Belangrijk", "indrukwekkend", "krachtig", "effectief", and "significant" need a clear basis. Give the result or consequence where available. Use "statistisch significant" for a defined statistical finding without implying practical importance.

> Unsupported: "De wijziging verbetert de prestaties aanzienlijk."
>
> Illustrative measurement: "In de benchmark daalde de mediane verwerkingstijd van 120 naar 90 milliseconden."

Name comparison conditions that affect the result. Without measurement, describe established behavior rather than suggesting a measured improvement.

### Preserve useful qualifications and modal meanings

Remove stacked uncertainty without removing uncertainty itself. A single "mogelijk" may replace several overlapping hedges; it cannot replace a condition determining truth.

> Padded: "Het zou eventueel mogelijk kunnen zijn dat het effect slechts tijdelijk is."
>
> Direct: "Het effect kan tijdelijk zijn."

Retain "in deze steekproef", "bij lage temperaturen", and other material bounds. Do not replace "kan" with "zal" for confidence or turn a recommendation into a requirement.

Modal verbs can express practical ability, permission, necessity, expectation, or inference. "Hij moet werken" differs from "Hij moet al aangekomen zijn". "U hoeft niet te komen" removes an obligation; "U mag niet komen" prohibits attendance.

Do not assume every "moeten" or "kunnen" has the same function. Negated "moeten" can vary by construction and context; use "niet hoeven" or "niet mogen" when the distinction must be explicit. Preserve the strength and source of a prediction rather than adding "zal" because English uses a future form.

### Make examples carry information

An example should demonstrate a principle, expose a limit, or distinguish similar cases. Explain its connection where needed, without stating the implication twice.

A single incident may illustrate an experience without showing prevalence. Distinguish anecdotes, counterexamples, and evidence for a general conclusion.

Identify hypothetical scenarios in factual writing. In fiction, invented detail belongs to the established world. Plausibility does not authorize invented reporting, measurements, quotations, or mechanisms.

### Give quotations a purpose

Quote when the wording supplies evidence, reveals a perspective, or matters to the argument. Identify the speaker and context. Do not leave a quotation to replace reasoning.

Paraphrase when exact wording adds little. Preserve scope, certainty, and attribution. A close paraphrase that keeps a source's structure while substituting words still depends on that source.

Attribute borrowed ideas as well as wording. Identify indirect citations rather than implying direct access to unconsulted material. Retain required page, section, or paragraph locators.

Distinguish translated quotations from original-language wording where it matters. A fluent Dutch translation may adjust syntax without repairing the speaker's reasoning, adding an explanation, or changing the claim.

## Organization and paragraph development

### Begin with something useful

Open with the claim, situation, observation, event, or genuine question that gives the piece a purpose. Remove greetings, broad declarations about the modern world, and announcements that the text will examine its topic.

> Padded: "In de snel veranderende wereld van vandaag is het belangrijker dan ooit om aandacht te besteden aan back-ups."
>
> Direct: "Een back-up helpt alleen als je de gegevens kunt terugzetten."

An opening can be quiet. It needs relevance rather than manufactured drama. A question should express an uncertainty the text addresses, not insinuate an unsupported allegation.

### Let the subject determine the structure

Organize around purpose and dependencies. Procedures follow action order; arguments develop reasons; references support lookup; narratives arrange experience and disclosure.

Do not force a prefabricated introduction, three equal sections, and a recap. Use the amount of structure the material requires.

Native Dutch does not require a long theoretical preamble or a compulsory sequence of short conversational paragraphs. Nor does every paragraph need an identical topic-sentence, evidence, explanation, and summary pattern. Make the progression intelligible in the appropriate form.

### Give each paragraph a distinct contribution

A paragraph can establish a point, develop it, provide evidence, qualify it, connect ideas, or create a narrative effect. Keep sentences together when they develop one movement.

Break a paragraph where subject, time, speaker, argumentative function, or attention changes meaningfully. Do not separate every sentence for emphasis or preserve an overloaded block merely because its sentences share a broad theme.

Details may establish context, expose a limitation, or complicate an argument. They need not all restate the central message. Repeated explanation can resemble depth while adding nothing. Supply a missing reason, example, consequence, condition, or alternative if needed; otherwise stop.

### Connect ideas through their relationship

Make each sentence's relationship to the preceding material recognizable: consequence, contrast, concession, explanation, sequence, or a new subject. Use a connector when it expresses that relation.

| Relationship | Suitable expressions where the relationship holds |
| --- | --- |
| Reason or cause | "want", "omdat", "doordat" |
| Consequence | "daarom", "daardoor", "zodat" |
| Concession | "hoewel", "toch", "desondanks" |
| Contrast | "maar", "daarentegen", "terwijl" |
| Sequence | "daarna", "vervolgens", "voordat" |

These forms differ in syntax and meaning. A reason, cause, intended purpose, and resulting effect are not interchangeable simply because they connect clauses. Keep a relevant distinction without enforcing unnecessary purist bans on ordinary usage.

> Mechanical: "Daarnaast bewaart de dienst logbestanden. Bovendien registreert de dienst fouten."
>
> Connected, if both describe the same behavior: "De dienst registreert fouten in de logbestanden."

Use simple sequence markers for real sequences. Repeated "en" can blur dependencies, while repeated "vervolgens" can give every event equal weight.

Cohesion also comes from maintaining a topic, stable terminology, and development of an established concept. Not every paragraph needs "bovendien", "echter", or "concluderend". Reproducing every English transition may make Dutch sound overexplained.

### Develop enough, then stop

Concision retains what readers need in less unnecessary language. It does not reduce a substantial thought to a generic judgment.

"Het boek was interessant" may need an explanation of what interested the writer. A paragraph that already demonstrates its point may need no summary. Match development to the reader's task and the cost of misunderstanding.

Do not give every short section a miniature introduction and conclusion. A heading can identify the subject; the first sentence should contribute information.

### Preserve complexity that belongs to the subject

Represent real exceptions, competing motives, uncertainty, and costs. Avoid forcing a binary or universal lesson onto material that does not support one.

Reduce linguistic difficulty while retaining conceptual complexity. Clear prose can explain why a problem remains unresolved. It need not manufacture a solution.

### Avoid manufactured contrasts and false ranges

State the point without staging a rejected alternative nobody proposed. "Het gaat niet om X, maar om Y" and "niet alleen X, maar ook Y" serve real distinctions; used automatically, they create empty emphasis.

> Staged: "Het gaat niet alleen om gegevens. Het gaat om mensen."
>
> Concrete where supported: "Door onjuiste adresgegevens kunnen besluiten de betrokkenen niet bereiken."

The alternative makes a particular claim requiring evidence. It is not a universal paraphrase of the staged contrast.

Use "van X tot Y" for a meaningful range, scale, or progression. "Van samenwerking tot innovatie" does not establish a range when the words are merely two topics. Name the topics and their relationship directly.

### Let substance determine the wording

The strength of a piece depends on what it includes, omits, relates, and concludes. Substituting suspect words cannot repair missing evidence, an empty argument, or an unearned narrative turn.

Do not rename one assertion repeatedly to make it resemble several insights. Avoid invented objections and false alternatives used solely to give a paragraph movement.

### End where the material has earned an ending

An ending may answer the opening question, establish a consequence, name the remaining uncertainty, or identify a justified action. Remove closing sentences that only repeat the introduction or praise the future.

Avoid automatic "Al met al kunnen we concluderen", uplifting predictions, engagement questions, and generic demands for action. Keep a synthesis where it connects findings readers need to hold together, rather than merely announcing completion.

A narrative can end with an image or action without an explanatory moral. Factual prose can end with a bounded conclusion instead of an invented remedy.

## Sentence structure and syntax

### Give the sentence an identifiable subject and action

Keep the relevant actor, action, and object easy to locate. A sentence need not begin with its subject, but readers should recognize its grammatical relationships without reconstruction.

> Dense: "De uitvoering van de aanpassing van de opslaglimiet vindt na beoordeling van de momenteel in het project opgenomen bestanden plaats via het configuratiebestand."
>
> Clearer: "Eerst worden de huidige projectbestanden beoordeeld. Daarna wordt de opslaglimiet in het configuratiebestand aangepast."

The alternative retains the qualification instead of quietly dropping it. If the file sets the limit automatically, state that behavior only when established. Do not invent automation or a human actor to obtain an active sentence.

Avoid introductory noun phrases that postpone the actual assertion. Name responsibility where responsibility matters. A process can legitimately be the topic of a sentence about that process.

### Build clauses around Dutch verb placement

In an ordinary declarative main clause, the finite verb normally follows the first constituent. A constituent may contain several words.

> "Het team beoordeelt de aanvraag morgen."
>
> "Morgen beoordeelt het team de aanvraag."
>
> "Na de volgende vergadering beoordeelt het team de aanvraag."

An English-shaped order such as "Na de vergadering het team beoordeelt de aanvraag" is not standard Dutch declarative order. A preceding subordinate clause normally fills the first position: "Als de vergadering afgelopen is, beoordeelt het team de aanvraag."

Yes-or-no questions normally begin with the finite verb: "Beoordeelt het team de aanvraag?" In introduced subordinate clauses, the verb or verb group occurs later: "omdat het team de aanvraag beoordeelt".

Do not treat the finite verb as invariably the last word of a subordinate clause. Dutch verb groups have their own ordering possibilities, and suitable material can follow the group.

### Keep the sentence bracket manageable

Dutch can separate the finite verb from a participle, infinitive, or separable verb component.

> "Ze heeft de aanvraag gisteren ingediend."
>
> "Ze dient de aanvraag morgen in."
>
> "Ze moet de aanvraag morgen indienen."

This bracket is normal grammar. Do not force its components together to imitate English. Instead, reduce intervening material that makes readers lose track of the construction.

> Overloaded: "Ze heeft de aanvraag na verschillende vragen over gegevens die in het oorspronkelijke formulier ontbraken gisteren ingediend."
>
> More manageable: "Ze heeft de aanvraag gisteren ingediend. Daarvoor waren er verschillende vragen over gegevens die in het oorspronkelijke formulier ontbraken."

The revision retains the sequence without asserting that the questions caused the delay. Move a qualification into a following clause or sentence when its weight obscures the main assertion.

Suitable phrases and clauses can stand after the final verb group: "We hebben lang gesproken over de nieuwe regeling." Do not force every complement inside the bracket on the assumption that more final verbs make prose more authentically Dutch.

### Preserve legitimate verb-group ordering

Dutch permits variation in the placement of a participle relative to an auxiliary in many two-verb groups.

> "Ik weet dat zij de aanvraag heeft ingediend."
>
> "Ik weet dat zij de aanvraag ingediend heeft."

Both orders can be standard. Rhythm, emphasis, register, region, and the particular construction affect preference. Do not correct one universally into the other, alternate them mechanically, or import a German default.

Longer groups have further constraints. Not every permutation preserves grammaticality, meaning, or register.

> "Hij heeft de brief moeten schrijven."
>
> "Hij moet de brief hebben geschreven."

The first reports a past necessity; the second can express an inference about a completed action. Reordering is not merely cosmetic.

Use an intelligible standard order in formal explanation. Recognize that regional and spoken variants can be legitimate in appropriate contexts. If a long group is difficult, recast the sentence without changing the relation among possibility, obligation, completion, and action.

Keep separable verb components and other elements in positions the construction allows. Do not place an arbitrary complement between auxiliaries because that position seems to fit the source-language order.

### Use word order to connect and emphasize

Use the opening of a sentence to maintain a topic, mark a real contrast, or establish a relevant frame. Do not front random material merely to vary sentence openings.

> "De commissie wees het tweede voorstel af."
>
> "Het tweede voorstel wees de commissie af."

The second can continue a discussion of proposals or contrast the second with another. The grammatical context must still make the roles clear.

Unlike German, Dutch nouns and articles do not carry an extensive productive case system that reliably distinguishes subject from object. Reordering can therefore create ambiguity even where a German-shaped counterpart would remain clear. Use a more explicit construction when both readings are plausible.

Established information often provides the starting point and newer information receives later prominence. This is a useful tendency rather than a rigid sorting rule. Pronouns, definiteness, emphasis, and verb choice affect natural ordering.

### Express one main thought without imposing one sentence length

A sentence can carry an assertion with its condition, reason, or consequence. Split it when several claims compete or dependencies force readers to backtrack.

> Overloaded: "De server wees de aanvraag af, waarin het token ontbrak, en de client probeerde het opnieuw, hoewel het account inmiddels geblokkeerd was, wat opnieuw een fout veroorzaakte."
>
> Clearer, if both causes are established: "De server wees de aanvraag af omdat het token ontbrak. De client probeerde het opnieuw. Die poging mislukte doordat het account inmiddels geblokkeerd was."

A grammatical repair must not resolve an uncertain causal relationship by invention. If the cause remains unknown, preserve that uncertainty.

Subordination can make relationships clearer than a series of isolated main clauses. Dutch's capacity for complex sentences is neither a requirement to overload them nor a reason to prohibit them.

### Use active and passive voice according to perspective

Use active voice when agency or responsibility matters: "Het team beoordeelt de aanvraag." Use passive voice when the process, affected object, or unknown actor is the appropriate focus: "De aanvraag wordt beoordeeld."

Do not invent an actor to remove a passive. Avoid hiding a known responsible party when the reader needs to know who acts.

Distinguish an ongoing or general process, a completed action, and a state according to context.

> "De deur wordt gesloten."
>
> "De deur is gisteren gesloten."
>
> "De deur is gesloten."

The second can describe a completed passive event. The third can describe a state or, in context, a completed action. Do not mechanically label every "zijn" plus participle as only a state or every such construction as a defect.

Dutch permits impersonal passives: "Er wordt gewerkt." Their grammar does not require an invented personal subject.

"Men", "we", and a passive are not interchangeable. "We beoordelen uw aanvraag" identifies a responsible group and a reader relationship; "Uw aanvraag wordt beoordeeld" foregrounds its treatment. "De aanvraag kan online worden ingediend" expresses possibility, not completion.

### Make clause relationships explicit

Coordinate ideas of comparable weight. Use subordination for a genuine reason, condition, concession, or dependency. Repeated "en" can conceal the actual relationship.

> Sequence only: "De batterij was leeg en het alarm ging niet af."
>
> Cause, if established: "Het alarm ging niet af doordat de batterij leeg was."

Preserve whether a clause states a condition, a reason, or an observed sequence. Distinguish "als" from "of" in instructions: "Controleer of het bestand bestaat" asks for a determination; "Controleer het bestand als het bestaat" makes the action conditional.

Use "toen" for a single past occasion where that is the intended relation, "als" for relevant conditions or repeated situations, and "wanneer" according to its temporal or conditional function. Avoid translating every source connector with the same Dutch word.

An appended "waarbij", "waardoor", or "wat" clause needs an identifiable relationship. If it ambiguously comments on an entire preceding account, name the intended proposition.

### Combine related actions without adding detail

Combine short sentences when they describe one continuous movement and separate emphasis is unnecessary.

> Repetitive: "Ze ging naar de markt. Ze kocht groenten. Ze ging naar huis."
>
> Connected: "Ze ging naar de markt, kocht groenten en ging naar huis."

Do not add freshness, emotion, speed, or a motive merely to improve flow. Retain the actor when an omitted subject could refer to someone else.

Parallel expressions need grammatical parallelism. Two verbs can share a complement only where their constructions permit it. Preserve required prepositions instead of producing an attractive but defective coordinated phrase.

### Express appended consequences as actual claims

Promotional riders can appear as "waardoor ... wordt gegarandeerd", "wat ... onderstreept", or a trailing participial expression. They often add an unsupported interpretation or benefit.

> Empty addition: "De dienst slaat het bestand op, wat een naadloze ervaring garandeert."
>
> Direct: "De dienst slaat het bestand op."

Where the consequence is substantive, identify it and support the connection. Do not replace the vague adjective while retaining an unsupported guarantee.

English nonfinite constructions may require a Dutch clause, infinitive group, noun phrase, or separate assertion. Repeated "waardoor" is no better than repeated translated participles if the relationship remains unearned.

### Keep modifiers, focus, and negation clear

Place limiting words so their scope is intelligible. Position, emphasis, and context can all contribute.

> "Alleen Noor controleert de aanvraag."
>
> "Noor controleert alleen de aanvraag."

The first limits the actor. The second can limit what Noor checks or does. Add an explicit contrast where a consequential distinction would otherwise remain ambiguous.

Preserve the meaning of "nog niet", "niet meer", "pas", "al", "bijna", and "slechts". Moving them for rhythm can change time, expectation, or scope.

Use "geen" and "niet" according to the construction: "Ik heb geen account"; "Het account is niet actief". Avoid double negation that accidentally changes the claim. A deliberate colloquial double negative belongs to its voice rather than to general explanatory prose.

Do not mechanically impose an English or German position for negation. Keep the intended constituent and contrast recognizable.

### Give reduced clauses a recoverable actor

Infinitive and participial clauses can compress information when the actor and relation are clear. They are not automatically more natural or more concise.

> Misattached: "Aangekomen op het station begon het te regenen."
>
> Clear: "Toen ik op het station aankwam, begon het te regenen."

The problem is the implied relation between arrival and the main clause's subject, not participles in general. Some constructions allow an understood actor supplied by an object or context. Use a full clause when readers would assign the action incorrectly.

Do not reproduce an English introductory participle merely because the words can be translated. Identify who acts, and preserve whether the relation is time, cause, condition, or manner.

### Unpack overloaded attributes

Dutch allows adjectives and expanded participial attributes before nouns: "de gisteren gepubliceerde versie". A familiar compact modifier need not become a relative clause.

Several nested relationships before the noun can overload comprehension.

> Dense: "de door de na de reorganisatie nieuw ingestelde commissie voorgestelde wijzigingen"
>
> More manageable as two sentences: "De commissie is na de reorganisatie ingesteld. Zij heeft de wijzigingen voorgesteld."

The alternative preserves the commission's formation, its timing, and its role. In another context, a lighter attribute or relative clause may integrate the same information more naturally.

Do not copy extended German noun phrases as though they were the default for Dutch exposition. Split or recast according to the actual dependencies and emphasis.

### Give pronouns clear referents

"Hij", "zij", "ze", "het", "dit", "dat", "die", and "wat" need recognizable antecedents. Agreement and proximity do not always resolve competing candidates.

> Ambiguous: "Toen de client de server bereikte, startte hij opnieuw op."
>
> Clear, if that is the event: "De server startte opnieuw op toen de client hem bereikte."

The explicit main-clause subject identifies what restarted without adding a cause or a different technical event. Where both entities could be meant, name the intended one.

Use "dit" or "dat" for a recognizable proposition; name it where the reference would otherwise cover several possible claims. A vague "hieromtrent" rarely repairs unclear reference.

Distinguish reference to a thing, a person, and an entire clause. The rules for relative pronouns and pronominal adverbs appear in their own subsections. Avoid making a single reference word carry an unexplained argument.

### Match personal pronouns to their function

Distinguish subject, object, and possessive forms where Dutch marks the difference. "Ik", "mij", and "mijn" do not substitute for one another. Use unstressed forms such as "me", "we", and "ze" where they fit the voice; emphatic forms should carry actual emphasis or contrast.

For ordinary plural personal reference, formal written usage distinguishes object "hen" from indirect-object "hun" without a preposition and possessive "hun".

> "Ik zag hen gisteren."
>
> "Ik gaf hun het bestand."
>
> "Ik gaf het bestand aan hen."
>
> "Dat is hun bestand."

Unstressed "ze" can provide a natural object form where the context permits it. Do not make every ordinary sentence conspicuously formal merely to display a pronoun distinction.

Use "zij" or "ze" as an ordinary plural subject in standard exposition, rather than colloquial subject "hun". These plural conventions are separate from a known individual's stated gender-neutral pronouns. Preserve that person's usage and clear agreement instead of applying a plural-object rule to every occurrence of "hen" or "hun".

### Use "er" according to its function

"Er" is often a necessary part of natural Dutch, not removable padding. Recognize its distinct functions.

| Function | Illustrative wording | Contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Presentative | "Er staat iemand voor de deur." | Introduces an indefinite participant into the account. |
| Locative | "Ze woont er al jaren." | Refers to an established place. |
| Quantitative | "Ik heb er nog drie." | Refers to a previously established countable category. |
| Prepositional | "We hebben er lang over gesproken." | Combines with a preposition to express an established relation. |

Do not translate every English location word into "er" or remove every "er" because it has no separately visible English equivalent. In "Ik heb er drie", omission may destroy the intended reference. In a location sentence, omission can leave the place unspecified instead of preserving the meaning.

Presentative "er" supports information structure and occurs naturally in impersonal passives such as "Er wordt gedanst". Its presence depends on construction and context; do not prefix it to every indefinite subject.

One occurrence can serve more than one role. Avoid duplicating it when a single form carries the required functions. Rephrase a difficult construction rather than treating insertion or deletion as a mechanical rule.

### Place and spell pronominal adverbs naturally

Dutch combines "er", "hier", "daar", or "waar" with prepositional elements. A joined form can be correct when the components are adjacent; splitting is often natural or required by the sentence.

> "We praten erover."
>
> "We praten er morgen over."
>
> "Daar heb ik niet op gerekend."

Do not keep every combination adjacent on the model of a German word or separate adjacent components that form one pronominal adverb. Distinguish them from two words belonging to different constituents.

The antecedent must remain clear. "Daarmee" can refer economically to a known means or proposition; it cannot clarify which of several preceding ideas is meant without context.

Reference to people can require "met wie", "over haar", or another personal construction. Actual usage admits contextual variation, but a pronominal adverb is not a universal replacement for any preposition plus person.

Keep spelling dependent on the grammatical relationship, not on whether a space looks visually tidy. Joining and splitting are meaning-bearing choices.

### Preserve articles and adjective agreement

Choose the correct article and maintain agreement through revisions. A change between a singular noun, a plural noun, and a substitute term can require changes throughout the phrase.

> "een goed boek"
>
> "het goede boek"
>
> "een goede uitleg"
>
> "goede boeken"

These illustrate ordinary attributive adjective patterns, not an exhaustive exception-free rule. Predicative adjectives do not acquire the same ending: "Het boek is goed". Established expressions and particular adjective types can behave differently.

### Choose relative pronouns by their antecedent

Relative "die" normally refers to singular "de" words and plurals; "dat" normally refers to singular "het" words.

> "de aanvraag die ik heb verstuurd"
>
> "het bestand dat ik heb verstuurd"
>
> "de bestanden die ik heb verstuurd"

English relative-pronoun omission does not transfer. "Wat" can refer to a preceding proposition and appears in other established constructions; it is not a general replacement for relative "dat" after every concrete noun. Human reference can require sensitivity to grammatical and semantic context rather than a universal noun-gender slogan.

### Preserve verb constructions and agreement

Use the complement and preposition the Dutch verb requires: "wachten op", "deelnemen aan", "afhangen van", and "zich herinneren" have their own constructions. A source-language preposition is not a sufficient guide.

Retain required reflexive pronouns and distinguish acts such as remembering from reminding someone. Do not add "zich" merely because a German counterpart is reflexive.

Maintain subject-verb agreement through changes of person, number, tense, and construction. A nearby plural noun does not automatically determine the number of the main verb; identify the actual subject.

Present-tense inversion with subject "jij" or "je" can remove an added second-person "-t": "Jij vindt"; "Vind jij?" This does not apply in the same way to "u" or to "je" used as an object or possessive: "Vindt je collega dat ook?" Identify the subject before applying the spelling rule.

Use correct finite and participial forms, including "-d", "-t", and "-dt". Sound alone may not distinguish them. A revision that changes person, tense, or construction must preserve the corresponding spelling.

Borrowed verbs take their correct Dutch forms. "Geüpdatet" does not acquire the English ending of the source verb. If an inflected form is cumbersome, choose an accurate alternative construction rather than misspelling it. A real update need not be renamed an improvement unless it actually improves something.

### Keep the small words that carry grammar

Retain articles, verbs, conjunctions, and prepositions where they clarify relationships. "Verwijder het back-upbestand" suits an instruction; "Back-upbestand verwijderen" suits a task heading or label.

Repeat a word where omission makes a coordination ambiguous. Concision should not force the reader to infer a missing actor or reconstruct incompatible complements.

Keep "dat" when it clearly introduces a subordinate clause. Some constructions permit alternatives, but English omission is not a general Dutch default.

Ordinary anticipatory "het" or presentative "er" can serve a grammatical function. Distinguish useful structure from empty openings such as repeated "Het is belangrijk om ...". Remove the padding, not required grammar.

### Use grammatically complete prose by default

Use complete sentences in ordinary explanation. Missing verbs, arrows, and compressed noun strings belong only where the medium supports recoverable shorthand.

> Compressed: "Datum ongeldig → stoppen, niet opslaan."
>
> Explanatory: "Als de datum ongeldig is, stopt het programma zonder iets op te slaan."

Labels, headings, status messages, dialogue, and purposeful literary effects can use fragments. That exception does not justify an explanation made entirely of dramatic fragments.

### Preserve tense, aspect, and temporal reference

Choose tense according to the temporal perspective and form. Dutch perfect and simple-past forms do not map mechanically onto English present perfect and simple past or onto German usage.

An account of completed events can use the perfect; sustained past narration often uses the simple past. Context, verb, and narrative frame affect the choice. Do not impose a single tense on all written Dutch.

Present forms can express scheduled future events: "De cursus begint morgen." Use "zullen" or "gaan" where prediction, intention, or another relevant meaning belongs; they are not interchangeable future decorations.

Continuing situations can use the present: "Ik woon hier al drie jaar". Do not change the construction so that the situation appears to have ended.

"Aan het" plus an infinitive can express an ongoing activity: "Ik ben de aanvraag aan het invullen". Posture-verb constructions such as "Ze zit te lezen" can be natural in appropriate contexts. They need not correspond literally to an English progressive, and adding one can introduce a viewpoint, posture, or attitude absent from the source.

Use an appropriate earlier-past form to orient prior events in a past account, without marking every sentence repeatedly once the frame is clear. A shift in tense should reflect time, perspective, or voice.

## Vocabulary and expression

### Prefer the familiar word that says the right thing

Choose the ordinary term where it accurately expresses the concept: "gebruiken" rather than "aanwenden", "helpen" rather than "ondersteuning bieden" where no distinction is lost, and "als" rather than "in het geval dat".

Precision can require a less common technical, legal, historical, or literary term. Rarity is not a virtue, but familiarity alone does not establish accuracy.

Natural Dutch may use a compound where English uses several words or a simple verb where literal translation produces abstraction. Judge the Dutch expression rather than its word count relative to the source.

### Replace inflated wording with the actual meaning

Repeated elevated language makes unrelated subjects sound alike. State the action, condition, result, or relationship instead of asserting its importance.

| Inflated or vague use | More direct Dutch treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "een cruciale rol spelen" | State what depends on it or what it changes. |
| "van essentieel belang zijn" | Name the requirement or consequence. |
| "diepgaande inzichten bieden" | State what is explained, observed, or understood. |
| "het volledige potentieel ontsluiten" | Name the capability or supported improvement. |
| "naar een hoger niveau tillen" | Describe the actual change. |
| "duurzaam verbeteren" | Specify the improvement and duration where established. |
| "holistisch" | Name what is included and how the parts relate. |
| "naadloos" | Describe the compatibility, transition, or absence of interruption. |
| "baanbrekend", "revolutionair" | State the established change without declaring historical importance. |
| "innovatief" | Name what is new and relevant. |
| "op maat gemaakt" | State the requirement the solution addresses. |
| "veelzijdig", "veelomvattend" | Explain the actual functions or scope. |
| "een dynamisch landschap" | Name the field, market, setting, or development. |
| "synergie creëren" | Explain what cooperation enables or saves. |
| "krachtig onderstrepen" | State the supported conclusion and its limits. |
| "een bewijs vormen van" | Identify the evidence and the specific claim it supports. |

These expressions are not prohibited strings. A literal seam, a defined sustainability criterion, or a genuine evidential relationship may require the word. Empty praise is the defect; replacing it with a grander synonym preserves it.

### Say "is" or "heeft" when that is the relationship

Use direct identification and possession where appropriate. "Dit programma is een parser" is clearer than "Dit programma fungeert als een vooruitstrevende parsingoplossing" when no additional function is meant.

Keep "dient als", "beschikt over", or "kenmerkt zich door" where the relationship matters. "De kamer dient ook als archief" identifies a use; "Het huis heeft drie ingangen" often suffices for possession.

Do not force every predicate into a striking action verb. State, existence, possession, and classification are legitimate meanings.

### Remove filler without removing meaning

| Padded expression | Direct expression where equivalent |
| --- | --- |
| "vanwege het feit dat" | "omdat" |
| "met als doel om" | "om" or a clause identifying the purpose |
| "in het geval dat" | "als" |
| "met betrekking tot" | Name the relationship, often with "over" or another precise preposition. |
| "ten aanzien van" | State the actual object or relationship. |
| "op dit punt dient te worden opgemerkt dat" | State the relevant information. |
| "het is belangrijk om te benadrukken dat" | State the consequential fact and, if needed, its consequence. |
| "samenvattend kunnen we concluderen dat" | Give a necessary synthesis or stop. |
| "eventueel mogelijkerwijs" | Retain the qualification the claim needs. |
| "in zekere zin" | Specify the limited respect in which the statement holds. |
| "in principe", "doorgaans" | Retain a genuine distinction between a rule and its exceptions. |
| "eigenlijk", "daadwerkelijk", "heel" | Keep a real correction, contrast, degree, or voice function. |

Do not turn a bounded statement into an absolute one. "In principe toegestaan" may leave room for relevant exceptions; deleting the phrase may change meaning. Explain consequential exceptions rather than hiding them behind vague qualification.

Replacing "een heel goed punt" with "een uitstekend punt" adds no substance. Explain its relevance where that explanation is needed.

### Keep terminology stable

Call an entity, operation, or defined concept by the same name. Repetition of an accurate noun is clearer than approximate synonym cycling.

Switching among "aanvraag", "verzoek", "melding", and "dossier" may imply distinctions the system does not contain. A character need not become "de hoofdpersoon", "de held", and "de centrale figuur" merely to avoid a name.

Vary construction when the passage is monotonous. Do not vary technical meaning. Explain genuine regional or institutional terminology differences instead of smoothing them away.

### Choose idiomatic combinations

Dutch words form established combinations. "Een besluit nemen", "een aanvraag indienen", "een vraag stellen", and "verantwoordelijkheid nemen" express particular actions naturally.

An individually correct word can still form an unnatural phrase. Compose the relationship in Dutch rather than matching source words one at a time.

Keep established prepositional combinations: "interesse in", "afhankelijk van", "verantwoordelijk voor". The source preposition does not decide the Dutch pattern.

Preserve differences among near-synonyms. "Herkennen", "erkennen", "beseffen", and "vaststellen" identify different acts. Fluency must not blur them.

Familiar functional combinations need not be replaced solely because they contain a noun. Assess the whole expression and the meaning it carries.

### Use compounds without hiding relationships

Dutch forms compounds productively. Familiar words such as "gegevensbescherming", "gebruikersnaam", and "handleiding" are not automatically noun-pile defects.

An unfamiliar compound can hide who acts, what belongs to what, or how parts relate. Unpack it where the relationship matters.

> Dense: "klantgegevensvrijgaveaanvraagbeoordeling"
>
> Clearer for one intended meaning: "beoordeling van de aanvraag om klantgegevens vrij te geven"

The expanded form identifies one grouping; a different intended relationship needs different wording. A hyphen can clarify components without resolving every ambiguity.

Do not keep English word spaces in a Dutch compound merely because the individual components are borrowed. Nor should every adjacent phrase be joined: a word group and a compound are different constructions.

Spelling and joining conventions belong under presentation. The lexical choice here is whether the compound names an intelligible and useful concept.

### Prefer direct verbs without banning nominal expressions

"We beoordelen de aanvraag" often improves "We voeren een beoordeling van de aanvraag uit". "Het team besloot" can express a decision more directly than an extended nominal construction.

An established expression can encode aspect or perspective. "In werking stellen" differs from "in werking zijn"; "ter beschikking stellen" differs from "ter beschikking staan".

An abstract noun is appropriate where the concept itself is the subject. "De authenticatie is mislukt" can identify an exact event. It need not become a vague statement about someone failing.

Remove noun chains that conceal dependencies. "Na afronding van de uitvoering van de controle" usually needs an account of what finishes before what begins. The revision should expose relationships, not merely reduce syllables.

### Choose verbs that preserve the actual action

A precise verb can replace a weak verb plus modifier when it describes the same action. "Slofte" may replace "liep langzaam" if the gait really is a shuffle. "Fluisterde" adds a manner of speech not present in every "zei zacht".

Keep adverbs expressing scope, frequency, degree, sequence, or uncertainty. "Mislukt vaak" and "mislukt" are different claims.

English broad-purpose verbs may require different Dutch verbs in different contexts. Name the actual act instead of repeatedly using translated catch-alls such as "realiseren", "adresseren", or "faciliteren".

Do not intensify a character's action just to avoid an adverb. An ordinary accurate phrase can be better than a conspicuous verb.

### Handle false friends, calques, and borrowings by meaning

Use the Dutch expression for the intended concept rather than the nearest-looking word.

| Dutch expression | Meaning to preserve |
| --- | --- |
| "eventueel" | A possibility or contingency, not an eventual outcome. |
| "actueel" | Current or topical, not an automatic equivalent of factual actuality. |
| "sensibel" | Sensitive in relevant usage, not a general equivalent of reasonable. |
| "brutaal" | Often rude, impudent, or cheeky, not automatically physically brutal. |
| "controleren" | Checking or inspecting in many contexts, not every form of control or influence. |
| "solliciteren" | Applying for a job, not every kind of solicitation. |
| "realiseren" | Bringing something about; "zich realiseren" can express becoming aware. The construction matters. |

Do not preserve an English metaphor or collocation simply because a word-for-word version is understandable. An intelligible calque may still sound translated.

Borrowings can be established and useful. "Software" or "account" need not acquire strained substitutes. Unexplained "ownership", "alignment", and "impact" can conceal ordinary distinctions among responsibility, agreement, and effect. Name the distinction the passage actually needs.

Integrate borrowed words into Dutch grammar and spelling where appropriate. Preserve exact product names, code, and interface labels. Do not alternate a borrowing and a Dutch term as though they named different concepts.

Avoid indiscriminate purism. Origin does not determine whether an expression is established, useful, or appropriate. Register, meaning, and audience do.

### Use particles for a real interpersonal effect

"Maar", "even", "toch", "wel", "eens", "hoor", and related particles can express invitation, reassurance, insistence, contrast, impatience, or shared expectation. They are not automatically filler.

> "Kom binnen."
>
> "Kom maar binnen."

The second can give permission or make an invitation gentler in context. It is not merely the first sentence with an extra word.

"Dat weet je toch" can appeal to assumed knowledge and may sound reassuring or reproachful. "Doe het even" can reduce the apparent burden of a request while also minimizing real effort.

Use particles according to construction and relationship. Do not sprinkle them into formal exposition or interface text to create artificial nativeness. Remove one when its attitude is unwanted, not merely because it lacks an obvious English counterpart.

The same word may be a connector, limiter, or particle in different contexts. Judge its function rather than applying a word ban.

### Use diminutives for their actual meaning and tone

Dutch diminutives can express size, familiarity, affection, mitigation, irony, or an established lexical meaning. They are not automatically childish and do not universally make prose friendly.

"Een huisje" may describe a small house or a particular affectionate perspective. "Een foutje" may minimize an error whose consequence matters. A request for "een formuliertje" can understate the burden on the reader.

Use a diminutive when that nuance belongs to the voice or referent. Do not add one to every object in conversational prose. Avoid asymmetric diminutives that diminish a person's professional work or status.

Established diminutive words can name the intended object directly. Do not expand them mechanically to remove informality that is not actually present. Their article and agreement follow Dutch grammar; a diminutive noun is normally a "het" word in the singular.

### Use literal meaning and purposeful imagery

In explanation, name the operation, relation, responsibility, or goal. Elevated metaphors can obscure an ordinary requirement.

| Vague metaphor or abstraction | More direct Dutch treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "de koers uitzetten" | Name the decision and intended direction. |
| "aan de knoppen draaien" | Name the parameter, intervention, or responsibility. |
| "als kompas dienen" | State the goal or decision criterion. |
| "een fundament leggen" | Name the prerequisite or supporting work. |
| "een ecosysteem bouwen" | Identify the interacting services, organizations, or conditions. |
| "een brug slaan" | Explain what is connected and how. |
| "door complexiteit navigeren" | Name the choices and constraints. |
| "een vliegwiel creëren" | Explain the repeated process and reinforcing effect. |
| "een nieuwe dimensie openen" | State the new capability or implication. |
| "een spanningsveld" | Identify the competing interests or requirements. |
| "een paradigmaverschuiving" | Explain the changed assumptions or method. |
| "een katalysator" | Name the accelerated process and evidence for the role. |
| "een tapijt van herinneringen" | Give the relevant memory or relationship. |
| "een ankerpunt" | Name the reference, support, or dependency. |

Literal and established technical meanings remain available. An actual ecosystem, chemical catalyst, mathematical dimension, bridge, or anchor can require the term.

Do not replace one vague metaphor with another. A decisive slogan such as "Aansluiten of afschaffen" may conceal the actual requirement. State the condition and action.

In narrative, an image can create an experience literal summary cannot. It should belong to the character, world, and moment rather than supply reusable atmosphere.

### Do not assign unsupported intentions

Describe animal behavior, system operation, or a process's effect without inventing thoughts or motives. Observed behavior does not establish the imagined declaration "Dit is van mij".

Technical shorthand such as "De server weigert de aanvraag" describes behavior. A product that "begrijpt wat je nodig hebt" needs a more precise account of the actual capability.

Literary personification can express a particular perception without asserting literal intention. Keep that purpose distinct from factual explanation.

### Express enthusiasm proportionately

Let the material establish interest. Avoid superlative chains, automatic praise, exclamation marks, flattering openings, and invitations to marvel together.

A measured voice can express curiosity, pleasure, grief, anger, or conviction. It need not be emotionally absent.

"Dat is heel indrukwekkend" does not become evidence as "Dat is werkelijk uitzonderlijk". State the supported achievement or make a relevant personal judgment recognizable as a judgment.

## Rhythm and coherence

### Vary sentence movement for meaning

Short sentences can establish a point or mark a turn. Longer sentences can sustain a connected explanation, condition, perception, or action. Choose length according to the thought rather than numerical alternation.

Use Dutch word-order resources to maintain a topic and shift emphasis, as described under syntax. Do not force a cycle of subject openings, inverted openings, and subordinate-clause openings.

Consider where a sentence's bracket closes and its emphasis lands. Readable written rhythm does not require reproducing spoken hesitation or adding particles everywhere.

### Avoid both monotony and artificial variety

Repeated "Het is ...", "Daarbij ...", and "Dit laat zien ..." may conceal differences among claims. Replacing them with equally empty introductions changes little.

> Mechanical: "Ik denk dat het voorstel nuttig is. Ik denk dat we het moeten proberen. Ik denk dat het zal werken."
>
> More direct: "Het voorstel lijkt me nuttig. We moeten het proberen. Ik verwacht dat het werkt."

The alternative retains judgment and expectation instead of declaring success. Wording variation does not authorize stronger certainty.

Avoid long subordinate-clause chains and strings of abrupt independent sentences. Link, subordinate, or separate according to meaning. A repeated construction can be purposeful in dialogue, rhetoric, or instructions.

Do not introduce errors, rare vocabulary, random digressions, or fragments merely to reduce predictability. Smoothness and irregularity are not independent measures of quality.

### Let paragraph length follow the material

Give a difficult explanation enough space and an important narrative consequence enough development. A short paragraph can emphasize a genuine turn; making every sentence a separate paragraph weakens that effect.

Breaks can mark thought, speaker, time, scene, and attention. Uniform lengths are not a standard, and constant variation is not one either.

Do not stretch or compress Dutch paragraphs to match an English source's word count. Preserve their function and relevant information. Compounds and grammatical differences make simple word-count comparisons unreliable as quality rules.

## Punctuation and presentation

### Use commas to clarify Dutch clause structure

Use periods and commas for ordinary separation. Dutch comma conventions are not a translation of German clause punctuation or an instruction to mark every pause.

An introductory subordinate clause commonly takes a separating comma, especially where two finite verbs meet: "Als de aanvraag compleet is, beoordelen we die." A short ordinary opening phrase does not automatically take one: "Na de vergadering beoordelen we de aanvraag."

Do not insert a comma before every "dat", "omdat", or infinitive group. "Ik weet dat de aanvraag compleet is" normally needs none. Longer or potentially confusing constructions may need a comma to expose their structure.

Infinitive clauses do not inherit a blanket German comma requirement: "Ze probeert het bestand te openen." Use punctuation according to the Dutch construction and its weight.

Short related independent clauses can sometimes be joined with a comma in Dutch. Use a period, conjunction, or suitable semicolon where otherwise the relationship is unclear or the sentence becomes an undifferentiated chain.

Do not insert an English serial comma mechanically into every list. A comma before "en" can clarify a difficult grouping or follow an inserted clause, but is not a default sign of careful prose.

### Distinguish restrictive and supplementary clauses

Before a restrictive relative clause, omit the comma that would present it as additional information. A supplementary clause normally receives separating commas. The distinction can change who or what the statement concerns.

> "De medewerkers die op vrijdag werken, krijgen een bericht."
>
> "De medewerkers, die op vrijdag werken, krijgen een bericht."

The first identifies the subset working on Friday. The second presents Friday work as additional information about the employees already identified. Do not add or delete the first comma merely to change rhythm.

A following comma can mark the boundary with the main clause, particularly where it separates verb forms. Not every short restrictive construction requires identical visible punctuation. Preserve its intended function rather than applying a mechanical two-comma pattern.

Reduced modifying clauses can also restrict or supplement a noun. Choose punctuation according to that meaning. If the distinction remains hard to see, recast the sentence explicitly instead of expecting one comma to carry a crucial ambiguity.

### Keep sentence punctuation restrained

Do not use em dashes, en dashes, or substitute hyphens as sentence separators. Integrate a short addition grammatically, give it a separate sentence, or use appropriate parentheses for genuinely supplementary information.

Parentheses can hold an abbreviation, brief aside, or necessary notation. Do not hide an essential condition in material readers are invited to skip. Repeated side comments fracture a direct explanation.

A semicolon can connect closely related clauses or separate complex list items. It is not an ornamental marker of sophistication or a substitute dash. Prefer a period when no special relationship needs marking.

Use question and exclamation marks according to communicative function. Avoid multiple exclamation marks, repeated rhetorical questions, and ellipses that manufacture suspense.

Preserve exact punctuation in faithful quotations, identifiers, commands, and formal notation. Lexical hyphens, minus signs, and conventional ranges are not sentence separators.

### Give colons an actual introductory function

A colon can introduce a list, example, quotation, explanation, or elaboration. The preceding words should establish what follows. Do not use one as an automatic bridge after every condition or bold label.

> Awkward: "Als u de instellingen wijzigt: Start de dienst opnieuw."
>
> Clear: "Start de dienst opnieuw nadat u de instellingen hebt gewijzigd."

Dutch normally continues with a lowercase letter after a colon, including before a single explanatory sentence. Proper names and introduced full-sentence quotations retain the appropriate capital; a multi-sentence passage or a publication's specified format can require another treatment.

> "De melding is duidelijk: het bestand ontbreekt."
>
> "Ze zei: 'Het bestand ontbreekt.'"

The inner single quotes in the second example represent a quotation within the double-quoted example. In ordinary authored dialogue, use the house double quotes.

Keep colons in exact times, paths, identifiers, and notation. Their technical use is separate from editorial transitions.

### Use straight quotation marks

Use straight double quotes for authored Dutch examples, dialogue, and quoted expressions. Use straight single quotes for a quotation inside one of those passages. This is a house convention; Dutch publications also use other consistent quotation styles.

Place punctuation according to whether it belongs to the quoted words or the surrounding sentence.

> "Ik kom morgen", zei ze.
>
> "Kom je morgen?", vroeg ze.
>
> Ze zei: "Ik kom morgen."

A comma joining a reporting clause normally stands outside the quotation when it does not belong to the quoted text. After a quoted question or exclamation, conventions permit variation; use a coherent publication style rather than mixing systems.

Some literary publishers put dialogue commas inside the quotation marks. That convention is not evidence of incorrect Dutch, but does not require changing the chosen house convention throughout ordinary explanatory prose.

Preserve faithful source quotations and required publication notation. Do not use quotation marks for decorative emphasis or to imply skepticism without explaining its basis.

### Use Dutch capitalization

Use sentence-case headings. Capitalize the first word and proper names, not every important-looking noun. Dutch ordinary nouns do not inherit German capitals.

> Informative heading: "Wanneer de back-up vervalt"
>
> Vague heading: "Belangrijke overwegingen"

Names of languages retain their normal capitals: "Nederlands", "Engels", and "Duits". Weekdays and months normally remain lowercase: "maandag", "april". Follow the established spelling of proper names and derived forms rather than an English title pattern.

When a word beginning with "ij" requires an initial capital, capitalize the digraph appropriately: "IJsselmeer". Treat sentence beginnings with elided forms according to Dutch convention: "'s Avonds is het rustig."

Courtesy pronouns "u" and "uw" do not take German-style capitals. Necessary religious conventions and sentence-initial capitalization are separate cases.

Use a logical heading hierarchy. Give headings a navigational role rather than adding one before every short paragraph.

### Join and hyphenate compounds correctly

Write Dutch compounds together where required: "klantenservice", "gebruikersnaam", "softwareversie". Do not inherit English spaces simply because the components are borrowed.

Keep genuine word groups separate. The difference between a compound and a phrase can affect meaning. Avoid trying to repair an unclear relationship merely by deleting spaces.

Use required hyphens in constructions involving suitable abbreviations, numbers, vowel collisions, or particular word formations.

> "e-mailadres"
>
> "pdf-bestand"
>
> "3D-printer"
>
> "auto-ongeluk"
>
> "in- en uitvoer"

An optional clarifying hyphen may help an unfamiliar compound. Do not split every familiar word. Linking elements such as "-s-" and "-en-" follow Dutch spelling rather than the apparent number of things in the sentence. Preserve established forms instead of inventing a productive rule for every compound.

Names and exact identifiers retain their own spelling. A brand's typography does not establish a universal rule for ordinary Dutch compounds.

### Use apostrophes where spelling requires them

Dutch uses apostrophes in specific plurals, possessives, elisions, and formations. Do not apply an English possessive apostrophe universally.

> "auto's"
>
> "oma's fiets"
>
> "Jans fiets"
>
> "Max' fiets"

The apostrophe can protect a vowel's pronunciation or mark a possessive after an appropriate sibilant; its presence depends on the construction. Do not remove required apostrophes while simplifying typography.

### Preserve diacritics and use emphasis accents sparingly

Keep a trema where it marks separate syllables: "ideeën", "geïntegreerd", "geüpdatet". A compound's vowel collision may instead require a hyphen. These marks have spelling functions, not decorative ones.

Use emphasis accents sparingly. "Een" does not need to become "één" whenever it names the number; add accents when they resolve ambiguity or provide justified emphasis. Avoid a passage full of "écht", "zéker", and "nú" to simulate a persuasive voice.

### Present numbers, dates, and units for the intended context

Use the Dutch decimal comma in ordinary prose: "1,5 liter". Keep grouping separators distinct from decimal notation and follow the intended locale or publication.

Write unambiguous dates. "3 april 2026" avoids competing interpretations of an international numeric date. Preserve the real date, year, and relevant timezone relationship.

A space normally separates quantities and unit symbols: "15 km", "23 °C". General prose commonly uses "20%" without a space; technical and scientific conventions may require "20 %". Choose the appropriate system consistently rather than imposing one convention on every context.

Currency signs commonly precede the amount with a space: "€ 12,50". Spell units and currencies naturally without changing the underlying value. Exact code, exported data, identifiers, and required scientific notation retain their syntax.

Distinguish formatting from conversion. A changed decimal separator does not change a value. Converting currency, units, or timezones requires a reliable basis and relevant reference conditions. Do not silently replace a dollar price with a euro price or a factual location with a local substitute.

### Use emphasis sparingly

Bold text should help readers locate a consequential distinction or required interface label. Do not bold every noun, acronym, sentence opening, or repeated term.

Avoid labels followed by restatement. "Prestaties: De prestaties zijn verbeterd" contributes no useful distinction. A label earns its place by introducing new information.

Use italics or quotation marks for real linguistic, editorial, or publication functions rather than habitual emphasis. Competing emphasis systems obscure the hierarchy.

### Let lists express actual structure

Use numbered lists for ordered sequences and bullets for parallel unordered items. Explain what the list represents and keep its grammar parallel.

Whole-sentence items retain sentence punctuation. Fragments and items grammatically integrated with the introduction follow a coherent convention suited to their construction. Do not force an action label into a full sentence when the medium needs a label.

Use paragraphs for connected argument. A list cannot replace the explanation of relationships. Tables suit comparisons and mappings. Do not force three items, clauses, or examples onto material that naturally has another shape.

### Remove decorative interface and chatbot residue

Begin with useful information. Omit automatic acknowledgments, flattery, congratulations, repeated offers of help, and generic sign-offs from substantive prose.

Typical padding includes "Natuurlijk!", "Zeker!", "Wat een goede vraag!", "Je hebt helemaal gelijk", "Ik hoop dat dit helpt", and "Laat het weten als je nog vragen hebt". A real greeting or closing may belong to correspondence; a quotation may require the words. Function determines whether they stay.

Omit decorative emojis from headings and bullets. Preserve symbols used as data, required interface content, or the subject of discussion.

Remove commentary about preparing the answer, assurances of completeness, and self-praise about clarity. Provide the material instead of announcing the quality of its presentation.

## Style-specific conventions

Apply the general principles throughout. Each style adds requirements and narrow exceptions that perform a concrete function. A document may contain an explanation followed by instructions; keep each passage appropriate to its role.

Style does not authorize invented evidence, unsupported certainty, decorative jargon, mechanical repetition, or obscure syntax. A scholarly convention needs a scholarly function; a literary device needs a narrative purpose.

### Academic

#### Formality with accessible language

Use precise, accessible Dutch appropriate to the scholarly context. Avoid slang, promotional language, conversational padding, forced intimacy, and ceremonial abstraction.

> Inflated: "De onderhavige studie beoogt het veelomvattende samenspel van de voornoemde factoren aan een diepgaande beschouwing te onderwerpen."
>
> Direct: "Deze studie onderzoekt het samenspel van de genoemde factoren."

Name the factors when the surrounding passage does not identify them. Retain a defined statistical term such as "interactie" when a looser paraphrase would change the concept.

Formality comes from explicit reasoning, disciplined claims, and precise terminology. Rare vocabulary, long noun groups, and unnecessary Latin do not supply those qualities.

#### Disciplinary voice and researcher position

Use first person when it identifies a research decision, argument, or relevant relationship to the material. "Ik lees de passage als een bezwaar tegen die interpretatie" locates an interpretive claim. "We sloten onvolledige antwoorden uit" identifies agency.

Neither first nor third person guarantees scholarly legitimacy. Match the particular field, form, and venue rather than assigning a single voice to a discipline. A passive can foreground a specimen while an active sentence identifies a methodological decision.

| Kind of writing | Relevant emphasis |
| --- | --- |
| Empirical research | Distinguish observations, reported experiences, analytical choices, and interpretation; identify consequential researcher agency. |
| Interpretive scholarship | Ground readings in the text, record, object, or experience and explain the inferential move. |
| Theoretical argument | Define concepts, expose assumptions, develop relationships, and distinguish proposals from established findings. |
| Practitioner-facing research | Explain consequences, intended users, conditions, and constraints without unsupported promises. |

Where positionality affects interpretation, describe actual access, involvement, institutional role, or experience. Generic neutrality claims and personal confession do not explain that relationship. Keep participant perspectives separate from researcher interpretation.

#### Research questions and contributions

State the question precisely enough to identify the phenomenon, population or material, setting, and relationship that matter. Distinguish descriptive, comparative, predictive, causal, and exploratory purposes.

Align the contribution with the problem established. Another prevalence estimate does not answer a claimed gap concerning a mechanism. Explain the substantive difference from the nearest relevant work rather than declaring an entire field unexplored.

Bound "de eerste studie" and absence claims by the literature actually covered. "Voor zover bekend" alone does not establish that scope. Where an absence claim matters, identify its relevant scope and date. A limitation in the review is not necessarily an absence across the field.

> Overbroad: "Naar dit probleem is nog geen onderzoek gedaan."
>
> More specific form: "De besproken studies schatten de frequentie op stedelijke scholen. Over het onderliggende mechanisme op plattelandsscholen is minder bekend."

Both statements in the alternative need support. Do not invent a population or mechanism gap to make a contribution appear original.

#### Titles, abstracts, and keywords

A title identifies the subject and scope needed to distinguish the paper. Prefer useful concepts to empty openers such as "Een onderzoek naar". Include population, setting, material, or design when it changes what readers should expect.

A declarative title needs the same restraint as the paper's claim. A question title should identify a question actually addressed. Brevity and wordplay do not justify dropping a decisive qualification.

An abstract should stand alone and represent purpose, approach, principal findings, and contribution accurately. Include decisive sample or contextual information and limits. Report actual findings instead of announcing that results will be discussed.

Keep abstract and body consistent. Do not add a causal claim, number, or recommendation absent from the paper. Define unfamiliar abbreviations sufficiently for independent reading.

Structured and unstructured abstracts serve different forms. Use the required form without a universal word count or sentence recipe. Remove repeated background before removing conditions that determine truth.

Keywords identify actual concepts, populations, settings, or distinctive methods using terms relevant readers would search for. Avoid generic "onderzoek" and "studie" unless those concepts are themselves the subject. Align terminology across title, abstract, keywords, and body.

#### Literature synthesis

Organize around questions, findings, methods, theoretical positions, or disagreements relevant to the argument. Use chronology where historical development matters. An author-by-author inventory is insufficient where readers need relationships.

Explain convergence, conflict, and dependence on measurement, population, design, or context. Distinguish an author's argument from an empirical result. Group references for the same proposition and separate references supporting different ones.

> Inventory: "Een studie onderzocht de deelname. Een andere studie bekeek eveneens de deelname. Een derde studie besprak hetzelfde onderwerp."
>
> Synthesis form: "De studies melden allemaal een dalende deelname, maar bestrijken verschillende perioden. Alleen de longitudinale studie volgt dezelfde personen gedurende meerdere jaren."

The synthesis requires those relationships to be established. Do not manufacture agreement, opposing camps, or a common mechanism to make the review look argumentative.

Explain why a gap matters. Limited coverage of a location does not automatically justify another study there. Retain foundational work for conceptual origins and current evidence for time-sensitive claims. Age and prestige alone do not decide relevance.

#### Sections that perform distinct functions

Let article type and reasoning determine organization. Functions may occupy separate sections or be combined where the form requires.

| Section | Writing function |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction | Establish the specific problem, context, uncertainty, question, and contribution without an unnecessarily broad historical preamble. |
| Literature or conceptual background | Establish concepts and evidence the argument depends on and explain the unresolved issue. |
| Methods | Describe design, participants or material, measures, procedures, analysis, and consequential choices sufficiently to understand how the evidence was produced. |
| Results or findings | Present relevant findings, including negative and inconclusive results; lead with what was found rather than an unexplained test name. |
| Discussion | Explain meaning, relation to prior work, alternatives, limits, and supported implications; separate them from proposals for future work. |
| Conclusion | State the established contribution and essential bounds without new evidence or a stronger version of the finding. |

When results and discussion are separate, keep reporting and interpretation distinguishable. Qualitative and interpretive work may integrate them more closely. A conventional label does not require suppressing explanation necessary to understand a finding.

| Form | Additional structural emphasis |
| --- | --- |
| Empirical paper | Connect question, design, findings, and interpretation; state what the design permits. |
| Research review | Explain coverage and selection principles; distinguish systematic coverage from a selective or interpretive account. |
| Theoretical paper | Establish a problem in an existing account, develop the reasoning, and identify what the extension explains or leaves open. |
| Case study | Supply context, selection rationale, evidence, and analytical relevance; bound transfer beyond the case. |
| Research proposal | Separate completed work and existing evidence from planned methods, expected outcomes, and possible benefits. |
| Policy brief | Present problem, evidence, options, tradeoffs, recommendation, and relevant constraints; distinguish findings from value judgments. |

These are available forms, not mandatory outlines or word allocations. Use the divisions and signposting the argument requires.

#### Methods and findings in concrete terms

Describe what was actually done, measured, excluded, and observed. Specify relevant selection, recruitment, material, timing, instruments, missing data, and analytical choices. Do not fill unknown details with plausible conventions.

Use "willekeurig toegewezen" only for actual random assignment. Separate a plan from a completed procedure and observed practice from formal approval.

Define constructs operationally where needed. A confidence self-report is not direct measurement of competence. An interview account differs from observing behavior. Name the distinction without dismissing a suitable method.

Distinguish prior hypotheses from explanations developed after seeing findings. Describe exploratory analyses as exploratory. An unexpected result can be informative without being rewritten as a prediction.

For qualitative findings, make the connection between interpretation and material intelligible. Preserve participants' words, context, and the meaning of translated terms. A quotation can illustrate a theme without establishing prevalence. Counts of coded interviews need a denominator and do not automatically describe a wider population.

#### Statistical findings with substantive meaning

Report important estimates with sample information, magnitude, units, and uncertainty. Include the statistic, degrees of freedom, exact probability value, effect measure, and interval where relevant to the method and venue.

Explain the substantive result rather than leaving symbols to carry it. Distinguish standard deviation, standard error, and an interval for an estimate. Identify adjusted estimates, comparison groups, and relevant differences between the original and analytical samples.

Describe multiplicity adjustments, sensitivity analyses, and consequential departures from the stated analysis when they affect interpretation. Do not label a finding "robuust" solely because one alternative specification produces a similar result.

Report material negative, contradictory, and inconclusive findings. Do not hide them through an outcome change, omitted subgroup, or selective emphasis on an exploratory pattern.

Keep numbers, directions, labels, and qualifications consistent across prose, tables, figures, titles, and abstracts. Use tables for comparative detail and prose for the principal finding and its meaning. Captions and notes need the sample, measures, units, abbreviations, uncertainty, and source information necessary to interpret the display.

#### Claims, evidence, and limits

Keep claims tied to the actual design. Self-reported productivity differs from direct measurement, and association differs from causation.

> Too strong: "De enquête bewijst dat thuiswerken de productiviteit verhoogt."
>
> Bounded, if supported: "In deze enquête meldden thuiswerkers een hogere productiviteit."

Place material limits beside the claim they affect. General evidence conventions govern attribution, modal strength, and inference. Academic tone does not require stacks of "mogelijk", "wellicht", and "er zou kunnen worden gesteld".

Do not append a generic limitations section while leaving earlier assertions overstated. Explain each consequential limitation's actual effect on interpretation, transfer, or precision.

#### Substantive sentences and stable terminology

Each sentence should establish a fact, define a concept, explain a relationship, provide evidence, or delimit the argument. Avoid assertions that merely announce importance.

Use a consistent name for a construct, measure, group, or method. Reduce repeated wording without substituting near-synonyms for defined terms.

> Empty repetition: "De resultaten zijn belangrijk. Deze belangrijke bevindingen hebben belangrijke implicaties."
>
> Substantive treatment: State the actual implication and the evidence supporting it.

Do not introduce a fashionable conceptual label for a relationship the passage could name precisely. Where a specialized distinction matters, define and maintain it.

#### Syntax that makes reasoning explicit

Keep the main proposition visible. Use subordination for actual dependencies and concessions without burying the assertion inside several layers of qualification.

Use active voice to identify decisions and passive voice to foreground methods or material. Avoid impersonal phrasing that conceals who interpreted evidence or made a choice.

Completed procedures and observations concern past events; definitions, the present argument, and continuing propositions may use the present. An enduring work can be discussed in the present while its publication history remains past.

Do not copy trailing English participles or overloaded German noun phrases. State inferential steps directly. Compact source-language abstracts do not require equally compressed Dutch sentences.

#### Necessary formal conventions

Use the publication's citation system consistently. Author-date and numbered systems organize references differently; neither is universal for Dutch scholarship. Keep reference entries, locators, and attached claims aligned.

Preserve required parentheses, brackets, abbreviations, bibliographic punctuation, statistical symbols, equations, variables, and units. Required notation can differ from ordinary locale formatting.

These requirements do not justify stacks of optional asides, unnecessary Latin, or inflated diction. Keep exact quoted wording distinct from the surrounding house typography.

#### Correspondence and declarations

Academic correspondence should identify the substantive point in professional Dutch. A submission letter can explain question, contribution, and relevance without praising the journal or exaggerating novelty.

A response to criticism should identify the issue, explain the change or reasoned disagreement, and locate the passage where useful. Separate completed changes from proposals. Formulaic thanks do not answer a methodological objection; respect does not require accepting unsupported criticism.

State contributions, funding, conflicts, ethics approval, consent, assistance, and material availability accurately when required. Describe actual activities rather than vague assurances. Do not imply approval, access, preregistration, independent review, or verification that did not occur.

Distinguish open material, restricted access, access on request, and unshareable material. Give actual conditions. Describe assistance by its real use and extent without transferring responsibility for the claims.

#### Worked example

> Before: "Het is belangrijk te benadrukken dat onze resultaten onomstotelijk bewijzen dat alle deelnemers baat hadden bij de interventie, wat mogelijk op een revolutionaire ontwikkeling wijst."
>
> Bounded alternative, for evidence supporting reported benefits only: "Deelnemers meldden na de interventie verbeteringen. Het onderzoek laat niet zien of de interventie die veranderingen heeft veroorzaakt."

The alternative requires evidence for the reported improvements and the inferential limitation. If benefits for all participants or a causal conclusion are supported, preserve that information rather than weakening the account automatically.

### Technical

#### Organize around the reader's purpose

A technical document should support one main purpose. Identify substantial material serving another purpose separately, connected by a descriptive link where useful.

| Form | Reader's need | Writing requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tutorial | Learn through a successful practical experience. | State what the reader will build, provide a supported sequence and visible results, and keep background proportionate. |
| How-to | Complete a specific task. | Assume relevant competence, name necessary actions and choices, and keep unrelated explanation outside the sequence. |
| Reference | Find dependable facts. | State options, behavior, constraints, and errors in a structure corresponding to the system. |
| Explanation | Understand a bounded subject and its reasons. | Explain design choices, context, alternatives, and tradeoffs apart from a task sequence. |

A tutorial can use "we" for a genuinely shared sequence. Instructions use the established reader address or a suitable command form. A reference describes the system; an explanation can express a reasoned design judgment.

Use task titles such as "Een instantie maken" and concept titles such as "Opbouw van het geheugen". Do not force an English title formula into an awkward Dutch phrase.

#### Make instructions actionable

Write direct commands: "Kies Opslaan", "Installeer het onderdeel", or "Selecteer het bestand". Dutch imperatives often work without an explicit address pronoun. Retain any stated "u" or "je" relationship coherently in surrounding explanations.

Use a neutral infinitive for appropriate task headings and labels: "Bestand opslaan". Do not alternate command forms and infinitives randomly within a procedural sequence.

Give each instruction one principal action. Put warnings and prerequisites before the action they govern. State the common case before exceptions unless an exception must be known first.

Name the visible result where it helps readers recognize progress: output, changed state, prompt, or relevant log entry. Do not invent a screen state or imply a command succeeded without evidence.

Avoid routine "alstublieft" or "alsjeblieft" in every step. Do not minimize effort with "gewoon", "simpelweg", "even", or "probleemloos". A supported duration may be stated separately.

Use the present for current behavior and distinguish future plans from existing capability. English future wording does not require "zal" in every instruction.

#### Keep terminology and syntax dependable

Use actual symbols, operations, files, flags, commands, and labels. Keep one meaning for each term. Distinguish actions such as "controleren", "valideren", "inschakelen", and "selecteren" when the system treats them differently.

Explain necessary terms near first use. Avoid idioms, unnecessary Latin abbreviations, and figurative descriptions that make interpretation harder.

Keep conditions close to the actions they govern and sentence brackets manageable. Several exclusions may need separate sentences or an actual list. Length is a density signal, not a fixed Dutch word limit.

Use a grammatical category noun where an identifier cannot fit naturally: a file, field, method, or option. Preserve the identifier itself. Do not repair Dutch grammar by changing code.

#### Preserve exact notation

Use explicit alternatives where a slash or "en/of" hides grouping. "A, B of beide" expresses an inclusive choice when that is intended; distinguish it from an exclusive choice.

Avoid improvised singular-plural forms such as "bestand(en)" in explanatory prose. State the relevant number or choose a grammatical formulation covering the intended cases.

Format code, commands, flags, and paths as code. Preserve case, punctuation, characters, and meaningful whitespace. Follow the programming language and project for indentation rather than a prose preference.

Keep interpolation variables, markup, placeholders, and link destinations exact. Reorder surrounding prose for Dutch syntax instead of translating a variable name. Variable content may affect articles, number, or adjective agreement; choose wording that remains grammatical for the supported values.

Use real names and paths in factual documentation. Mark illustrative values as examples. Counts and directory trees must correspond to the state described; include their reproducible basis where material.

#### Make documents easy to navigate

Use informative task and concept headings with a logical hierarchy. Prefer descriptive links to "klik hier". Provide essential context on the current page instead of making readers follow a link for the basic explanation.

Use Dutch list punctuation. Avoid "enzovoort" where it conceals an unspecified category; state whether a list is partial and what defines its scope.

Name interface controls accurately, including their language. Do not translate a displayed English label into a nonexistent Dutch control. The explanatory prose can remain Dutch while an exact label retains its actual spelling.

#### Keep change descriptions focused

A change description or commit message should identify the concrete problem and resulting behavior. Include the condition exposing the issue and relevant evidence.

Logs, commit lists, and measurement tables support the account rather than replace it. Distinguish observed behavior, intended behavior, and an unverified expectation.

Interface text follows the screen and action. Documentation conventions do not justify paragraphs inside buttons. A Dutch action label often uses an infinitive; a status message should distinguish an available action from a completed one.

Keep UI state and consequence exact. "Opslaan" is an action; "Opgeslagen" reports completion. Do not announce success for an action that is still in progress or only scheduled.

#### Example

> Dense: "De configuratie van de parameters van het importbudgetcontrolescript vindt plaats via het bestand `budget.json`. Hierbij is het belangrijk op te merken dat uitvoering met `--write`, waarmee de opgeslagen waarde aan het huidige aantal wordt aangepast, alleen bij een verlaging mag plaatsvinden. Bij overschrijding mislukt de controle."
>
> Clearer: "Het script leest het importbudget uit `budget.json`. Als het huidige aantal het budget overschrijdt, mislukt de controle. Gebruik `--write` alleen om het opgeslagen budget te verlagen."

The revision preserves the file, condition, and restricted action. Keep additional conditions required to use the command correctly rather than deleting them for brevity.

### Absolute

#### Directness without lost meaning

Give the useful answer in plain Dutch. Keep the reason, condition, consequence, or necessary limitation close to it. Explain required terms through their effect and remove jargon the reader does not need.

Directness does not require brusqueness, hostility, excessive certainty, or telegraphic English-shaped notes. A neutral complete sentence can be concise.

#### Complete sentences with no padding

Omit greetings, pre-narration, decorative headings, repeated conclusions, flattering acknowledgments, and invitations to continue. Keep articles, verbs, reference words, and agreement that make the sentence intelligible.

> Compressed: "Token ontbreekt. Opnieuw proberen zinloos."
>
> Clear, for the established issue: "Er ontbreekt een token in de aanvraag. Voeg er een toe voordat u de aanvraag opnieuw verstuurt."

The alternative assumes a missing token is established and can be supplied. Preserve uncertainty where that diagnosis is only possible.

A fragment can serve a real label or status. It should not become the default for all explanation.

#### Necessary explanation stays

Keep a qualification that changes the answer, a warning that affects action, and the distinction between recommendation and requirement.

> Insufficient: "Verwijder het bestand."
>
> Sufficient for the stated case: "Verwijder het duplicaat pas nadat u hebt gecontroleerd of het origineel opent."

The condition belongs to this recommendation; it is not a generic precaution to invent in every case. Direct prose should be sufficient for the real stakes.

#### Worked example

> Before: "Natuurlijk! Op hoofdlijnen lijkt er mogelijk sprake te zijn van een authenticatiegerelateerde problematiek die wellicht door middel van tokenvernieuwing zou kunnen worden verholpen."
>
> After, if expiry is the relevant possibility: "Het token kan verlopen zijn. Vernieuw het en verstuur de aanvraag opnieuw."

If expiry is established, say "Het token is verlopen". If renewal needs a condition or different procedure, retain it. Directness does not authorize turning an unspecified issue into a convenient diagnosis.

### Article

#### Concrete material and a reason to read

Lead with an artifact, observation, example, event, supported number, or specific tension. Let it establish why the subject matters. Introduce the broader point after concrete material where that order helps readers.

Avoid sweeping declarations about the modern world, irrelevant biography, and announcements that the article will explore its topic. An opening can be restrained and still give a reason to continue.

A Dutch article can be skeptical, reflective, conversational, expansive, or terse. Do not impose a generic local personality or translate a promotional English voice word for word.

#### Headlines, standfirsts, and news leads

A headline should reflect the evidence and certainty of the story. Preserve consequential limits of population, timing, and scope. A question headline must not insinuate an unsupported allegation.

Search-friendly phrasing and brevity do not justify stronger claims. Appropriate headline compression must not turn an allegation into fact, an association into a cause, or a proposal into a decision.

A standfirst should add development, context, or stakes rather than repeat the headline with synonyms. Keep scope consistent across headline, standfirst, and body.

A news lead establishes the consequential development and essential people, place, or timing. Do not force every detail into the first sentence. Prefer a decision's consequence to a routine statement that a meeting occurred.

> Meeting-centered: "De raad besprak dinsdag de tarieven."
>
> Outcome-centered, if established: "De raad besloot dinsdag het bustarief in januari van 2 naar 2,50 euro te verhogen."

The alternative adds a decision, amount, and effective date that must be known. Include the year where necessary. It is not a permissible paraphrase of a meeting whose outcome is unknown.

A feature may open with a scene or individual experience. Give readers a timely statement of the broader subject and stakes. Delayed explanation should create meaningful interest, not conceal a weak premise.

#### A coherent line of development

Give each section a distinct contribution. Develop the line through evidence, examples, implications, and relevant objections. Avoid a symmetrical outline unsupported by the material.

Keep the writer's actual position recognizable. A distinctive voice need not become a generic sequence of commentary formulas.

A sustained thought may need a longer sentence or paragraph. Expansion should add explanation or experience instead of another paraphrase of the thesis. Let transitions express actual relationships.

#### Credibility and proportion

Support factual claims with available evidence. Distinguish reported cases, illustrative examples, opinion, and interpretation. Do not invent customer experience, results, credentials, conversations, or personal vulnerability.

Attribute allegations and disputed accounts clearly. Separate what records establish, what a source says, what the subject disputes, and what remains unknown.

Use reporting verbs according to the stance actually warranted. Do not substitute "beweerde" for every repeated "zei" or "erkende" for a neutral statement. Maintain source boundaries across a long passage without adding distancing "zou" mechanically.

Represent an available response substantively. Do not imply someone refused to comment unless that happened or treat a missing response as evidence of guilt.

#### Data reporting with context and human consequences

Lead with the finding answering the reporting question. Supply historical, geographic, or other meaningful comparisons to explain its scale. Use comparable periods and definitions.

Explain whether a change concerns the underlying phenomenon or the way it was recorded. Administrative records measure what their collection system captures, not necessarily every event.

Connect patterns to affected people where supported. Individual accounts can show experience without establishing prevalence. Make their role and selection intelligible, especially when an unusual case supplies the opening.

Keep the distinction between data and testimony visible. Expert interpretation can explain a result without replacing it or eliminating uncertainty. A discrepancy between accounts and records may remain part of the story.

Implications should follow from established findings. Distinguish an observed trend from a forecast and a possible action from a demonstrated remedy. Striking figures do not justify an inevitable future.

#### Public explanations of methodology

A methodology passage should explain how the finding was produced and where its limits lie. Identify the data publisher and source, relevant release or access date, coverage period, population, geographic scope, and consequential definitions.

Explain material exclusions, missing records, transformations, adjustments, and comparison choices in plain language. Do not equate reported incidents with every incident that occurred.

Describe relevant sensitivity to definitions or exclusions and known limitations of coverage or accuracy. State what verification actually happened rather than giving vague assurances.

Explain access to data, code, and supporting material, including genuine restrictions. A concise passage may direct readers to fuller detail but must not hide a limitation that changes the central finding.

#### Captions, chart text, and accessible descriptions

A caption identifies the relevant person, place, event, or evidence and necessary context. Distinguish the image's date from the event's date. Identify archival, illustrative, or reconstructed material when readers might otherwise mistake it for a record of the event.

Chart titles, labels, annotations, and notes need the quantities, units, periods, populations, uncertainty, and source information required to read them. Distinguish counts from rates and adjusted from unadjusted values.

Do not let a title assert a cause the chart cannot establish. Keep units, number presentation, labels, and comparison directions consistent with the prose.

Alternative text should convey relevant information or function in context. For charts, give the important relationship and essential uncertainty instead of only "Grafiek van de resultaten". A fuller text description or accessible data table may be needed for detail.

Avoid redundant visual inventories and unsupported interpretation. Supply required credits accurately.

#### Corrections and substantive updates

A correction identifies the material error and states the corrected information plainly. Explain any change to the central finding. Do not disguise an error as "verduidelijkt" or "bijgewerkt".

Distinguish correction from new information about an evolving event. Give enough timing and context to understand what changed, without repeating a misleading or harmful detail beyond what the correction needs.

#### Characteristic formulas to remove

Remove empty openings such as "In een wereld die voortdurend verandert", automatic "gamechanger" claims, unsupported "baanbrekend" judgments, and bridges such as "Maar waarom is dit belangrijk?" where the consequence can be stated directly.

Avoid fake vulnerability arcs, biography padding, staged revelations, and closing questions included only to solicit engagement. Replacing a formula with smoother Dutch does not supply missing substance.

A genuine question or relevant personal experience can belong to the piece. The problem is fabricated connection or unearned importance, not first person itself.

#### Guides, essays, opinion, and newsletters

| Form | Writing requirements |
| --- | --- |
| Technical guide | Open with the practical result. Use relevant commands, code, screenshots, and concrete output where available. End with a useful consequence. |
| Essay | Develop a connected inquiry or argument from a specific observation, tension, or real contradiction. Let the progression earn the conclusion. |
| Opinion | Make the position clear and answer to evidence. Represent serious alternatives fairly rather than invent weak opponents. |
| Newsletter | Make the opening screen useful. Keep diary material when it serves the piece and labels when they help navigation. |

An article may use narrative techniques, but reported anecdotes remain factual. Literary devices do not authorize invented reporting.

#### Worked example

> Before: "In het dynamische werklandschap van vandaag is onze revolutionaire tool een echte gamechanger voor drukbezette teams. Waarom dat belangrijk is? Heel eenvoudig!"
>
> Alternative for an illustrative product with the stated behavior: "De tool slaat elk concept automatisch op. Als het versturen mislukt, blijft het werk van het team bewaard."

The alternative replaces promotion with a capability and consequence. Both must be true. Unknown behavior cannot be supplied through stylistic improvement.

### Storytelling

#### Particular experience rather than generic atmosphere

Make the scene belong to these characters, in this world, under these conditions. A viewpoint character notices according to attention, knowledge, desire, fear, experience, and immediate purpose.

An inventory of everything a camera could record often contributes less than a detail revealing what matters to the character. A detail may be visual, auditory, tactile, olfactory, or conceptual. Do not add smells, bodily sensations, or unusual defects to every scene simply to make it vivid.

> Generic: "De kamer was prachtig en vol herinneringen."
>
> Illustrative scene: "Iemand had om de lichte rechthoek heen gepoetst waar vroeger de foto van haar vader had gestaan."

The alternative invents a situation. It demonstrates fictional specificity, not faithful factual paraphrase. Choose details belonging to the authorized story instead of novelty for its own sake.

#### Voice, viewpoint, and distance

Keep narrator register and perspective intelligible. A shift in viewpoint or narrative distance should have a function and sufficient orientation.

Distinguish the narrator's knowledge from the character's. Do not give a character information they have not acquired. In close viewpoint, filter description and comparison through what the character could notice and understand.

Vocabulary and syntax may reflect occupation, education, age, relationships, and emotional state. Do not reduce those influences to caricature or use grammatical deviation as shorthand for intelligence.

Free indirect discourse can place a character's judgment inside third-person narration without an explicit thought tag.

> "Ze schoof de brief terug. Moest ze hem soms alweer geloven?"

Keep the perspective clear enough that a character's uncertainty or prejudice is not mistaken for an established narrator's fact. Do not preserve English interior syntax or distribute Dutch particles indiscriminately.

#### Make narrative elements work together

Meaning, character, plot, disclosure, world, relationships, and prose influence one another.

| Concern | Writing implication |
| --- | --- |
| Meaning | Let choices, consequences, relationships, and contrasts carry the thematic question. |
| Character change | Make desires, beliefs, pressures, decisions, and effects intelligible where the arc depends on them. |
| Plot | Establish consequential relationships among events rather than a list of incidents. |
| Mystery | Shape what readers know, suspect, misunderstand, and later reinterpret. |
| World | Let physical and social conditions enable and constrain action. |
| Relationships | Give bonds, status, obligations, and conflict specific stakes. |
| Prose | Make movement, detail, voice, and rhetorical choices serve the moment. |

A setting detail can reveal a rule and pressure a relationship. Dialogue can change an alliance and disclose a motive. Let those effects arise from the situation instead of loading every sentence equally.

A flaw, crisis, and transformation can structure an arc. Stasis, refusal, tragic failure, ensemble change, and developing perception are also available forms. Do not impose the same corrective lesson on every character.

#### Causality, cost, and consequence

Make choices matter where the story depends on agency. Establish the circumstances making an action possible, its immediate effect, and consequences altering later choices.

Coherence does not require a single-track plot or a protagonist controlling every outcome. Chance, institutions, others, and external events can matter. Avoid arbitrary convenience and artificial tidiness.

Keep world rules consistent. Powers, technologies, and institutions should not gain unprepared capabilities solely to solve a current problem. Reader understanding of a capability should support its credibility when used to resolve conflict.

Limits can produce more meaningful choices than extra powers. Develop established possibilities before adding new ones. A disadvantage should affect action instead of disappearing when victory is needed.

Maintain physical details, geography, travel time, seasons, relationships, and knowledge. Changed behavior needs an intelligible basis where the narrative depends on it. Continuity contributes to meaning.

#### Information, suspense, and revelation

Give readers enough orientation to understand the present scene while withholding information that creates a purposeful question. Productive uncertainty differs from confusion about actor, place, or physical action.

Mystery may concern events, motives, relationships, world rules, or future outcomes. Plant consequential details fairly. A revelation should change understanding where the story promises that effect.

Flashbacks, flash-forwards, delayed disclosure, and multiple timelines are available tools. Use them when they improve experience or interpretation. Temporal complexity is not obligatory, and linearity is not a defect.

An ending may resolve, partly resolve, or leave questions open. A continuing chapter may create forward interest without an automatic cliffhanger. A completed work may close its questions without resolving every relationship neatly.

Let choices and consequences carry thematic meaning. The outcome can retain ambiguity, acceptance, action, or unresolved cost. Do not append a universal lesson to explain an already intelligible scene.

#### Emotion, behavior, and subtext

Use emotion labels, action, silence, dialogue, sensation, or thought according to the moment. "Ze was bang" can suffice. Repeated tightening chests, held breaths, cold blood, and shaking hands can be formulaic too.

| General statement | Particular fictional treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "Ze werd boos." | "Pas toen ze haar hand opende, zag ze de afdruk van haar duimnagel in haar huid." |
| "Hij was doodsbang." | "Zijn hand trilde zo dat hij de sleutel niet in het slot kreeg." |
| "Het afscheid maakte haar verdrietig." | "Ze legde de kussens nog één keer recht en trok de deur dicht zonder om te kijken." |

Each alternative adds behavior and implications that must belong to the scene. They are not interchangeable translations of emotion. Self-awareness, bodily pressure, reluctance, and practical difficulty are added fictional choices.

Avoid explaining subtext just after readers can infer it. Characters may evade, conceal, misunderstand, or speak plainly. Not every emotional exchange needs an unspoken second meaning.

#### Dialogue with identifiable speakers

Distinguish speakers through vocabulary, syntax, rhythm, attention, and intention. A line belongs to a person in a relationship, not a generic witty narrator.

Dialogue can advance action, reveal character, establish a world, create humor, or express disagreement. Avoid making every exchange a philosophical debate, miniature speech, or declaration of theme.

Ordinary "zei" and "vroeg" can identify speech unobtrusively. Replacing every tag with "siste", "fluisterde", "sneerde", or an adverb may change the action and create noise. A relevant action beat can reveal intention or hesitation.

Keep speaker identity intelligible. Distinct voices help, but every line need not be identifiable without context or attribution. Do not add an action after every utterance solely to avoid a repeated tag.

Address, emphatic pronouns, particles, and diminutives carry relationship. A shift from "u" to "je" can matter. An "even", "hoor", or "toch" should fit the speaker rather than mark generic native speech.

Use reductions and dialect where they belong. Edited dialogue need not reproduce every recorded hesitation. Technical exactness, formality, and restraint can also be credible voices.

#### Pacing through syntax and proportion

Short clauses can create urgency. A longer sentence can sustain movement, pressure, or reflection. Neither length alone determines pace.

Give emotional consequences enough room. Keep transitions proportionate to importance. Remove repeated action, redundant exchanges, and description that delays a scene without changing its effect.

Prepare emotional peaks through relationships, desire, risk, and prior experience. Intense wording cannot replace preparation. Keep an effective action or image without immediately interpreting it.

Paragraph and section breaks shape attention, pauses, speakers, time, and viewpoint. Let variation follow the scene instead of a long-short numerical pattern.

#### Narrative tense and represented speech

Choose a coherent narrative frame. Simple past can sustain past narration; present tense can establish an immediate frame; perfect constructions may suit conversational recollection. None guarantees authenticity or literary quality.

Use tense shifts for changes of time, perspective, frame, or voice. Dialogue may use a conversational tense different from narration. An earlier event may require an appropriate past-perfect orientation before the earlier frame is established.

Keep direct speech, attributed reports, hypothetical thought, and free indirect discourse distinct. Preserve who knows, believes, doubts, or imagines what.

Do not import German subjunctive reporting or copy English backshift mechanically. Establish the Dutch frame and make temporal relationships intelligible within it.

#### Anti-cliché openings

| Stock opening | More particular treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "In een wereld waarin ..." | Begin with a character's particular action, constraint, or predicament. |
| "Het was een donkere, stormachtige nacht." | Establish what the weather changes or what the character notices. |
| "Ze wist toen nog niet ..." | Create dramatic irony through a consequential knowledge difference. |
| "Er was eens ..." | Keep the convention where the tale needs it; otherwise begin with the relevant event or detail. |

A familiar form can be intentional. Do not replace a fairy-tale opening with artificial novelty where its form establishes the desired voice.

#### Anti-cliché emotion

| Stock wording | More particular treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "Een rilling liep over haar rug." | Name a relevant response or consequential action. |
| "Haar hart sloeg een slag over." | Show what she does or notices in response. |
| "De tranen stroomden over zijn wangen." | Use tears when relevant; otherwise find the particular expression or resistance. |
| "Een golf van emoties overspoelde haar." | Name the feeling or a specific action. |
| "Het bloed stolde in zijn aderen." | Establish the fear and its effect. |
| "Ze had vlinders in haar buik." | Use a relevant nervous thought, behavior, or perception. |
| "De tijd stond stil." | Describe narrowed attention or altered duration. |
| "Zijn wereld stortte in." | Establish the realization and its first consequence. |

These are choices, not a requirement to physicalize every emotion. Bodily detail can become repetitive. A familiar idiom may fit a credible speaker.

#### Anti-cliché description

| Stock wording | More particular treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "doordringende blauwe ogen" | Choose a detail relevant to the viewpoint. |
| "ravenzwart haar" | Describe consequential appearance, movement, or action without automatic beauty formulas. |
| "gebeeldhouwde trekken" | Give a distinguishing feature where appearance matters. |
| "een prachtige vrouw" | Name a particular perception or effect, or keep the direct judgment if sufficient. |
| "De zon brandde meedogenloos." | Describe what the heat changes in this environment. |
| "een onheilspellende stilte" | Identify a missing or remaining sound and the reason for unease. |
| "De kamer werd in duisternis gehuld." | Clarify what changed and what remains perceptible. |

Do not invent scars, asymmetry, smells, or physical defects solely to distinguish someone. Particularity should follow the situation.

#### Anti-cliché action

| Stock wording | More particular treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "De hel brak los." | Show the first failure or action and its consequences. |
| "Ze vochten voor hun leven." | Describe a consequential act, mistake, technique, or desperate choice. |
| "bliksemsnel" | Give the motion and effect without automatic intensification. |
| "tegen alle verwachtingen in" | Establish the real disadvantage or expectation. |
| "op het nippertje" | Show timing and the consequence of almost being too late. |

Concise summary remains available when the action is not the scene's focus. Meaningful specificity does not require exhaustive choreography.

#### Anti-cliché dialogue

| Stock wording | More particular treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "We moeten praten." | Raise the particular matter unless avoidance is the point. |
| "Je begrijpt het niet." | Make the actual misunderstanding visible. |
| "Het is niet wat het lijkt." | Give the explanation, evasion, or lie particular to the speaker. |
| "Ik heb hier een slecht gevoel over." | Identify the observation or conflict causing concern. |
| "Dat ging lekker." | Give a response particular to the failure where generic sarcasm adds nothing. |

A familiar line can belong to an interaction or required quotation. Familiarity does not justify making every character conspicuously inventive.

#### Recurrent generic imagery

| Formula | More direct or particular treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "een tapijt van ..." | Name the pattern, elements, or relationship. |
| "het gewicht van herinneringen" | Establish the particular burden, recollection, or action. |
| "door de complexiteit van het leven navigeren" | Show choices and constraints. |
| "in het weefsel van het lot" | State the event or remove the generic commentary. |
| "een voorbode van ..." | Identify the sign and what it reasonably predicts. |
| "een kakofonie van ..." | Identify relevant sounds. |
| "een symfonie van gevoelens" | Name feelings or interactions. |
| "de dans van de schaduwen" | Describe movement and its significance to the observer. |
| "gefluisterde beloften" | State what was said, implied, or hoped for. |
| "een echo uit het verleden" | Name the memory, reference, or actual repeated sound. |
| "het weefsel van de werkelijkheid" | Describe the fictional world's particular change. |
| "En zo kwam het dat ..." | Begin with action or observation unless the narrative form needs the formula. |

Literal meanings remain available. An actual echo, dance, whisper, woven object, or musical performance may belong. Reusable elevation detached from the situation is the problem.

#### Genre conventions without automatic plots

| Genre | Familiar shortcuts requiring particular causes and consequences |
| --- | --- |
| Fantasy | An orphaned farm child automatically chosen to save the world; conveniently exact prophecy; evil without motive; uniformly wise elves and gruff dwarves; magic without cost or limit. |
| Science fiction | Immediate hostility as the default consequence of AI sentience; an unexamined last-person premise; faster-than-light travel without social consequences; cosmetically different aliens; technology failing only when convenient. |
| Mystery | Surprise based only on the least suspected person; alcoholism as ready-made detective depth; a victim's secret life replacing development; an accidental clue solving everything; a villain explaining the entire plan. |
| Romance | Hostility becoming love without development; body description replacing an encounter; decisive misunderstanding sustained by implausible silence; an obvious love-triangle choice; a rain kiss as an automatic climax. |

A convention can work with particular meaning and consequences. Novelty does not repair weak causality, and familiarity does not invalidate a premise. Do not add ambiguity, nonlinear structure, or tragedy solely to oppose a convention.

#### Choose comparisons from the viewpoint

Draw images from the character's world and experience. A merchant may understand a threat through debt; a courtier through reputation and rumor. The image should reveal viewpoint as well as describe action.

Avoid comparisons attachable to any scene. Surprise earns its place through revelation rather than unfamiliar words alone. A source-language image dependent on an idiom or sound pattern may need another Dutch expression to perform the same function.

Cross-sensory images can express unusual perception; a strained comparison can represent an extreme state of mind. Keep them intelligible rather than mixing sensory domains simply to sound poetic.

#### Literary techniques

Choose a technique by the effect needed. Classical terminology does not justify needless repetition, decorative complexity, or invented logic. Sound and wordplay must work in Dutch rather than mechanically preserve a source-language pattern.

| Purpose | Available techniques | Useful application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Connect sound | Alliteration repeats initial sounds; assonance repeats vowels; consonance repeats consonants. | Establish a restrained relation among consequential words. |
| Shape atmosphere through sound | Sibilance emphasizes sibilants; cacophony uses harsh combinations; euphony uses smoother ones. | Support a perception without a conspicuous sound exercise. |
| Place a sound in the scene | Onomatopoeia uses sound-imitating words such as "zoemen", "kraken", and "rinkelen". | Make consequential sound perceptible without replacing events with effects. |
| Create a rhyme-like cadence | Internal rhyme and similar endings connect nearby phrases. | Support ritual, comedy, or musical speech without making ordinary narration jingle. |
| Establish a refrain | Anaphora repeats openings; epistrophe repeats endings; symploce combines them. | Develop a concern or insistence rather than mechanical rhythm. |
| Carry an idea forward | Anadiplosis repeats an ending at the next opening; epanalepsis returns to an opening phrase. | Make continuity or changed implication perceptible. |
| Express insistence | Epizeuxis repeats immediately; diacope separates repeated words. | Convey particular pressure or a demand. |
| Characterize through redundancy | Deliberate restatement expresses evasion, resignation, obsession, or ceremony. | Reveal the speaker rather than merely duplicate information. |
| Develop a word's meaning | Polyptoton varies grammatical form; ploce and antanaclasis repeat with changed senses. | Expose ambiguity or evolving understanding. |
| Articulate contrast | Antithesis balances opposites; paradox and oxymoron express apparent contradiction. | Represent real tension instead of manufacturing opposition. |
| Reverse a relationship | Chiasmus reverses structures or ideas; antimetabole reverses repeated words. | Make the reversal matter to the thought or exchange. |
| Create balance | Isocolon uses parallel units; a tricolon uses three. | Give a justified distinction rhythm without forcing the count. |
| Control emphasis | Periodic sentences delay the main assertion; loose sentences supply it early. | Match unfolding to expectation, reflection, or action within Dutch syntax. |
| Control clause movement | Parataxis places independent clauses together; hypotaxis subordinates them. | Express sequence, dependency, or layered thought. |
| Control accumulation | Asyndeton omits conjunctions; polysyndeton repeats them. | Create urgency, persistence, or exhaustion where appropriate. |
| Build rhetorical intensity | Arrange phrases in increasing importance or force. | Let a realization develop instead of beginning every clause at maximum intensity. |
| Represent interruption | Aposiopesis breaks off an utterance; anacoluthon changes its grammatical course. | Express an actual interruption or disrupted thought. |
| Compress recoverable information | Ellipsis omits understood words; apposition identifies through an adjacent phrase. | Retain what the intended reader can recover without making explanation telegraphic. |
| Sustain an image | Simile compares explicitly; metaphor implicitly; an extended metaphor develops the image. | Express perception particular to character and scene. |
| Explain through comparison | Analogy relates domains; allegory extends representation across a narrative. | Develop a relationship while preserving limits. |
| Transfer a perception | Personification gives human qualities to the nonhuman; a transferred epithet shifts a modifier to an associated object. | Reveal viewpoint without asserting literal intention. |
| Name by association | Synecdoche uses part and whole; metonymy uses association; merism evokes a whole through parts or extremes. | Use intelligible cultural shorthand. |
| Layer knowledge | Dramatic irony gives readers knowledge a character lacks; foreshadowing prepares a later event. | Create fair suspense or reinterpretation. |
| Reorder time | Flashback reveals earlier events; flash-forward changes anticipation. | Let chronology serve disclosure without losing orientation. |
| Enter an event directly | In medias res begins amid consequential action. | Establish involvement and then supply necessary context. |
| Create irony | Verbal irony separates intended and literal meaning; situational irony reverses an established expectation. | Reveal a meaningful discrepancy or relationship, including through an opposite-meaning nickname. |
| Vary emotional scale | Hyperbole exaggerates; litotes and meiosis understate; bathos shifts from important to trivial. | Express attitude, restraint, or comic reversal. |
| Represent address and desire | Apostrophe addresses an absent person or abstraction; rhetorical questions express stance. | Give appeals, wishes, and warnings a credible speaker and stakes. |
| Reveal avoidance | Euphemism softens; dysphemism makes harsher; indirect naming signals taboo or evasion. | Characterize a relationship to the subject without hiding facts in exposition. |
| Draw attention through omission | A speaker mentions something while professing not to discuss it. | Expose manipulation or avoidance without habitual indirect narration. |
| Name through social perception | Epithets, titles, and culturally meaningful names stand for a person or quality. | Show social judgment while keeping identity clear. |
| Develop an allusion | References to a work, myth, event, or chain of associations add another layer. | Supply enough context for the relevant connection; obscurity is not depth. |
| Use wordplay | Puns exploit sound or meaning; zeugma and syllepsis give one governing word different applications or senses. | Create relevant Dutch wit rather than preserve an unworkable source pun. |
| Build fictional vocabulary | Neologisms coin terms; portmanteaus blend words; kennings create compressed metaphorical names. | Name distinctions the fictional culture needs. |
| Shape character diction | Category shifts, paired nouns expressing one idea, or a word inserted inside another distinguish an utterance. | Let unusual formation belong to a speaker and remain intelligible. |
| Represent a verbal mistake | Malapropisms substitute similar-sounding words; spoonerisms transpose sounds. | Characterize a moment without stereotyping a group. |
| Focus description | Description develops an artwork, place, era, season, appearance, or behavior. | Give consequential attention; an inventory may express fixation where appropriate. |
| Accumulate detail | Enumeratio lists parts; amplification develops them; distributio assigns roles or consequences. | Establish scope without making every passage an inventory. |
| Reveal thought in motion | Aporia expresses genuine doubt; self-correction revises an initial formulation. | Make uncertainty or changed judgment intelligible. |
| Reveal a reasoning strategy | Anticipated objections, eliminated alternatives, and derived contradictions expose reasoning. | Supply a basis; an unsupported slippery slope is not a proof. |
| Constrain a choice | Alternatives carry different costs or dangers. | Establish actual constraints without a false binary or hidden plausible option. |
| Expose an unstated assumption | A conclusion depends on a premise assumed to be shared. | Reveal worldview or unreliable judgment; factual argument still needs defensible premises. |
| Represent imagined speech | A character rehearses a conversation or imagines a response. | Reveal anticipation, fear, or judgment while distinguishing imagination from an actual exchange. |
| Express a worldview concisely | A maxim or pointed observation states a particular understanding. | Fit the speaker and moment without an automatic lesson. |
| Return to an earlier idea | A closing image, phrase, or observation recalls prior material. | Earn closure through changed meaning rather than an appended moral. |

Sound, repetition, and syntax may support the same moment. Do not stack devices until construction overwhelms experience. Quiet, connective, ordinary passages can sustain the larger work.

#### Necessary literary exceptions

Metaphor, repetition, fragments, unusual order, nonstandard grammar, direct address, questions, and exclamations may serve a specific character or narrative effect. Their justification is a concrete function such as pressure, disruption, cultural language, or viewpoint.

A proverb, curse, or strategic compliment should belong to a speaker and situation. Do not replace ordinary conversation everywhere with conspicuous originality.

Keep dialect coherent and respectful. Deliberate speech deviations do not require mistakes in narration. Exceptions do not authorize generic bodily metaphors, philosophical speeches, nonlinear scenes, or flourishes throughout.

Represent interruption through an appropriate construction, action, or break without adding decorative sentence dashes. Exact quoted documents and required embedded notation retain their punctuation.

#### Worked example

> Before: "Een golf van verdriet overspoelde haar toen ze besefte dat echte moed soms betekent dat je moet loslaten. Haar hart deed pijn onder het gewicht van haar herinneringen."
>
> Illustrative scene: "Ze legde zijn reservesleutel in de envelop. Een tijdlang hield ze haar hand erop."

The alternative invents a particular action and leaves its emotional implication available. It is not a paraphrase of every grief. A direct label may suit another moment better. Preserve the treatment that serves this character and scene without explaining an already intelligible implication.
